# Chapter 136 — Multijurisdictional Deployment

> **Legal status:** Corrected English master for consolidation. This file controls over conflicting earlier Chapter 136 draft language.

## Requirement

Organizations deploying AI across multiple jurisdictions must determine which legal regimes, actor roles, sector rules, contractual duties, and local restrictions apply to each deployment, then implement a controlled method for resolving conflicts, applying local overlays, and preventing unauthorized expansion of use.

## Plain-English explanation

A system approved in one country is not automatically lawful in another. The organization must assess territorial reach, local role, intended purpose, affected population, data flows, employment and consumer rules, sector requirements, language and accessibility needs, and whether the EU AI Act applies because the system or its output is used in the Union.

## Deployment requirements

The multijurisdictional process should address:

1. legal entities, establishments, markets, users, and affected persons;
2. territorial and extraterritorial applicability;
3. provider, deployer, importer, distributor, representative, and product-manufacturer roles;
4. AI-risk classification and prohibited-use differences;
5. privacy, automated-decision, employment, equality, consumer, safety, cybersecurity, and sector law;
6. localization, transfer, retention, access, and government-request requirements;
7. language, accessibility, notice, consent, consultation, and worker-representation duties;
8. supplier and subcontractor locations and dependencies;
9. local-control overlays, release restrictions, and geo-fencing where needed;
10. change management for new countries, purposes, populations, or legal developments.

## GlobalWay example

GlobalWay plans to deploy an employee-allocation system in the EU, United Kingdom, United States, Colombia, and Mexico. It creates a common global control baseline and jurisdiction overlays for worker consultation, privacy notices, automated-decision safeguards, data transfers, retention, accessibility, and local approval requirements.

## Control activity

No AI system may be expanded into a new jurisdiction or materially new population until Legal and Compliance complete an applicability assessment, approve required local overlays, confirm operational ownership, and document unresolved conflicts or restrictions.

## Evidence

- jurisdiction and entity map;
- applicability and role assessments;
- legal and sector obligation register;
- local control overlays;
- data-flow and transfer analysis;
- notices, consultations, and approvals;
- supplier and location records;
- geo-restriction and release evidence;
- legal-change and reassessment history.

## Audit test

Select multinational deployments and recent country expansions. Verify that the organization identified all relevant entities, jurisdictions, roles, data flows, affected populations, and local duties; implemented approved overlays; prevented unauthorized use; and reassessed material changes.

## Primary legal references

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended: Article 2 territorial scope, actor obligations, prohibited practices, high-risk requirements, transparency duties, and enforcement provisions as applicable.
- Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and other applicable national, regional, sector, employment, equality, consumer-protection, cybersecurity, and product-safety law.
- Current consolidated official texts and applicable local law control over generalized summaries.
