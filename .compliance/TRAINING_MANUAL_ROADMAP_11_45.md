# Controlled Training Manual Roadmap — Manuals 11–45

**Status:** PLANNING / PRE-STAGE ONLY  
**Roadmap date:** 2026-08-27  
**Release policy:** This roadmap does not authorize release, bypass upstream dependencies, or replace any mandatory review gate.

## Objective

Extend the controlled GRC Practical Manuals series beyond Manual 10 into a deep, practitioner-oriented training library covering major privacy, security, financial, operational-resilience, sector, cloud, software, AI, and assurance regimes.

The roadmap is depth-driven rather than quota-driven. A topic becomes a standalone manual only when it has enough distinct legal, regulatory, standards, technical, operational, evidence, audit, or training substance to justify independent treatment. Closely overlapping topics may be combined after architecture review.

Every promoted manual inherits the repository's fail-closed release model, including authoritative-source verification, controlled English master, technical/editorial/legal review as applicable, localization and human semantic review, accessibility and rendered-document QA, repository/workflow security review, provenance, exact-head checksums/manifest, changed-scope reconciliation, and Final Human Release Approval.

## Training-grade content standard

Each standalone manual should be designed to teach a practitioner how to understand, implement, operate, evidence, assess, improve, and explain the subject rather than merely summarize requirements. Where applicable, the controlled architecture should include:

- regulatory / standards context and applicability;
- defined terminology and scope boundaries;
- Essential / Structured / Enhanced implementation paths;
- governance, roles and accountability;
- implementation workflows and decision routing;
- control objectives and practical control activities;
- evidence expectations and example evidence schemas;
- audit / assessment procedures and sampling considerations;
- common failure modes and remediation patterns;
- third-party and supply-chain considerations;
- incident, exception and change-management handling;
- metrics, monitoring and maturity progression;
- crosswalks to adjacent frameworks without implying legal equivalence;
- scenario-based training exercises and practitioner questions;
- accessible instructional graphics with text equivalents;
- controlled English source plus approved localization workflow;
- authoritative-source provenance and source-state watch.

## Source and legal boundaries

- Laws and regulations must be separated from standards, frameworks, certifications and professional-practice references.
- Proposed rules, drafts, consultations and standards under publication must never be represented as current final requirements.
- Copyrighted standards must be cited and interpreted without reproducing protected text beyond permitted quotation limits.
- Crosswalks are implementation aids, not declarations of equivalence, certification, compliance or legal sufficiency.
- Jurisdiction-specific manuals require current-law verification at the exact release candidate head.
