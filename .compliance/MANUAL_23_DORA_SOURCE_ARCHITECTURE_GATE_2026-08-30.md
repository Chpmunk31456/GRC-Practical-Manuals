# Manual 23 — DORA Source and Controlled Architecture Gate

**Series order:** 23  
**State:** controlled downstream preparation; not publication authorization  
**Predecessor:** Manual 22 — Cloud Security controlled lane

## Controlled source decision

Manual 23 will use Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (Digital Operational Resilience Act) as the primary statutory baseline, together with applicable delegated/implementing acts, current ESAs/EU supervisory materials, and organization-specific implementation procedures kept as distinct source layers. Release-time source verification must confirm current applicability, dates, RTS/ITS state, and any supervisory updates.

## 32-chapter controlled architecture

Prepare controlled chapters covering: governance/accountability; scope and applicability; ICT risk-management framework; asset and dependency mapping; protection/prevention; detection; response/recovery; backup/restoration; learning and improvement; communication; ICT incident classification; major-incident reporting; cyber-threat notification; testing strategy; vulnerability assessments; scenario testing; threat-led penetration testing; tester independence; remediation; third-party ICT risk; pre-contract due diligence; contractual controls; subcontracting/chain risk; concentration risk; register of information; critical ICT third-party providers; exit/transition planning; operational resilience metrics; evidence architecture; supervisory-readiness; localization; source-watch/change control; provenance/accessibility/release controls.

Every substantive chapter must identify source layer, applicability, accountable owner, operating procedure, evidence object/location, review/test method, exception/remediation path, and reassessment trigger.

## Mandatory boundaries

- Do not treat DORA as replacing GDPR, NIS2, sectoral prudential rules, or national law.
- Distinguish statutory DORA requirements from RTS/ITS, supervisory guidance, contractual practice, and internal implementation choices.
- Do not generalize financial-entity scope beyond supported applicability.
- Keep major-incident reporting, voluntary cyber-threat notification, and other notification regimes distinct.
- Preserve third-party, subcontractor, concentration-risk, register-of-information, and critical-provider distinctions.
- Reverify all dates, thresholds, templates, and supervisory technical standards before candidate freeze.
