## Comparison findings

### Alternate corrected master

`72_AI_Contracting_and_Compliance_Clauses_CORRECTED_MASTER.md` substantially duplicates the controlling corrected master. It adds several useful clause categories, especially:

- explicit substantial-modification and provider-role reassessment;
- regulatory inquiry and inspection cooperation;
- liability, insurance, and remediation language;
- contract reassessment after regulatory change, incident, or renewal.

These concepts should be preserved in the final integrated Chapter 72 or Appendix P before archival. The alternate corrected master must not remain a competing publication source.

### Detailed original draft

`72_AI_Contract_Clauses_and_Allocation_of_Responsibilities.md` contains extensive implementation material not fully represented in the compact corrected master, including:

- a pre-contract role-allocation schedule;
- detailed clause families for scope, documentation, audit, incidents, change, data, oversight, transparency, security, dependencies, regulatory cooperation, and exit;
- a contract approval workflow;
- control IDs `EUAI-CON-01` through `EUAI-CON-08`;
- evidence-register requirements;
- detailed audit procedures and metrics;
- a GlobalWay contracting scenario.

This content remains useful and must be migrated selectively into:

1. the final integrated Chapter 72;
2. Appendix P — Contract Clause Checklist;
3. Appendix F — Role Assessment Worksheet, where responsibility allocation is relevant;
4. Appendix U — Control Register;
5. Appendix V — Evidence Register.

## Legal-modality findings

The detailed draft generally uses recommended wording such as “should,” but several headings and statements could be misread as direct statutory mandates, including “Mandatory clause families.” In the integrated source, these must be identified as organization-required contracting controls unless a specific clause is directly required by applicable law.

Contractual deadlines, including advance-notice periods, must not be presented as AI Act deadlines unless the legislation expressly provides them. Any negotiated notice period is an organizational or contractual control.
