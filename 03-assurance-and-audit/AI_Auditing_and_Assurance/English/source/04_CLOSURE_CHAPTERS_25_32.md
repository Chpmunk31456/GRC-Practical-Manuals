# Manual 05 — AI Auditing and Assurance
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 25–32

> Original audit implementation guidance. This material does not replace engagement-specific professional judgment or create an audit opinion by itself.

## Chapter 25 — Quality review

Before reporting, a reviewer should confirm that significant conclusions are supported by sufficient evidence, scope changes are documented, findings are internally consistent, and unresolved review comments are visible.

High-impact engagements should use reviewer independence proportionate to risk.

## Chapter 26 — Report structure

Reports should present objective, scope, criteria, methods, overall conclusion, findings, management responses, limitations, and follow-up requirements. Executive summaries should not omit material qualifications that affect interpretation.

## Chapter 27 — Assurance language

Assurance language must match the engagement performed. Readiness assessments, advisory reviews, internal audits, external audits, certification activities, and technical tests are not interchangeable.

The report should avoid implying certification, legal compliance, or formal audit opinion unless the engagement legitimately provides that result.

## Chapter 28 — Corrective-action governance

Open findings should be tracked to accountable owners and dates. Extensions should be approved and justified. High-severity overdue actions should escalate to the appropriate risk authority rather than remaining indefinitely open.

## Chapter 29 — Audit evidence retention

Evidence and workpapers should be retained according to applicable policy, legal, contractual, and professional requirements. Access should be restricted because AI audit evidence can contain sensitive architecture, data, security findings, model information, or personal information.

## Chapter 30 — Continuous assurance and monitoring

Where risk warrants, periodic audit can be supplemented by continuous or recurring control indicators. Automated monitoring should have defined ownership, thresholds, exception handling, and validation.

Continuous monitoring does not remove the need for human interpretation and periodic reassessment of whether the indicators remain meaningful.

## Chapter 31 — Audit program maturity

A mature AI audit program maintains a system inventory, risk-informed audit universe, reusable workpapers, competency plans, specialist access, consistent severity methods, findings analytics, and lessons learned.

Maturity should improve efficiency without converting audits into checklist exercises detached from system context.

## Chapter 32 — Manual release boundary

Before this manual is published, the controlled English master, authoritative-source verification, technical/editorial review, `es-419` and `pt-BR` semantic review, graphics/accessibility review, DOCX/PDF and page QA, provenance, repository/security audit, and Final Human Release Approval must be complete.

Material content changes after human approval reopen the affected review gates.
