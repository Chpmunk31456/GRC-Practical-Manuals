# Applicability, Decomposition, and Interpretation

## 1. Treat applicability as a documented decision

Applicability depends on facts. Relevant facts may include legal entity, location, regulated role, industry, license, revenue, workforce size, customer type, processing activity, data category, technology, product function, contract, transaction, or risk classification. Record the facts used, assumptions, uncertainties, reviewer, decision, and reassessment trigger.

## 2. Use controlled applicability states

Recommended states are:

- under review;
- applicable;
- partially applicable;
- conditionally applicable;
- not applicable with rationale;
- applicability deferred pending facts or guidance;
- superseded or no longer applicable.

Do not use blank fields to represent a decision.

## 3. Decompose complex provisions

Separate a provision into actionable units when it contains different actors, actions, objects, conditions, timing, evidence, exceptions, or consequences. Each analytical unit should retain its parent source reference.

A useful decomposition model records:

- regulated actor;
- required or prohibited action;
- protected object, person, process, system, or data;
- triggering condition;
- jurisdiction and scope;
- timing or frequency;
- threshold or materiality condition;
- exception, exemption, or alternative;
- documentation, notification, reporting, or retention duty;
- expected evidence;
- consequence or escalation condition.

## 4. Separate source language from interpretation

Maintain distinct fields for authorized source text or location, organization interpretation, assumptions, uncertainty, and implementation requirement. This prevents an internal summary from being mistaken for the official requirement.

## 5. Preserve qualifiers

Terms such as reasonable, appropriate, material, promptly, without undue delay, periodically, independent, documented, and risk-based may carry legal or regulatory meaning. Do not remove them merely to simplify the register. Record how the organization operationalizes the qualifier and who approved that approach.

## 6. Record exclusions and exemptions carefully

An exemption may depend on continuing facts. Record the factual basis, reviewer, approval, evidence, expiration or reassessment date, and change trigger. Absence of enforcement history is not an exemption.

## 7. Manage conflicts and overlap

When obligations overlap, preserve each source independently and link them to common controls where justified. When requirements conflict or impose different thresholds, timelines, retention periods, notice duties, or geographic restrictions, escalate rather than selecting the least demanding rule.

## 8. Handle translations

Identify whether the reviewed text is official, certified, authorized, or machine-assisted. Where legal meaning may depend on the authoritative language, retain the original reference and require qualified language review.

## 9. Approval criteria

An applicability or interpretation record should not be approved unless the source and status are verified, relevant facts are documented, exclusions are supported, uncertainties are visible, accountable owners are assigned, and a reassessment date or event trigger is defined.

## 10. Reassessment triggers

Reassess when the source changes, guidance or case law develops, business facts change, a new jurisdiction or product is introduced, a contract changes, thresholds are crossed, an incident exposes an assumption, or a reviewer identifies an error.
