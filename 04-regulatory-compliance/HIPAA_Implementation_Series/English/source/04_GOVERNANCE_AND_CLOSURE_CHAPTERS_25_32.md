# Manual 06 — HIPAA Implementation and Audit
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 25–32

> Controlled educational implementation guidance. Current-law obligations remain separated from proposed Security Rule changes. Final applicability and legal determinations require qualified human review.

## Chapter 25 — Change management and reassessment

Material changes to systems, applications, cloud services, vendors, data flows, access models, facilities, workforce processes, business relationships, or regulatory sources should trigger risk and control reassessment.

Change records should identify what changed, why, affected PHI/ePHI, impacted safeguards, testing performed, approvals, residual risk, and whether prior evidence remains valid. Material changes after review reopen affected approval gates.

## Chapter 26 — Regulatory source watch

The organization should monitor authoritative HHS/OCR rulemaking and guidance relevant to its HIPAA program. Source-state records should identify current law separately from proposals, effective dates, superseded material, and the impact of official changes on controls, documentation, training, contracts, and technical implementation.

The HIPAA Security Rule NPRM remains readiness-only until a final rule changes the controlled baseline. Proposed requirements must be visibly labeled and must not be presented as current obligations.

## Chapter 27 — Privacy and security governance reporting

Management reporting should summarize material risk, significant findings, overdue remediation, incidents, exceptions, business-associate issues, evaluation results, and regulatory changes in a form that supports accountable decisions.

Metrics should be traceable to evidence and should not hide unresolved high-risk conditions behind aggregate percentages. Governance bodies should record decisions, assigned actions, owners, and escalation where necessary.

## Chapter 28 — Internal audit and independent review

Independent or second-line review should use defined scope, criteria, evidence, sampling, testing, and reporting methods appropriate to the engagement. Reviewers should disclose conflicts, limitations, and areas not tested.

A repository QA pass, certification held by a vendor, or existence of a policy must not be represented as an independent HIPAA compliance opinion. Formal assurance requires competent human judgment and sufficient appropriate evidence.

## Chapter 29 — Evidence package for readiness or audit

A controlled readiness package should assemble the current scope statement, entity-role assumptions, PHI/ePHI inventory and data flows, risk analysis, risk-treatment records, safeguard evidence, workforce records, business-associate records, incident/breach-assessment records, evaluations, findings, remediation validation, exceptions, and approvals.

The package should reference the exact versions and dates reviewed so later changes can be distinguished from the assessed state.

## Chapter 30 — Management review and approval

Management should review whether significant risks, findings, exceptions, incidents, remediation, vendor issues, and regulatory changes have been addressed or consciously accepted by authorized decision-makers.

Approval should record reviewer, date, decision, evidence considered, findings, required remediation, exceptions, and residual risk. Approval without sufficient context or authority should not satisfy the human-review gate.

## Chapter 31 — Release and publication controls

Publication artifacts derived from this manual should preserve current-law/proposed-rule boundaries, legal and assurance disclaimers, source references, accessibility features, language metadata, reading order, links, tables, figures, alt text, and version/provenance information.

DOCX and PDF candidates should be regenerated after material source or content changes and subjected to page-level visual and accessibility QA before release.

## Chapter 32 — Closure, maintenance, and continuous improvement

Manual 06 closes only when source verification, controlled English, legal/editorial/technical review, localization and semantic review, accessibility, publication QA, repository-security review, provenance, exact-head reconciliation, and Final Human Release Approval are complete for the same candidate.

After release, new HHS/OCR rules, material guidance, organizational changes, incidents, audit findings, or control failures should trigger impact analysis and, where material, a new controlled review cycle. Completion is a controlled baseline, not a claim that future compliance or security is guaranteed.
