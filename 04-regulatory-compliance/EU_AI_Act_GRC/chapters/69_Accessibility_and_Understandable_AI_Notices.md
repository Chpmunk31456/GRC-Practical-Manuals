# Chapter 69 — Accessibility and Understandable AI Notices

## 69.1 Purpose

Transparency is not effective when a person cannot perceive, understand, navigate, or act on the notice. This chapter establishes controls for making AI-related notices accessible, understandable, timely, and usable across digital, physical, and assisted-service channels.

The governing principle is simple:

> A notice is not effective merely because it exists. It must reach the person, in a form the person can perceive and understand, before the AI interaction or exposure matters.

For GlobalWay Travel Services, this applies to customer chatbots, booking interfaces, mobile applications, kiosks, call-centre tools, disruption alerts, recruitment systems, employee applications, accessibility-assistance channels, and AI-generated public communications.

## 69.2 Legal and governance context

Article 50 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 requires specified providers and deployers to inform natural persons when they interact with certain AI systems or are exposed to specified AI-generated, manipulated, emotion-recognition, or biometric-categorisation uses. The transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026.

Accessibility obligations may also arise under other instruments, including the European Accessibility Act, national accessibility laws, transport-sector requirements, employment law, consumer-protection law, public-sector web accessibility rules, and contractual accessibility commitments.

GlobalWay must therefore treat accessibility as both:

- a condition of effective AI transparency; and
- a separate compliance and human-rights requirement where applicable.

This chapter does not replace jurisdiction-specific legal review.

## 69.3 Requirement pattern

**Requirement → Plain-language explanation → Travel-agency example → Control activity → Evidence → Audit test**

### Requirement

AI notices must be presented in a manner that affected persons can reasonably perceive, understand, and use in the relevant context.

### Plain-language explanation

A notice hidden in fine print, available only through a mouse, shown for two seconds, written in technical jargon, or inaccessible to a screen reader does not provide meaningful transparency.

### Travel-agency example

GlobalWay deploys an airport-disruption chatbot. A traveller using a screen reader must be told that the service is AI-enabled and must be able to reach a human agent without navigating an inaccessible visual-only control.

### Control activity

Accessibility and usability testing is built into design, procurement, release approval, change management, and post-deployment monitoring.

### Evidence

- accessibility requirements;
- design files and approved notice text;
- automated and manual test results;
- assistive-technology test records;
- user-research findings;
- defect and remediation logs;
- release approvals;
- accessibility statements;
- escalation and accommodation procedures.

### Audit test

Select a sample of AI notices across channels and verify that users can perceive, understand, navigate, and act on them using relevant assistive technologies and alternative interaction modes.

## 69.4 Accessibility is a system property

GlobalWay must not treat accessibility as a final formatting check. It must be addressed across:

1. **content** — wording, structure, language, and reading level;
2. **interface** — navigation, focus, controls, timing, and error handling;
3. **media** — captions, transcripts, audio description, and alternatives;
4. **device compatibility** — desktop, mobile, kiosk, call centre, and assistive technology;
5. **process** — escalation, accommodation, correction, and human support;
6. **evidence** — repeatable testing and documented remediation.

## 69.5 Rehabilitation-engineering perspective

The control objective is to remove barriers in the environment, interface, workflow, and information architecture. The person is not the defect.

GlobalWay should evaluate the interaction between:

- the user’s abilities and preferences;
- the task being performed;
- the physical or digital environment;
- the interface and information design;
- assistive technologies;
- time pressure, fatigue, stress, and travel disruption;
- available human support.

This approach supports universal design while preserving individual accommodation paths when universal design alone is insufficient.

## 69.6 Notice content standard

Each AI notice should answer, in plain language:

1. **What is happening?**
2. **Where is AI involved?**
3. **What may the AI do?**
4. **What will a human decide or review?**
5. **What information is used?**
6. **What choices does the person have?**
7. **How can the person reach a human?**
8. **How can the person challenge, correct, or report a problem?**

Not every notice requires all eight points on the first screen. Layered disclosure may be used, provided the first layer communicates the essential fact and the deeper information remains easy to access.

## 69.7 Plain-language requirements

Notices should:

- use direct sentences and familiar words;
- avoid unnecessary legal and technical terminology;
- explain unavoidable terms;
- use meaningful headings;
- keep important actions close to the explanation;
- avoid ambiguity about whether the user is interacting with AI;
- distinguish AI assistance from human decision-making;
- use consistent terminology across channels;
- provide translated versions where required by user population, service context, or law.

A notice should not rely on colour, iconography, or animation alone to communicate meaning.

## 69.8 Visual accessibility

Controls should address:

- sufficient text and non-text contrast;
- scalable text without loss of content or function;
- readable spacing and line length;
- visible keyboard focus;
- meaningful heading hierarchy;
- text alternatives for informative images;
- avoidance of flashing or motion that may trigger harm;
- user control over moving or time-limited content;
- clear error identification and correction instructions;
- layouts that remain usable under magnification and reflow.

## 69.9 Hearing and audio accessibility

Where notices or explanations use audio or video, GlobalWay should provide, as applicable:

- accurate captions;
- transcripts;
- sign-language interpretation for high-impact or required contexts;
- visual alternatives to audio-only alerts;
- audio descriptions for important visual information;
- controls that do not depend solely on sound.

Automated captions may support delivery but must be reviewed when errors could alter legal, safety, travel, employment, or financial meaning.

## 69.10 Motor and interaction accessibility

AI notices and controls should be operable without requiring precise pointer movement, complex gestures, or rapid responses.

Controls include:

- complete keyboard operation;
- logical focus order;
- adequate target size and spacing;
- no keyboard traps;
- alternatives to drag-and-drop or gesture-only interaction;
- sufficient time to read and respond;
- the ability to pause, extend, or repeat timed notices;
- compatibility with switch, speech, and alternative-input technologies.

## 69.11 Cognitive and communication accessibility

GlobalWay should account for cognitive load, language differences, neurodiversity, literacy variation, stress, fatigue, and time pressure.

Controls include:

- short, structured explanations;
- one primary action per step where practical;
- consistent labels and navigation;
- examples for unfamiliar concepts;
- confirmation before consequential actions;
- prevention of dark patterns or misleading urgency;
- easy return to prior information;
- support for alternative communication methods;
- access to a human representative.

## 69.12 Screen-reader and semantic requirements

Digital notices must use semantic structure rather than visual appearance alone.

GlobalWay should verify:

- correct document or page language metadata;
- meaningful page titles;
- headings used in logical order;
- programmatically associated labels and instructions;
- status messages announced without forcing focus;
- accessible names for buttons, links, and controls;
- meaningful link text;
- tables with proper headers and reading order;
- alternative text that communicates purpose rather than appearance alone;
- no inaccessible text embedded only inside images.

## 69.13 Multichannel equivalence

Where an AI service is available through multiple channels, essential transparency and human-support options must remain materially equivalent.

Examples include:

- web and mobile booking;
- airport or hotel kiosks;
- telephone and voice assistants;
- email, SMS, and push notifications;
- customer-service chat;
- accessible travel-assistance desks;
- employee and recruitment portals.

A person must not lose a substantive right or choice merely because they use an accessibility channel.

## 69.14 Timing and persistence

A notice must be provided early enough to influence the person’s decision or behaviour.

GlobalWay should ensure that:

- chatbot notices appear before or at the start of the AI interaction;
- emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation notices appear before exposure;
- AI-generated content disclosures accompany the relevant content;
- time-sensitive notices remain available long enough to perceive and understand;
- users can reopen or retrieve the notice later;
- material notice changes trigger renewed presentation where appropriate.

## 69.15 Human support and accommodation

Accessible transparency requires a practical alternative when the standard interface remains unsuitable.

GlobalWay must define:

- how a person requests human assistance;
- how a person requests an accommodation or alternative format;
- who receives and resolves the request;
- response-time expectations;
- escalation for travel disruption, safety, employment, or other high-impact situations;
- how the organisation prevents retaliation or disadvantage for requesting assistance.

The human-support route must itself be accessible.

## 69.16 GlobalWay example — disrupted traveller

A traveller with low vision receives an AI-generated rebooking recommendation after a flight cancellation.

**AI may:**

- identify available flights and hotels;
- rank options using approved criteria;
- explain operational constraints;
- prepare a proposed itinerary.

**Human decision:**

- approve exceptions;
- resolve disability-assistance conflicts;
- confirm high-cost or safety-sensitive changes;
- handle complaints and overrides.

**Accessibility controls:**

- screen-reader-compatible notice;
- high-contrast and scalable presentation;
- no colour-only status indicators;
- keyboard-operable choices;
- plain-language explanation;
- telephone and human-agent alternative;
- preserved accessibility-service requirements in the rebooking workflow.

**Evidence:**

- design acceptance criteria;
- screen-reader and keyboard test results;
- user-journey test record;
- human-escalation logs;
- defect remediation evidence.

**Audit test:**

Reperform the disrupted-traveller journey using keyboard-only navigation, screen magnification, and a screen reader. Verify that the AI notice, choices, consequences, and human escalation are all available and understandable.

## 69.17 GlobalWay example — recruitment notice

A candidate uses a GlobalWay recruitment portal containing an AI-enabled résumé-screening function.

The notice must not merely say “technology may be used.” It should clearly explain the role of AI, the role of human reviewers, relevant candidate choices, and how to request an accommodation or correction.

The accommodation path must not disclose disability information more broadly than necessary or negatively influence the employment decision.

## 69.18 Procurement requirements

Vendor due diligence for AI interfaces should require evidence of:

- conformance testing against applicable accessibility standards;
- keyboard and screen-reader compatibility;
- captions and transcripts where relevant;
- language metadata and localisation support;
- accessible error handling;
- known limitations and remediation plans;
- accessibility regression testing;
- support for user-configurable presentation;
- accessible documentation and support channels;
- contract rights to test, remediate, and terminate for unresolved material defects.

A vendor accessibility statement is evidence, not proof.

## 69.19 Change management and regression testing

Accessibility controls can fail after seemingly minor changes.

Regression testing is required when GlobalWay changes:

- notice wording or placement;
- interface components;
- colour, typography, spacing, or layout;
- authentication or consent flows;
- chatbot frameworks;
- mobile or kiosk software;
- media players;
- language versions;
- vendor models or embedded tools;
- escalation routes.

High-impact defects should block release.

## 69.20 Stop and escalation conditions

Deployment or publication must stop when:

- the notice cannot be perceived by a material user group;
- essential controls are not keyboard accessible;
- screen readers receive incorrect or incomplete information;
- captions or translations materially alter meaning;
- the human-escalation route is unavailable;
- a time-limited notice disappears before it can reasonably be understood;
- testing reveals exclusion from a consequential travel, employment, or service decision;
- the organisation cannot explain who owns remediation.

## 69.22 Evidence register

Minimum evidence may include:

- approved accessibility requirements;
- applicable legal and standards assessment;
- user stories and acceptance criteria;
- notice inventory;
- plain-language review record;
- automated scan results;
- keyboard and screen-reader test scripts;
- assistive-technology test results;
- captions, transcripts, and translation QA;
- user-testing records involving persons with disabilities;
- issue logs and remediation evidence;
- release approvals and exceptions;
- vendor accessibility documentation;
- accommodation and escalation records;
- complaint and incident trends.

## 69.23 Metrics and reporting

Useful measures include:

- percentage of AI notices passing automated accessibility checks;
- percentage passing manual keyboard and screen-reader testing;
- open defects by severity and age;
- time to remediate critical accessibility defects;
- percentage of AI interfaces with accessible human escalation;
- notice comprehension test results;
- abandonment rate at AI disclosure points;
- accommodation request response time;
- repeat complaints by channel or disability-related barrier;
- accessibility regression failures after release.

Metrics must not be used to identify or profile individuals with disabilities unnecessarily.

## 69.24 Audit programme

Auditors should:

1. obtain the inventory of AI notices and delivery channels;
2. identify applicable legal and contractual accessibility requirements;
3. sample high-impact and high-volume use cases;
4. inspect notice timing, wording, placement, and persistence;
5. perform keyboard-only testing;
6. perform screen-reader testing;
7. inspect captions, transcripts, and alternatives;
8. review language metadata, semantic structure, and reading order;
9. test human escalation and accommodation paths;
10. inspect defects, exceptions, and remediation evidence;
11. verify that changes trigger regression testing;
12. report barriers by severity, user impact, and legal exposure.

## 69.27 Human concern

People may hesitate to disclose a disability or request assistance because they fear delay, embarrassment, discrimination, or loss of service.

GlobalWay should make accessible options visible without requiring unnecessary disclosure. The organisation should also make clear that requesting human help or an alternative format will not reduce service quality or negatively affect an employment, travel, or customer decision.

## 69.28 Management checklist

Before approving an AI notice, management should confirm:

- the notice clearly states where AI is involved;
- the essential message is understandable without specialist knowledge;
- the notice works with keyboard and screen readers;
- text can be resized and reflowed;
- captions, transcripts, and alternatives are present where needed;
- timing does not prevent comprehension;
- language and semantic metadata are correct;
- the human-support route is accessible;
- translations preserve legal and operational meaning;
- defects are documented and remediated;
- the release has appropriate legal and accessibility approval;
- monitoring and regression testing are active.

## 69.29 Key takeaway

Accessible AI transparency is not achieved by adding a disability icon or publishing a separate accessibility statement. It is achieved when people can perceive the notice, understand what AI is doing, make an informed choice, reach a human, and challenge or correct the outcome through the channel that works for them.
