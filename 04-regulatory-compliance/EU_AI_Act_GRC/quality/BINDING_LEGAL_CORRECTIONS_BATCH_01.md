# Binding Legal Corrections — Batch 01

**Status:** Controlling publication correction package

**Scope:** Foundation timeline; Chapters 2–6, 18, 20–21, 26–35; Appendices C–F; related figures and translations.

**Authority:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Where this correction package conflicts with an earlier draft, summary, table, figure, appendix, or translation, this package controls until the affected file is directly revised and reverified.

## 1. Current binding source baseline

Use the following legal hierarchy:

1. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended.
2. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Digital Omnibus on AI.
3. Current consolidated EUR-Lex text.
4. Official European Commission and EU AI Office guidance, clearly identified as non-binding unless the instrument itself has binding force.
5. Harmonised standards and common specifications when formally available and applicable.

Draft guidance, consultation documents, press releases, FAQs, and secondary commentary must not be presented as binding law.

## 2. Corrected implementation timeline

| Date | Binding milestone |
|---|---|
| 1 August 2024 | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 entered into force. |
| 2 February 2025 | Chapters I and II began applying, except the later-added Article 5 provisions identified below. This includes the original prohibited-practice rules, definitions, and AI-literacy obligations. |
| 2 August 2025 | Chapter III, Section 4; Chapter V; Chapter VII; Chapter XII; and Article 78 began applying, subject to the Article 101 exception and other transitional provisions. GPAI governance and provider obligations therefore began applying from this date. |
| 27 July 2026 | Articles 102–110 apply under amended Article 113(d). Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 also entered into force on this date. |
| 2 August 2026 | The general application date for provisions not subject to a specific exception or later date. Article 50 applies from this date, subject to the specific transition for certain pre-existing generative systems. |
| 2 December 2026 | Article 5(1), first subparagraph, points (ba) and (bb), and Article 5(1a) and (1b) apply. These include the new prohibitions concerning AI systems generating non-consensual sexually explicit or intimate content and child sexual abuse material. |
| 2 December 2026 | Providers of AI systems, including GPAI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video, or text content and placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must take the necessary steps to comply with Article 50(2). |
| 2 August 2027 | Providers of GPAI models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 must comply, subject to the applicable transitional provisions. |
| 2 December 2027 | Chapter III, Sections 1–3, except Article 6(5), apply to high-risk AI systems classified under Article 6(2) and Annex III. |
| 2 August 2028 | Chapter III, Sections 1–3, except Article 6(5), apply to high-risk AI systems classified under Article 6(1) and Annex I. |
| 2 August 2030 | Public-authority providers and deployers covered by the amended Article 2(2) transition must take the necessary steps to comply, where that provision applies. |

## 3. Mandatory wording for high-risk timing

Do not say that “all high-risk AI obligations apply from 2 December 2027” or “all high-risk product obligations apply from 2 August 2028.”

Use this formulation:

> Under amended Article 113(c), Chapter III, Sections 1, 2, and 3—except Article 6(5)—apply from 2 December 2027 for systems classified as high-risk under Article 6(2) and Annex III, and from 2 August 2028 for systems classified as high-risk under Article 6(1) and Annex I. Other provisions may have earlier, later, or separate application dates and must be assessed individually.

Every high-risk roadmap, checklist, conformity-readiness document, and figure must preserve this narrow scope.

## 4. Mandatory correction for Chapter 34

Chapter 34 must state:

- the new prohibitions are binding amendments, not proposals;
- the relevant provisions apply from **2 December 2026**, not 2 February 2025;
- adoption, entry into force, and application are distinct concepts;
- non-consensual sexually explicit or intimate content and child sexual abuse material are separate statutory categories;
- each assessment must map the exact statutory elements and avoid treating every harmful intimate-content scenario as automatically satisfying the same legal test;
- suspected cases must be suspended and escalated to qualified legal and safeguarding personnel;
- evidence of rejection, suspension, reporting, and decommissioning must be retained.

## 5. Element-by-element prohibited-practice test

Chapters 26–34 and Appendix D must include, for each prohibited-practice category:

1. exact Article 5 paragraph and point;
2. actor and conduct covered;
3. purpose, context, intent, or harm threshold where applicable;
4. affected population or protected characteristic where applicable;
5. exceptions, authorisations, and safeguards;
6. distinction between prohibited and regulated-but-permitted uses;
7. legal escalation trigger for ambiguity;
8. required suspension, rejection, or decommissioning action;
9. evidence retained;
10. reassessment trigger after a material change.

## 6. Biometric and emotion-recognition precision

### Emotion recognition

Do not state that all emotion-recognition systems are prohibited. Assess the setting, purpose, workplace or education context, medical or safety exception, and other applicable requirements.

### Biometric categorisation

Do not state that all biometric categorisation is prohibited. Determine whether the system infers sensitive or protected characteristics within the statutory prohibition and whether another regulated category applies.

### Remote biometric identification

Distinguish:

- real-time and post remote biometric identification;
- law-enforcement and non-law-enforcement use;
- publicly accessible spaces;
- permitted purposes;
- prior authorisation, necessity, proportionality, temporal and geographic limits, and other safeguards;
- high-risk classification and transparency obligations where the use is not prohibited.

## 7. Scope and role corrections

Chapters 2–5 and 18 and Appendix F must verify the current amended wording for:

- provider;
- deployer;
- importer;
- distributor;
- authorised representative;
- product manufacturer;
- actors established outside the Union;
- placing on the market and putting into service;
- substantial modification;
- Annex I product-sector interactions;
- the amended treatment of Section B product legislation and machinery.

No organization should assign a role solely by contract label. Role follows the facts, conduct, branding, modification, market placement, and intended purpose.

## 8. Appendix corrections

### Appendix C — Applicability Assessment

Add explicit fields for:

- amended regulation version checked;
- applicable Article 113 date;
- Article 2 exclusions or special product-sector treatment;
- pre-existing-system transitional rule;
- Article 50(2) legacy-system deadline;
- Article 5 points (ba) and (bb) screening.

### Appendix D — Prohibited-Practice Checklist

Add the element-by-element test in Section 5 and the 2 December 2026 date for the newly added prohibitions.

### Appendix E — High-Risk Classification Worksheet

Separate:

- Article 6(1) / Annex I classification;
- Article 6(2) / Annex III classification;
- Article 6(5);
- Chapter III Sections 1–3 application date;
- other independently applicable obligations.

### Appendix F — Role Assessment Worksheet

Revalidate every role definition against the amended consolidated text and include product-manufacturer and substantial-modification consequences.

## 9. Graphics controls

Quarantine or replace any graphic that:

- shows 2 February 2025 as the application date for the newly added intimate-content or child-sexual-abuse-material prohibitions;
- presents 2 August 2026 as the universal date for every high-risk requirement;
- omits the 27 July 2026, 2 December 2026, 2 December 2027, or 2 August 2028 milestones where relevant;
- describes all emotion recognition, biometric categorisation, or remote biometric identification as categorically prohibited;
- cites a superseded article number or retention period.

## 10. Translation control

Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese translation must not begin for affected passages until the English legal corrections are applied and closed. All dates, article references, exceptions, and role definitions must be translated from the legally cleared English source, not from earlier drafts.

## 11. Closure criteria

Batch 1 is closed only when:

- Appendices C–F are directly revised;
- affected graphics are corrected or removed;
