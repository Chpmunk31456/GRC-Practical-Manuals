# Chapter 120 — Regulatory-Examination Readiness

## Purpose

This chapter explains how organizations should prepare to respond promptly, accurately, consistently, and transparently to regulatory examinations, supervisory inquiries, inspections, and evidence requests concerning AI systems.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should maintain documented regulatory-readiness procedures, accountable response teams, current evidence, legal and technical review capability, communication controls, and escalation paths. Responses should be complete, truthful, traceable to reliable evidence, and consistent across legal entities, systems, and jurisdictions.

## Plain-language explanation

Regulatory readiness is not a last-minute document exercise. An organization should already know which systems are in scope, who is accountable, what evidence exists, where it is stored, and how to answer questions without contradiction or delay.

## Readiness framework

Establish:

- a regulatory-response policy;
- named legal, compliance, technical, privacy, security, and business contacts;
- jurisdiction and authority mapping;
- secure evidence repositories;
- document-retention and legal-hold procedures;
- response approval and quality-control steps;
- translation and accessibility support;
- executive and board escalation criteria;
- procedures for onsite or remote inspection.

## Core evidence set

Maintain current and retrievable:

- AI inventory and ownership records;
- applicability, role, and classification assessments;
- prohibited-practice screening;
- risk and fundamental-rights assessments;
- technical documentation;
- quality-management records;
- data-governance evidence;
- validation and testing results;
- transparency and human-oversight materials;
- logs and monitoring records;
- vendor and contractual evidence;
- incident, complaint, and corrective-action records;
- conformity and registration records where applicable;
- exceptions and risk acceptances;
- board and management reporting.

## Request intake and control

When an inquiry is received:

1. authenticate the authority and request;
2. preserve the original communication;
3. identify deadlines, scope, jurisdiction, and confidentiality requirements;
4. activate the response team;
5. issue preservation instructions where appropriate;
6. assign each request item to an accountable owner;
7. track collection, review, approval, and delivery;
8. record assumptions, limitations, and follow-up commitments.

## Response quality

Responses should be:

- factually accurate;
- supported by cited evidence;
- limited to the question while remaining complete;
- consistent with prior submissions and public statements;
- reviewed for legal privilege and confidentiality;
- clear about uncertainty, missing information, and remediation;
- version controlled;
- approved by authorized personnel;
- delivered through approved secure channels.

Never create, alter, backdate, conceal, or selectively destroy evidence in response to an inquiry.

## Interviews and demonstrations

Prepare personnel to:

- explain their actual responsibilities;
- distinguish documented practice from planned improvement;
- describe system limitations and incidents candidly;
- avoid speculation;
- identify the evidence supporting an answer;
- escalate questions outside their expertise;
- demonstrate relevant controls and systems safely.

Use mock interviews and system demonstrations for high-risk or complex systems.

## Examination simulation

Periodically simulate:

- urgent information requests;
- technical-documentation production;
- system and model-version reconciliation;
- incident timeline reconstruction;
- regulator interviews;
- onsite access requests;
- cross-border coordination;
- confidential-data handling;
- evidence gaps and corrective-action disclosure.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

A national authority asks GlobalWay for evidence concerning its recruitment-screening system, including classification, bias testing, human oversight, and vendor changes. GlobalWay activates its response team, preserves the request, verifies the production version, and uses its evidence index to assemble approved records.

During review, the team finds that one vendor-change assessment is incomplete. Rather than obscure the gap, GlobalWay discloses it, explains the interim controls, provides the remediation plan, and commits to a verified completion date.

## Control activities

- Maintain a regulatory-response plan and authority map.
- Keep critical AI evidence current and indexed.
- Authenticate and centrally track requests.
- Apply legal, technical, and factual quality review.
- Preserve evidence and manage legal holds.
- Conduct mock examinations and interviews.
- Escalate material gaps and inconsistent statements.
- Track commitments made to authorities through closure.

## Evidence

- regulatory-response policy;
- authority and jurisdiction map;
- response-team roster;
- evidence index;
- request and production logs;
- approval records;
- legal-hold notices;
- interview preparation materials;
- mock-examination results;
- correspondence with authorities;
- commitment and remediation tracker;
- lessons-learned reviews.

## Audit tests

1. Select material AI systems and test whether core evidence can be produced promptly.
2. Review response-team roles, escalation, and approval authority.
3. Trace prior regulatory requests from intake through delivery and commitments.
4. Verify preservation and legal-hold procedures operated correctly.
5. Compare submissions with internal records and public statements.
6. Review mock-examination findings and remediation.
7. Confirm commitments to authorities were tracked and closed with evidence.

## Metrics

- time to acknowledge and fulfill requests;
- overdue regulatory commitments;
- evidence-production failures;
- inconsistent or corrected submissions;
- material gaps found during mock examinations;
- systems without current evidence indexes;
- legal-hold or preservation failures;
- response-team training completion;
- repeat examination findings.

## Management checklist

- Can we identify every material AI system and accountable owner quickly?
- Can we produce current, version-matched evidence?
- Are regulatory responses centrally controlled and approved?
- Can personnel explain actual practice without speculation?
- Are known gaps disclosed and remediated transparently?
- Are commitments to authorities tracked through verified closure?
