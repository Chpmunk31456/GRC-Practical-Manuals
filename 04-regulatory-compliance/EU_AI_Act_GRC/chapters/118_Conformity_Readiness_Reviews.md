# Chapter 118 — Conformity-Readiness Reviews

## Purpose

This chapter explains how to evaluate whether an AI system and its supporting governance, documentation, controls, and evidence are ready for applicable conformity-assessment activities.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations responsible for high-risk AI systems should perform structured readiness reviews before formal conformity assessment, market placement, putting into service, substantial modification, or other legally significant events. The review should confirm that applicable requirements, documentation, testing, quality-management controls, registrations, declarations, and evidence are complete, current, internally consistent, and traceable to the exact system version.

## Plain-language explanation

A conformity-readiness review is a disciplined pre-assessment. It identifies gaps before they become formal nonconformities, delays, regulatory exposure, or deployment failures. It is not a substitute for any required external assessment, but it improves the quality and defensibility of the evidence package.

## Readiness scope

Define:

- legal entity and responsible role;
- intended purpose and system classification;
- system, model, software, and data versions;
- applicable conformity route;
- harmonized standards or common specifications relied upon;
- lifecycle stage and target date;
- jurisdictions and sector requirements;
- third parties and notified bodies where relevant;
- substantial-modification considerations.

## Review domains

Assess at minimum:

- quality-management system;
- risk-management system;
- data and data-governance practices;
- technical documentation;
- record-keeping and logging;
- transparency and instructions for use;
- human oversight;
- accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity, and resilience;
- validation and testing;
- post-market monitoring;
- serious-incident and corrective-action processes;
- registration and declaration requirements;
- document control and version traceability.

## Evidence-package integrity

Confirm that evidence:

- relates to the exact system and version under review;
- identifies owners, dates, approvals, and scope;
- is complete and reproducible;
- does not contain contradictory claims;
- distinguishes provider and deployer responsibilities;
- identifies unresolved limitations and accepted risks;
- remains accessible for the required period;
- is protected against unauthorized alteration;
- can be produced promptly to assessors or authorities.

## Gap classification

Classify gaps as:

- blocking: prevents lawful progression or meaningful assessment;
- major: significant weakness requiring remediation before approval;
- minor: limited weakness that does not undermine the overall system;
- observation: improvement opportunity or emerging concern.

Define owners, deadlines, retesting, and approval conditions for each gap.

## Dry-run review

Conduct a simulated assessment using:

- document requests;
- evidence walkthroughs;
- interviews with accountable owners;
- traceability tests from requirement to control to evidence;
- re-performance of selected technical tests;
- version and configuration verification;
- challenge of assumptions and exceptions;
- sample regulator or assessor questions.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay prepares a recruitment-screening system for conformity assessment. The readiness review finds that technical documentation references model version 4.1, while fairness testing covers version 4.0, and the human-oversight procedure does not reflect a recent interface change.

GlobalWay classifies both gaps as blocking, updates the documentation, repeats targeted testing on version 4.1, retrains reviewers, and performs a second readiness review before proceeding.

## Control activities

- Establish conformity-readiness criteria and ownership.
- Confirm the applicable assessment route.
- Freeze and identify the system version under review.
- Build a requirement-to-evidence index.
- Perform independent legal, technical, and documentation challenge.
- Classify and remediate gaps.
- Revalidate after material change.
- Require formal readiness approval before progression.

## Evidence

- conformity strategy;
- applicability and classification assessments;
- readiness checklist;
- requirement-to-evidence matrix;
- quality-management records;
- technical documentation;
- validation and test results;
- gap register;
- remediation and retest evidence;
- readiness approval;
- assessor or notified-body correspondence where applicable.

## Audit tests

1. Verify the conformity route and responsible legal role were documented.
2. Trace selected requirements to controls and evidence.
3. Confirm all evidence matches the system version under review.
4. Review unresolved gaps and approval conditions.
5. Reperform selected document and technical traceability tests.
6. Confirm material changes trigger renewed readiness review.
7. Verify management did not proceed while blocking gaps remained open.

## Metrics

- systems approaching conformity milestones;
- blocking and major readiness gaps;
- evidence items missing or inconsistent;
- time to remediate readiness findings;
- repeated version-traceability failures;
- assessments delayed by documentation gaps;
- systems approved with conditions;
- changes requiring renewed review.

## Management checklist

- Is the conformity route correct?
- Does every material requirement have current evidence?
- Does the evidence match the exact system version?
- Are blocking gaps able to stop progression?
- Can the organization respond confidently to assessor challenge?
- Will material change trigger renewed review?
