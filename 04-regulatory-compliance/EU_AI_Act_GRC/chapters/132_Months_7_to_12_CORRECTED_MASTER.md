# Chapter 132 — Months 7–12

> **Legal status:** Corrected English master for consolidation. This chapter is a recommended implementation roadmap, not a statutory timetable. It controls over conflicting earlier Chapter 132 language.

## Requirement

Organizations should use months 7–12 to mature AI governance, complete priority remediation, extend assurance coverage, validate operating effectiveness, and institutionalize monitoring, reporting, and continuous improvement.

## Plain-English explanation

The first year should end with a defensible operating model rather than a collection of disconnected projects. The EU AI Act does not require this roadmap to be completed within twelve months. Binding duties, effective dates, conformity requirements, and authority expectations must be tracked separately.

## Months 7–9 priorities

1. expand control testing across material systems and business units;
2. validate post-market monitoring, incident, complaint, and corrective-action processes;
3. reassess systems affected by significant model, data, purpose, supplier, or jurisdiction changes;
4. complete high-priority supplier remediation and continuity planning;
5. improve data quality, technical documentation, logs, and version linkage;
6. review transparency notices, affected-person communication, and accessibility;
7. test human oversight and escalation in realistic operating conditions.

## Months 10–12 priorities

1. perform enterprise-wide maturity and residual-risk assessment;
2. refresh the AI inventory, role map, and classification register;
3. conduct internal audit or independent assurance over selected high-risk areas;
4. validate readiness for applicable conformity and regulatory interactions;
5. approve the next annual plan, budget, staffing, technology, and training priorities;
6. report unresolved material risk, overdue remediation, and accepted exceptions to appropriate governance bodies.

## GlobalWay example

GlobalWay expands testing to recruitment, traveler-assistance, fraud, and workforce-allocation systems. It identifies inconsistent evidence retention in two business units, requires corrective action, and delays broader deployment of a supplier model until monitoring, incident, and exit controls are validated.

## Control activity

Management must conduct a formal first-year review that evaluates legal readiness, control effectiveness, evidence quality, unresolved risk, supplier dependence, incident performance, training effectiveness, and programme sustainability.

## Evidence

- updated inventory and classification records;
- control and assurance results;
- monitoring and incident records;
- change and reassessment files;
- supplier remediation and continuity evidence;
- maturity assessment;
- internal-audit or independent-assurance reports;
- approved annual plan and governance minutes.

## Audit test

Confirm that first-year conclusions were supported by current evidence, material systems received proportionate assurance, significant changes triggered reassessment, unresolved risk was escalated, and the next annual plan addressed identified weaknesses.
