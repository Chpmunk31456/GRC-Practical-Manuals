# Manual 46 — Universal AI Governance Foundation

**Build status:** CONTROLLED DEVELOPMENT

**Scope:** Universal, jurisdiction-aware enterprise AI governance foundation.

## Purpose

Manual 46 is the universal foundation for the AI-governance series. It is designed to apply across industries, jurisdictions, organization sizes, AI architectures and deployment models. It is not an interview manual and it is not tied to a single employer, regulator, standard or technology stack.

The manual establishes the common governance operating model used by later specialist manuals. Jurisdiction-, framework-, technology- and interview-specific material is treated as an extension of this universal core.

## Universal governance objective

Enable an organization to govern AI consistently across the full lifecycle by integrating accountability, risk, legal obligations, security, privacy, data governance, human oversight, transparency, testing, assurance, monitoring, incident management, third-party governance, evidence and continuous improvement.

## Universal governance spine

**Accountability → Inventory → Classification → Risk/Impact Assessment → Data Governance → Security → Privacy → Human Oversight → Transparency → Testing/Validation → Documentation → Approval → Deployment → Monitoring → Incident/Change Management → Third-Party Governance → Evidence/Audit → Retirement → Continuous Improvement**

## Core principles

1. **Accountability is explicit.** Every AI system or use case has identifiable business and technical ownership.
2. **Governance is risk-based.** Control intensity scales with impact, autonomy, criticality, data sensitivity, reversibility and legal exposure.
3. **Governance covers systems, not only models.** Data, prompts, retrieval, tools, integrations, users, agents, vendors and downstream actions are in scope.
4. **Lifecycle governance starts before deployment.** Intake, classification and impact assessment precede production approval.
5. **Human oversight is meaningful.** Oversight must include actual authority, competence, information and the ability to intervene.
6. **Security and privacy are integral.** They are part of AI governance rather than separate downstream reviews.
7. **Evidence matters.** Governance decisions, control operation, validation and exceptions must be demonstrable.
8. **Third-party AI remains governed.** Outsourcing a model or platform does not outsource accountability.
9. **Monitoring is continuous.** Drift, misuse, incidents, model/provider changes and new legal obligations trigger review.
10. **Governance enables adoption.** The objective is safe, lawful and accountable use of AI, not indiscriminate restriction.

## Part VIII — Global framework interoperability

Manual 46 uses major frameworks as reference points without making any one of them the governing structure of the manual:

- EU AI Act.
- ISO/IEC 42001.
- NIST AI RMF and NIST AI 600-1.
- Singapore Model AI Governance Framework, AI Verify and Agentic AI materials.
- OECD AI Principles.
- Other jurisdictional or sector-specific requirements as applicable.

Framework-specific obligations and mappings belong in the later specialist manuals and crosswalks.
