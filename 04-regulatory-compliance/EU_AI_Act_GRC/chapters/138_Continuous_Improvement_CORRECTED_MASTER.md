# Chapter 138 — Continuous Improvement

> **Legal status:** Corrected English master for consolidation. This file controls over conflicting earlier Chapter 138 draft language.

## Requirement

Organizations should operate a documented continuous-improvement process that uses monitoring, incidents, complaints, audit findings, regulatory developments, technical changes, user feedback, performance data, and lessons learned to strengthen AI governance and compliance. Where the EU AI Act or other law requires corrective action, post-market monitoring, risk-management updates, quality-management updates, or documentation changes, those duties are binding.

## Plain-English explanation

AI governance cannot remain static. Models, data, suppliers, attack methods, business uses, legal requirements, and affected populations change. Improvement must therefore be systematic, evidence-based, risk-prioritized, and linked to accountable decisions rather than relying on informal lessons or periodic policy refreshes.

## Improvement cycle

The programme should:

1. collect signals from monitoring, logs, incidents, complaints, appeals, audits, testing, suppliers, and authorities;
2. determine whether the signal indicates nonconformity, emerging risk, substantial modification, changed intended purpose, or control weakness;
3. contain immediate risk and initiate legally required reporting or corrective action;
4. perform root-cause and affected-scope analysis;
5. update risk assessments, technical documentation, controls, instructions, notices, training, contracts, and monitoring as needed;
6. validate remediation and check for unintended consequences;
7. share lessons across relevant systems and business units;
8. track overdue actions and recurring failure patterns;
9. measure whether changes improved outcomes and control effectiveness;
10. preserve version-linked evidence and management approval.

## GlobalWay example

GlobalWay identifies repeated traveler-assistant escalation failures after supplier model updates. It strengthens change notification, regression testing, human-oversight staffing, supplier escalation, and release gates, then validates improvement through monitoring and independent testing across languages and travel-disruption scenarios.

## Control activity

Material AI findings, incidents, complaints, legal changes, and system changes must enter a controlled improvement workflow with severity, ownership, deadlines, validation criteria, and closure approval. Improvements may not weaken mandatory controls or alter intended purpose without appropriate reassessment.

## Evidence

- monitoring and signal register;
- incident, complaint, and audit records;
- root-cause and scope analysis;
- corrective and preventive action plans;
- updated risk, control, and technical documentation;
- validation and regression-test results;
- lessons-learned records;
- management review and closure approval;
- trend and effectiveness metrics.

## Audit test

Select material incidents, complaints, findings, supplier changes, and legal developments. Confirm that signals entered the improvement process, immediate risk was addressed, root cause and scope were supportable, required artifacts were updated, remediation was validated, and recurring issues were escalated.

## Primary legal references

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended: Articles 9, 17, 20, 72, 73, and other applicable risk-management, quality-management, monitoring, incident, corrective-action, documentation, and authority-cooperation provisions.
- Applicable privacy, cybersecurity, product-safety, employment, consumer-protection, and sector-specific law.
- The broader continuous-improvement cycle in this chapter is a recommended management practice except where a cited legal duty makes a particular action mandatory.
- Current consolidated official texts control over older summaries.
