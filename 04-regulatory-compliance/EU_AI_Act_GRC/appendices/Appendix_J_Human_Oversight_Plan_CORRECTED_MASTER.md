## 2. Oversight objectives

Define the risks oversight is intended to prevent or reduce, including automation bias, over-reliance, foreseeable misuse, abnormal behavior, inaccurate or discriminatory output, safety risk, and use outside the intended purpose.

## 3. Provider-designed oversight measures

Record capabilities enabling people to:

- understand relevant capacities and limitations;
- remain aware of automation bias;
- correctly interpret output;
- disregard, override, or reverse output;
- interrupt or stop operation safely;
- detect anomalies and escalate;
- use logs, explanations, and source context needed for review.

## 6. Validation

Test comprehension, authority, override, stop, fallback, workload, response time, automation-bias resistance, accessibility, language, abnormal-condition detection, and escalation effectiveness.

## 7. Monitoring

Track acceptance, override, disagreement, escalation, error, subgroup outcome, complaint, fatigue, workload, training, and failed-intervention indicators.

## 8. Failure and fallback

Document safe suspension, manual alternative, incident response, evidence preservation, affected-person communication, continuity, and restoration approval.
