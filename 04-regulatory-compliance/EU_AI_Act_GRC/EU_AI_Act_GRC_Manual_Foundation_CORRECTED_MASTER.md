## 1. Editorial objective

The finished manual must be accurate, practical, readable, human-sounding, professionally presented, and suitable for executives, lawyers, compliance teams, auditors, security teams, procurement, HR, operations, technology teams, and nontechnical business owners.

Each substantive chapter should use the following sequence where appropriate:

1. requirement;
2. plain-English explanation;
3. GlobalWay Travel Services example;
4. control activity;
5. evidence;
6. audit test.

Binding legal duties, contractual duties, organization-imposed controls, recommended practices, and optional enhancements must be distinguished explicitly.

## 2. Authoritative source hierarchy

Use sources in the following order:

1. **Binding EU legislation and the current consolidated EUR-Lex text.**
2. **Binding delegated, implementing, or amending instruments.**
3. **Official European Commission and EU AI Office guidance**, clearly identified as non-binding unless incorporated through a binding instrument.
4. **Approved codes of practice, harmonised standards, common specifications, and recognized technical standards**, with legal status stated accurately.
5. **Secondary commentary**, used only for context and never as the sole authority for a legal conclusion.

## 3. Controlling implementation timeline

The timeline must be reverified against the current consolidated EUR-Lex text immediately before publication.

| Date | Binding application milestone |
|---|---|
| 1 August 2024 | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 entered into force. |
| 2 February 2025 | Chapters I and II began applying, including Article 4 AI-literacy duties and the original Article 5 prohibited practices, subject to later amendments. |
| 2 August 2025 | Governance provisions and obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models began applying under the applicable transitional rules. |
| 27 July 2026 | Articles 102–110 began applying under Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. |
| 2 August 2026 | General application date for provisions not subject to a specific exception or later transitional date. |
| 2 December 2026 | New Article 5 intimate-content and child-sexual-abuse-material prohibitions apply. Providers and deployers of synthetic-content systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must comply with Article 50(2) by this date. |
| 2 August 2027 | Existing GPAI models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 must comply, subject to the applicable transitional provisions. |
| 2 December 2027 | Chapter III, Sections 1–3 apply to high-risk AI systems classified under Article 6(2) and Annex III, except Article 6(5). |
| 2 August 2028 | Chapter III, Sections 1–3 apply to high-risk AI systems classified under Article 6(1) and Annex I, except Article 6(5). |
| 2 August 2030 | Providers and deployers of the specified legacy high-risk systems intended for use by public authorities must complete the applicable transitional compliance steps. |

### Timeline drafting controls

- Do not state that every high-risk obligation applies from 2 August 2026.
- Distinguish Article 6(2)/Annex III systems from Article 6(1)/Annex I product-safety systems.
- Identify the 2 December 2026 Article 5 and Article 50(2) rules where relevant.
- Record the exact provision, actor, trigger, date, and transitional treatment.
- Do not treat guidance, codes, standards, readiness methods, internal milestones, or maturity scores as statutory duties.

## 5. Recurring case study

**GlobalWay Travel Services** is a fictional multinational travel-management company serving corporate travelers and enterprise clients in the European Union and other regions.

Representative AI use cases include customer-support chatbots, itinerary recommendations, automated planning, travel-risk alerts, fraud detection, customer-service analytics, personalized offers, recruitment screening, employee analytics, supplier scoring, and generative-AI assistance for travel consultants.

## 6. Legal-modality standard

Use mandatory language only when supported by the applicable legal source, actor, trigger, and date.

- **Must / shall:** binding duty or approved internal control explicitly identified as such.
- **Should:** recommended governance or assurance practice.
- **May:** permitted option or optional enhancement.
- **Contractually required:** obligation arising from agreement rather than directly from the AI Act.
- **Organization requirement:** internal control adopted by management and not represented as statutory law.

When uncertainty remains, state the uncertainty and require qualified legal review rather than presenting a definitive conclusion.

## 7. Article-to-control and evidence framework

Every applicable legal element should be traceable to:

1. legal source and provision;
2. regulated actor and trigger;
3. application date and transition rule;
4. policy, procedure, and control identifier;
5. accountable owner and operator;
6. system, model, version, and jurisdiction;
7. required evidence and retention location;
8. test procedure and latest result;
9. gap, exception, remediation, or legal-review status.

## 8. Graphics and accessibility standard

Every retained figure must be original or properly licensed, legally accurate, accessible, and consistent with the surrounding chapter.

Each figure requires:

- figure number and descriptive caption;
- meaningful alt text;
- plain-language labels;
- adequate contrast;
- no reliance on color alone;
- readable text at normal page zoom;
- adjacent written explanation;
- verified terminology, dates, arrows, actor roles, and legal pathways.

A decorative or legally ambiguous graphic must be corrected or excluded from the publication build.
