# Chapter 134 — GPAI Readiness Roadmap

> **Legal status:** Corrected English master for consolidation. This chapter is a recommended readiness roadmap. It does not replace binding provider duties, systemic-risk obligations, AI Office supervision, or applicable codes and standards. It controls over conflicting earlier Chapter 134 language.

## Requirement

Organizations that develop, place on the market, modify, integrate, or rely on general-purpose AI models must determine their legal role and establish a readiness plan proportionate to whether they are a GPAI provider, a downstream provider, a deployer, or a provider of a GPAI model with systemic risk.

## Plain-English explanation

GPAI obligations differ from high-risk-system obligations. A company using a third-party model is not automatically the model provider, but integration, substantial modification, rebranding, or downstream system development can create additional duties. Systemic-risk designation creates enhanced evaluation, risk, cybersecurity, incident, and reporting obligations.

## Readiness workstreams

1. identify models, versions, providers, licences, integrations, fine-tuning, and downstream uses;
2. determine whether the organization is a GPAI provider, downstream provider, deployer, importer, distributor, or authorised representative;
3. document model capabilities, limitations, intended and foreseeable uses, and downstream dependencies;
4. prepare technical documentation and downstream information required by the applicable role;
5. establish and maintain a copyright-compliance policy and required training-content summary where applicable;
6. assess open-source exemptions and their limits;
7. determine whether systemic-risk criteria or designation apply;
8. for systemic-risk models, establish model evaluation, adversarial testing, systemic-risk assessment and mitigation, cybersecurity, incident reporting, and energy/resource reporting processes;
9. monitor applicable AI Office decisions, implementing acts, harmonised standards, and codes of practice;
10. align contractual, monitoring, change, and incident obligations across the supply chain.

## GlobalWay example

GlobalWay uses several external GPAI models in traveler assistance and document summarization. It records model versions and suppliers, reviews downstream documentation and contractual restrictions, evaluates whether fine-tuning or branding changes its legal role, and requires change notification, incident cooperation, and exit support from providers.

## Control activity

No material GPAI integration may proceed until the responsible governance functions confirm legal role, documentation sufficiency, copyright and data controls, supplier obligations, security testing, monitoring, and the effect of model or integration changes. Potential systemic-risk issues must be escalated promptly.

## Evidence

- GPAI inventory and role assessment;
- model and integration documentation;
- provider and downstream information;
- copyright policy and training-content summary where applicable;
- open-source analysis;
- systemic-risk assessment or designation records;
- evaluation, adversarial-testing, cybersecurity, and incident evidence;
- supplier contracts and change notifications;
- AI Office and code-of-practice monitoring records.

## Audit test

Select GPAI models and downstream integrations. Confirm that legal roles were supported, documentation and copyright duties were addressed, open-source treatment was not overstated, systemic-risk triggers were considered, supplier controls were enforceable, and material changes prompted reassessment.

## Primary legal references

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 where applicable: Articles 51–56, 88–93, Annexes XI–XIII, and related provisions, as applicable.
- Applicable AI Office decisions, implementing acts, and codes of practice must be identified by status; non-binding materials must not be represented as law.
- Current consolidated EUR-Lex text controls over older summaries.
