# Legal and Citation Audit — Batch 06 (Chapters 92–103)

## Scope

This batch governs the legal and citation review of Chapters 92–103 of the English EU AI Act GRC manual. It covers enterprise risk assessment, fundamental-rights, safety, bias, privacy, cybersecurity, explainability, human autonomy, operational resilience, third-party, legal, financial, reputational, residual-risk, and exception-management topics.

## Controlling legal baseline

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended.
- Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 where applicable.
- Current consolidated EUR-Lex text.
- Applicable Union and Member State law on data protection, equality, employment, consumer protection, cybersecurity, product safety, fundamental rights, and sector regulation.
- European Commission guidance, standards, codes, and frameworks must be identified as binding, voluntary, or non-binding according to their actual legal status.

## Cross-chapter legal corrections

1. Do not present enterprise risk categories as statutory AI Act classifications.
2. Distinguish provider, deployer, importer, distributor, authorised representative, product manufacturer, and GPAI-provider duties.
3. Link every risk assessment to a specific system or model, intended purpose, legal role, version, population, jurisdiction, and lifecycle stage.
4. Separate inherent risk, control design, operating effectiveness, residual risk, and formal risk acceptance.
5. Require reassessment after material technical, purpose, data, population, jurisdiction, supplier, or legal change.
6. Do not use risk acceptance to waive mandatory legal obligations.
7. Preserve evidence sufficient for conformity, monitoring, incident response, authority review, complaints, and audit.
8. Escalate unresolved legal interpretation to qualified counsel rather than guessing.

## Chapter-specific corrections

### Chapter 92 — Inherent Risk Assessment

- Define inherent risk as risk before considering controls, not as a legal classification.
- Require multidimensional assessment across rights, safety, privacy, security, discrimination, autonomy, operational, legal, financial, and reputational impact.
- Require separate likelihood, severity, scale, reversibility, affected-person vulnerability, and uncertainty analysis.

### Chapter 93 — Fundamental Rights Risk

- Map risks to relevant Charter rights and affected groups.
- Distinguish Article 27 fundamental-rights impact assessment duties from broader good-practice assessment.
- Require consultation, mitigation, monitoring, and complaint or redress pathways where applicable.

### Chapter 94 — Safety Risk

- Distinguish health, physical, psychological, societal, and product-safety risks.
- Link controls to Article 9 risk management, Article 15 robustness, applicable sector law, and post-market monitoring.
- Require foreseeable misuse, interaction, dependency, and emergency-state analysis.

### Chapter 95 — Bias and Discrimination Risk

- Avoid assuming statistical parity is always legally required or sufficient.
- Require legal context, protected-group analysis, proxy-variable review, subgroup testing, accessibility, and human-review controls.
- Distinguish data correction, model mitigation, process safeguards, and legal accommodation.

### Chapter 96 — Privacy and Data Protection Risk

- Keep GDPR and AI Act roles and obligations distinct but coordinated.
- Require lawful-basis, purpose-limitation, minimisation, special-category-data, DPIA, data-subject-rights, retention, and processor-controller analysis.
- Do not imply AI Act compliance establishes GDPR compliance.

### Chapters 97–103

- Cybersecurity, explainability, autonomy, operational resilience, third-party, legal, financial, reputational, and residual-risk chapters must distinguish legal duties from recommended enterprise controls.
- Risk acceptance must be documented, time-limited, within authority, and prohibited where it would approve unlawful use or noncompliance with mandatory obligations.

## Evidence expectations

- approved risk methodology;
- system-specific risk assessments;
- legal-role and classification records;
- rights, safety, privacy, security, bias, and autonomy analyses;
- control mappings and test evidence;
- residual-risk calculations and uncertainty statements;
- mitigation, escalation, and acceptance records;
- reassessment history and change triggers;
- management and board reporting.

## Closure criteria

This batch remains open until corrected masters for Chapters 92–103 are consolidated, citations are verified against current official sources, terminology is consistent, and all audit tests can independently establish design and operating effectiveness.
