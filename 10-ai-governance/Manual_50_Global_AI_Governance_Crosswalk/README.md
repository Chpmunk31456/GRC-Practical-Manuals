## Scope

The manual harmonizes enterprise AI-governance controls without treating different legal, standards, assurance, and policy regimes as equivalent. It preserves source-specific legal scope, actor definitions, thresholds, assurance mechanisms, evidence requirements, and normative status.

Core control domains include governance and accountability, AI inventory, role and value-chain responsibility, risk classification, impact assessment, data governance and privacy, security and robustness, transparency, human oversight, TEVV, documentation, deployment approval, third-party risk, monitoring, incident management, change/revalidation, independent assurance, AI literacy, continuous improvement, and agent identity/autonomy/action provenance.

## Evidence rule

Evidence may be reused across regimes only when the target relationship, scope, sufficiency, limitations, and additional source-specific evidence are explicitly documented. A blank or qualified mapping is preferred over unsupported equivalence.

## Source-status discipline

- EU AI Act: binding law within scope; legal obligations remain source-specific.
- ISO/IEC 42001: voluntary management-system standard unless otherwise incorporated; alignment is not certification.
- NIST AI RMF / AI 600-1: voluntary framework/profile guidance.
- Singapore frameworks / AI Verify: governance and assurance guidance/tools, not automatic legal certification.
- OECD AI Principles: intergovernmental policy principles, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific law.
