# Chapter 130 — Days 31–90

## Purpose

This chapter provides a practical implementation plan for days 31 through 90, moving from initial control and triage to repeatable governance, formal risk assessment, prioritized remediation, and measurable accountability.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

By day 90, the organization should have a validated inventory, approved governance structure, risk-tiered assessment process, defined control baseline, active remediation programme, initial AI-literacy training, and executive reporting supported by reliable evidence.

## Plain-language explanation

The second and third months convert emergency actions into operating processes. The goal is to stop relying on one-time spreadsheets and informal decisions. Intake, classification, review, approval, monitoring, and escalation should begin functioning as normal business controls.

## Days 31–45: validate scope and accountability

- reconcile the rapid inventory with procurement, cloud, software, data, and business records;
- confirm system, business, technical, risk, and data owners;
- document provider, deployer, importer, distributor, authorized-representative, or product-manufacturer roles where relevant;
- identify legal entities, jurisdictions, sectors, and affected populations;
- establish inventory quality checks and recurring attestations;
- close or escalate ownerless and undocumented systems.

## Days 46–60: assess and classify

Perform proportionate assessments covering:

- EU AI Act applicability and role;
- prohibited-practice screening;
- high-risk classification;
- transparency obligations;
- GPAI dependencies;
- privacy and fundamental-rights impacts;
- cybersecurity and resilience;
- human oversight;
- third-party and concentration risk;
- substantial-modification risk.

Record uncertainty and require legal or specialist review where classification is disputed.

## Days 61–75: implement baseline controls

Operationalize controls for:

- intake and approval;
- inventory and ownership;
- data governance;
- technical documentation;
- validation and testing;
- transparency notices;
- human oversight;
- logging and monitoring;
- incident management;
- vendor due diligence and contracting;
- change management;
- evidence retention;
- exceptions and residual-risk acceptance.

Integrate key controls into procurement, development, release, privacy, security, HR, and third-party workflows.

## Days 76–90: measure, train, and report

- launch role-based AI-literacy training;
- establish key risk and control indicators;
- begin control self-assessments;
- identify evidence gaps;
- test selected high-priority controls;
- define issue severity and remediation standards;
- establish executive and board dashboards;
- approve resource, budget, and technology needs;
- confirm the months 4–12 assurance plan.

## Prioritization method

Prioritize remediation using:

- legal deadline and classification;
- potential safety or rights impact;
- scale and sensitivity;
- control weakness;
- incident or complaint history;
- system change velocity;
- vendor dependency;
- evidence availability;
- ease of immediate risk reduction.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

During days 31–90, GlobalWay validates its 42-system inventory and identifies four additional embedded AI features. It completes classification for all critical systems, establishes intake and release gates, issues mandatory vendor clauses, trains product owners and travel-consultant supervisors, and begins monthly executive reporting.

Its recruitment tool remains restricted until bias, oversight, and vendor-documentation gaps are closed. The traveler assistant proceeds under enhanced monitoring and human-escalation controls.

## Control activities

- Validate inventory completeness and ownership.
- Complete risk-tiered applicability and classification assessments.
- Establish the enterprise control baseline and overlays.
- Integrate controls into business and technology workflows.
- Launch role-based training.
- Define evidence, testing, and remediation standards.
- Establish executive and board reporting.
- Approve the assurance and resource plan.

## Evidence

- validated inventory;
- role and classification assessments;
- control library and overlays;
- approved procedures;
- workflow and release-gate records;
- vendor due-diligence files;
- training records;
- control self-assessments;
- issue and remediation register;
- dashboards and governance minutes;
- assurance plan.

## Audit tests

1. Reconcile the inventory to independent source systems.
2. Verify ownership and legal-role decisions for critical systems.
3. Sample classification assessments for evidence and approval.
4. Confirm key controls are embedded in operational workflows.
5. Review training scope and completion by role.
6. Trace dashboard metrics to reliable source data.
7. Verify high-priority deficiencies have accountable remediation plans.

## Metrics

- inventory validation rate;
- systems with completed classification;
- critical systems with full control mapping;
- overdue assessments;
- systems lacking required evidence;
- training completion by role;
- high-severity deficiencies;
- overdue remediation;
- vendor contracts missing required clauses;
- control-test pass rate.

## Management checklist

- Is the inventory complete enough for decision-making?
- Are role and risk classifications approved?
- Are lifecycle controls operating in real workflows?
- Are people trained for their responsibilities?
- Can management see material gaps and overdue actions?
- Is the next assurance phase funded and scheduled?
