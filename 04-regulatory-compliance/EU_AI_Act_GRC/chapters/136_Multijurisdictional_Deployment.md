# Chapter 136 — Multijurisdictional Deployment

## Purpose

This chapter explains how organizations should govern AI systems deployed across multiple countries, legal entities, sectors, languages, and regulatory regimes.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should identify applicable laws and roles by jurisdiction, establish a defensible global control baseline, implement local overlays, manage cross-border data and vendor dependencies, preserve consistent evidence, and prevent deployment where legal, technical, or operational requirements are not satisfied.

## Plain-language explanation

A system approved in one country is not automatically ready for another. The same AI capability may have different classifications, transparency duties, employment restrictions, privacy requirements, sector rules, language expectations, and regulator contacts. Multijurisdictional deployment requires controlled localization rather than informal reuse.

## Global baseline and local overlays

Establish:

- a global AI governance policy;
- a common inventory and classification method;
- minimum lifecycle, risk, security, privacy, oversight, and evidence controls;
- jurisdiction-specific legal overlays;
- sector and customer overlays;
- documented criteria for stricter local requirements;
- a process for resolving conflicting obligations.

## Jurisdiction assessment

For each deployment, document:

- countries and territories involved;
- legal entities and accountable owners;
- provider, deployer, importer, distributor, employer, controller, processor, and other roles;
- intended purpose and affected populations;
- applicable AI, privacy, employment, consumer, safety, accessibility, discrimination, and sector rules;
- regulator and notification contacts;
- data locations and cross-border transfers;
- local language and cultural requirements;
- vendor and subprocessor locations;
- restrictions, approvals, and launch conditions.

## Deployment decision model

Use controlled outcomes:

- approved globally without additional controls;
- approved subject to local configuration or notice;
- approved only for limited users or purposes;
- pilot approved with enhanced monitoring;
- deferred pending legal or technical evidence;
- prohibited in the jurisdiction;
- withdrawn or suspended after change or incident.

## Data and infrastructure considerations

Assess:

- lawful data collection and use;
- localization or residency requirements;
- international-transfer mechanisms;
- training and reuse restrictions;
- sensitive and special-category data;
- retention and deletion rules;
- regional cloud and model endpoints;
- government-access and sovereignty risk;
- incident, logging, and evidence availability across regions.

## Localization and user protection

Validate:

- translated notices and instructions;
- accessibility requirements;
- local human-oversight competence;
- cultural and linguistic performance;
- local bias and subgroup risk;
- customer-support and complaint channels;
- emergency, incident, and regulator escalation;
- local fallback or service-continuity arrangements.

## Change management

Reassess deployment after:

- expansion to a new country or sector;
- new language or affected population;
- model or provider change;
- new data source or transfer route;
- local law or regulator guidance change;
- material incident or complaint;
- acquisition, restructuring, or new legal entity;
- substantial modification or repurposing.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay plans to deploy its traveler assistant in several EU Member States and Latin American markets. It applies one global security and oversight baseline, then adds country-specific transparency notices, privacy controls, language testing, regulator contacts, and data-transfer assessments. Deployment in one country is delayed because the local-language escalation process and regional log-retention arrangement are incomplete.

## Control activities

- Maintain a jurisdiction and legal-role register.
- Define a global baseline and documented local overlays.
- Require country approval before production launch.
- Validate language, accessibility, bias, oversight, and support locally.
- Control cross-border data and evidence flows.
- Track regulator contacts and notification requirements.
- Reassess after legal, technical, organizational, or geographic change.
- Block deployment when required local controls are incomplete.

## Evidence

- jurisdiction assessments;
- role and entity maps;
- legal-obligation matrix;
- global baseline and local overlays;
- privacy and transfer assessments;
- localized notices and instructions;
- language, accessibility, and subgroup tests;
- local approvals and restrictions;
- vendor and infrastructure records;
- regulator contact lists;
- monitoring, incident, and change records.

## Audit tests

1. Select jurisdictions and verify applicable roles and laws were assessed.
2. Confirm local requirements are mapped to controls and evidence.
3. Review language, accessibility, oversight, and subgroup testing.
4. Verify data-transfer and residency controls.
5. Trace country launch approval to completed local readiness criteria.
6. Confirm legal and provider changes trigger reassessment.
7. Review whether prohibited or restricted deployments are technically enforced.

## Metrics

- jurisdictions with current assessments;
- deployments awaiting local approval;
- local control gaps;
- translation and accessibility defects;
- cross-border transfer exceptions;
- regional evidence gaps;
- legal changes awaiting assessment;
- deployments restricted or suspended;
- multijurisdictional incidents and complaints.

## Management checklist

- Do we know where each AI system operates and which entity is accountable?
- Is the global baseline strong enough for every deployment?
- Are local legal and sector overlays documented?
- Have language, accessibility, bias, and oversight been tested locally?
- Are cross-border data and evidence flows lawful and controlled?
- Can deployment be blocked or suspended by jurisdiction?
