# Legal and Citation Audit — Batch 01

**Scope:** Chapters 1–35 and foundation timeline

**Review status:** Initial authoritative-source review completed; chapter-level corrections still to be applied and verified.

## Authoritative sources used

1. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (Artificial Intelligence Act), official EUR-Lex text.
2. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 amending Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and related legislation.
3. European Commission, AI Act implementation and navigation pages.
4. European Commission report reviewing prohibited practices and high-risk AI.
5. European Commission draft guidance on high-risk AI systems, where relevant and clearly identified as non-binding draft guidance.

## High-priority legal findings

### 1. Foundation implementation timeline requires expansion

The existing foundation correctly records the delayed application dates for Chapter III, Sections 1–3:

- 2 December 2027 for Article 6(2) / Annex III high-risk systems;
- 2 August 2028 for Article 6(1) / Annex I high-risk systems.

The publication timeline must also identify these newer binding milestones introduced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744:

- 27 July 2026: Articles 102–110 apply;
- 2 December 2026: the new Article 5 prohibitions concerning AI systems generating non-consensual sexually explicit or intimate content and child sexual abuse material apply;
- 2 December 2026: providers of AI systems, including GPAI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video, or text content and placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must take the necessary steps to comply with Article 50(2), subject to the amended transitional rule.

**Required action:** Update Chapter 6, the foundation timeline, executive summary, implementation roadmap, and any date graphics so these milestones are explicit and consistent.

### 2. Chapter 34 requires precise effective-date treatment

The new prohibition covering AI systems that generate non-consensual sexually explicit or intimate content or child sexual abuse material is now part of the amended AI Act. It is not merely a proposal or policy recommendation.

However, it does not share the February 2025 application date of the earlier Article 5 prohibitions. The amended provisions apply from 2 December 2026.

**Required action:** Chapter 34 must:

- cite Regulation (EU) 2026/1744;
- distinguish adoption from application;
- state the 2 December 2026 application date;
- avoid describing the prohibition as already applicable before that date;
- separate the two prohibited categories clearly;
- avoid terminology that could imply every harmful intimate-content use is automatically within the same legal element without analysing the statutory wording.

### 3. High-risk timing must be stated narrowly

The delayed dates apply specifically to Chapter III, Sections 1, 2, and 3, subject to the amended Article 113 framework. They should not be presented as if every obligation relating to high-risk systems, every governance provision, or every enforcement provision is delayed to the same date.

**Required action:** Chapters 6, 20–21, 36–52, 118, 133, and related appendices must distinguish:

- classification and scoping provisions;
- requirements in Chapter III, Sections 1–3;
- other obligations and enforcement provisions with separate dates;
- system-specific transitional rules.

### 4. Foundation source register needs a current amendment entry

The source register currently names Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 but not Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.

**Required action:** Add Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 as a binding source and require the consolidated/amended text to control over older summaries.

### 5. Prohibited-practice chapters require element-by-element tests

Chapters 26–34 should not rely only on category labels. Each chapter should map the relevant legal elements, conditions, exceptions, and scope limitations.

**Required chapter controls:**

- identify the exact Article 5 paragraph and point;
- define the prohibited conduct without expanding it beyond the text;
- identify any purpose, context, intent, or harm threshold;
- identify exceptions and safeguards;
- distinguish provider and deployer conduct where relevant;
- provide a documented legal escalation trigger for ambiguous cases;
- retain evidence of suspension, rejection, or decommissioning decisions.

### 6. Remote biometric identification must preserve the statutory distinctions

Chapter 33 must distinguish:

- real-time versus post remote biometric identification;
- law-enforcement use versus other contexts;
- publicly accessible spaces;
- the narrow permitted purposes and authorization/safeguard conditions;
- separate classification and transparency implications where the conduct is not prohibited.

A simplified statement that remote biometric identification is categorically prohibited would be inaccurate.

### 7. Emotion recognition and biometric categorisation require scope precision

Chapters 31 and 32 must distinguish prohibited uses from regulated-but-not-prohibited uses.

The manual should avoid treating all emotion-recognition systems or all biometric categorisation systems as prohibited. The legal assessment depends on context, inferred characteristics, workplace/education setting, medical or safety exceptions, and the precise statutory element.

### 8. Scope and role analysis must use the amended text

Chapters 2–5 and 18 should use the current amended wording for:

- scope;
- provider, deployer, importer, distributor, authorised representative, and product-manufacturer roles;
- obligations for actors established outside the Union;
- product-sector interactions and Annex I changes introduced by the 2026 amendment.

**Required action:** Recheck all quoted or paraphrased definitions against the current EUR-Lex text before final publication.

## Citation standard for Chapters 1–35

Each material legal proposition should cite one of the following:

- the relevant Article and paragraph of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as amended;
- the relevant annex;
- Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 where the amendment itself is material;
- official Commission guidance only as interpretation, implementation support, or example, never as a substitute for the binding text.

Draft or consultation guidance must be labelled as draft and non-binding.

1. every critical and high-priority correction above is applied;
