# Manual 05 — AI Auditing and Assurance
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 17–24

> Original audit implementation guidance. This material supports controlled assurance work and does not itself constitute an audit opinion.

## Chapter 17 — Finding development

A finding should connect criterion, condition, cause, consequence, and evidence. Vague statements such as “governance should improve” are not sufficient. The finding should identify what requirement or approved expectation was not met, what was observed, why it matters, and what evidence supports the conclusion.

## Chapter 18 — Severity and prioritization

Severity should reflect consequence and likelihood in context. Factors may include safety, security, privacy, regulatory, financial, operational, customer, workforce, and reputational effects.

The audit team should use a consistent severity method and document when professional judgment overrides a mechanical score.

## Chapter 19 — Root cause analysis

Root cause analysis should distinguish immediate failure from systemic cause. Examples include unclear ownership, ineffective approval design, inadequate testing, weak supplier governance, poor change control, missing competencies, or incentives that bypass controls.

Remediation aimed only at the visible symptom should not be represented as root-cause correction.

## Chapter 20 — Management response

Management responses should state agreement or disagreement, planned action, owner, target date, and any accepted residual risk. Disagreement should be supported by evidence or rationale rather than simply removing the finding from the report.

## Chapter 21 — Residual risk and acceptance

When remediation does not fully eliminate risk, the remaining exposure should be documented and accepted by an authority appropriate to its significance. The auditor should not silently convert an unresolved control gap into an acceptable state.

## Chapter 22 — Reporting limitations

Reports should state scope, period, criteria, procedures, material limitations, unresolved evidence gaps, and the intended meaning of the conclusion. Readers should be able to distinguish an internal readiness review, advisory assessment, audit, certification activity, or other assurance engagement.

## Chapter 23 — Remediation validation

Closure requires evidence that corrective action was implemented and, where appropriate, that it operates effectively. Validation may include document review, configuration inspection, retesting, sample testing, interviews, or observation.

Management assertion alone should not close a material finding when objective evidence is reasonably available.

## Chapter 24 — Follow-up and recurrence

Follow-up should consider whether findings recur, whether similar systems have the same weakness, and whether remediation introduced new risk. Repeated findings may indicate a governance or control-system problem rather than isolated execution failure.
