# Chapter 133 — High-Risk Readiness Roadmap

## Purpose

This chapter provides a practical roadmap for organizations preparing AI systems that may qualify as high-risk under the EU AI Act.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should identify potentially high-risk systems early, confirm legal classification, assign accountable owners, implement the required lifecycle controls, maintain complete technical and governance evidence, and verify readiness before placing a system on the market or putting it into service.

## Plain-language explanation

High-risk readiness is not a single approval. It is a coordinated programme covering classification, risk management, data governance, technical documentation, records, transparency, human oversight, accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity, quality management, conformity activities, registration, deployment controls, monitoring, incidents, and change.

## Phase 1: identify and classify

- validate the AI inventory;
- determine intended purpose and actual use;
- map provider, deployer, importer, distributor, and other roles;
- assess Annex I and Annex III relevance;
- review exclusions and special conditions;
- identify sector-specific legal overlays;
- document legal rationale, assumptions, and review triggers.

## Phase 2: establish governance

- appoint business, technical, legal, risk, data, security, and compliance owners;
- define decision and escalation authority;
- establish quality-management responsibilities;
- approve the risk-management plan;
- identify competent human-oversight personnel;
- define independent validation and assurance roles.

## Phase 3: implement core controls

Implement and evidence:

- continuous risk management;
- data and data-governance controls;
- technical documentation;
- automatic record keeping and logs;
- instructions for use and transparency information;
- human-oversight design and procedures;
- accuracy, robustness, resilience, and cybersecurity controls;
- change, configuration, release, and incident management;
- supplier and component governance.

## Phase 4: validate readiness

Perform:

- design-effectiveness review;
- operating-effectiveness testing;
- technical validation;
- bias and subgroup testing;
- oversight and escalation testing;
- cybersecurity and resilience testing;
- documentation-to-production reconciliation;
- evidence completeness and retrievability review;
- conformity-readiness assessment.

## Phase 5: authorize deployment

Before release:

- confirm applicable conformity route;
- complete required declarations and markings where applicable;
- complete registration where required;
- approve instructions for use;
- verify deployer obligations and handoff;
- resolve or formally govern residual findings;
- approve monitoring, incident, and change plans;
- record release authority and conditions.

## Phase 6: operate and monitor

After deployment:

- monitor performance, drift, incidents, complaints, and overrides;
- validate continued effectiveness of human oversight;
- maintain logs and evidence;
- reassess after material change;
- investigate serious incidents;
- implement corrective action;
- suspend, restrict, recall, or withdraw when necessary;
- support regulatory requests and inspections.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay treats its recruitment-screening system as potentially high-risk. It confirms the system’s intended purpose, maps provider and deployer roles, documents the classification rationale, establishes a risk-management file, performs subgroup and oversight testing, reconciles the deployed version to technical documentation, and blocks release until a failed human-escalation test is corrected.

## Control activities

- Maintain a high-risk readiness register.
- Assign accountable owners and deadlines.
- Map every required control to evidence.
- Require technical and legal validation before deployment.
- Track unresolved findings and conditions of approval.
- Reassess after changes, incidents, or new legal guidance.
- Maintain post-market and regulatory-response readiness.

## Evidence

- classification assessment;
- role assessment;
- high-risk readiness plan;
- quality-management records;
- risk-management file;
- data-governance evidence;
- technical documentation;
- validation and test results;
- conformity-readiness review;
- registration and declaration records where applicable;
- release approval;
- monitoring, incident, and corrective-action records.

## Audit tests

1. Verify classification is current and legally supported.
2. Trace each applicable high-risk obligation to a control and evidence owner.
3. Confirm technical documentation matches the deployed system.
4. Review validation coverage for accuracy, fairness, oversight, robustness, and cybersecurity.
5. Confirm unresolved findings were corrected or formally governed.
6. Verify post-market monitoring and incident processes operate.
7. Confirm material changes trigger reassessment.

## Metrics

- potentially high-risk systems identified;
- confirmed high-risk systems;
- readiness controls complete;
- evidence gaps;
- failed validation criteria;
- deployment approvals with conditions;
- overdue remediation;
- material changes awaiting reassessment;
- monitoring and incident actions overdue.

## Management checklist

- Have we identified every potentially high-risk system?
- Is the classification legally defensible?
- Are all mandatory lifecycle controls implemented and evidenced?
- Does the deployed system match the approved documentation?
- Can failed criteria block deployment?
- Are monitoring, incidents, and changes governed after release?
