# Manual 13 — SOX ITGC / ICFR Controlled Implementation

## Chapter 17 — Control design effectiveness
Assess whether each control, if performed as designed by a competent owner at the stated frequency, would prevent or detect the identified financial-reporting risk in time. Document the control objective, trigger, population, performer, precision, evidence, dependencies, and escalation logic. A control can be well operated yet poorly designed if it does not address the risk with sufficient precision.

## Chapter 18 — Operating effectiveness
Evaluate whether the control actually operated throughout the relevant period, by appropriate personnel, with consistent evidence and timely follow-up. Define the population and testing period before selecting items. Exceptions should be investigated for cause, duration, affected population, compensating activity, and possible impact on related controls.

## Chapter 19 — Evidence design and retention
For every control, specify expected evidence, source system, owner, retention location, period covered, linkage to the control population, and reviewer attributes. Favor evidence generated as a natural result of the control rather than screenshots created only for audit. Preserve immutable or traceable records where practical and document any manual evidence assembly steps.

## Chapter 20 — Population completeness and sampling readiness
Testing depends on a reliable population. Document how populations are generated, why they are complete, which filters were applied, and how duplicates, exclusions, or missing records are handled. Sampling approaches should be selected by competent reviewers based on the control, frequency, risk, population, and applicable audit methodology. This manual does not prescribe a universal sample size.

## Chapter 21 — Service organizations and third parties
Map outsourced systems and services that affect ICFR. Identify complementary user-entity controls, subservice organizations, provider control reports, contract obligations, incidents, changes, and gaps. A SOC report or other assurance report is an input to management evaluation, not automatic proof that all relevant risks are controlled.

## Chapter 22 — Cloud and SaaS considerations
Document the shared-responsibility model for financially relevant cloud services. Address tenant configuration, privileged access, identity federation, change processes, logging, backup/recovery, interfaces, provider changes, and evidence availability. Track configuration drift and significant service changes as reassessment triggers.

## Chapter 23 — AI and automation in financially relevant processes
Where AI, machine learning, robotic process automation, or agentic automation influences financially relevant calculations, classifications, reconciliations, approvals, reports, or evidence, identify model/tool purpose, inputs, outputs, human oversight, change controls, access, validation, monitoring, fallback, and evidence. Do not assume AI-generated evidence is reliable without validation of provenance and completeness.

## Chapter 24 — Deficiency identification and escalation
Record control failures, design gaps, evidence failures, recurring exceptions, and dependency failures in a structured deficiency process. Separate factual control findings from the legal/audit classification of a deficiency, significant deficiency, or material weakness. Escalate classification decisions to competent management and audit professionals using current SEC/PCAOB criteria and organization-specific facts.
