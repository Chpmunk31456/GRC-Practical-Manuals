# Governance, Source Authority, and Obligation Inventory

## 1. Purpose

A compliance obligations register is a controlled inventory of requirements that an organization has determined may govern its activities. It supports accountability, implementation planning, monitoring, evidence retention, and change management. It does not replace the authoritative source or a qualified applicability decision.

## 2. Define obligation categories

Use controlled categories so unlike requirements are not treated as interchangeable:

- law or statute;
- regulation or binding rule;
- regulatory order, permit, license, or authorization;
- contract or customer commitment;
- court, tribunal, or administrative decision;
- mandatory industry or payment-program requirement;
- adopted standard or certification criterion;
- internal policy or governance commitment;
- voluntary code, framework, or guidance.

Record whether each item is mandatory, voluntarily adopted, contractually binding, or informational.

## 3. Establish source authority

Each source record should identify:

- official title and identifier;
- issuing authority;
- jurisdiction and sector;
- authoritative publication location;
- source version or consolidation date;
- publication, adoption, effective, transition, and enforcement dates;
- current status, including draft, proposed, adopted, effective, amended, stayed, delayed, superseded, withdrawn, or expired;
- language and translation status;
- access, copyright, and licensing conditions.

Secondary summaries may support discovery but should not become the authoritative requirement record.

## 4. Separate dates

Do not collapse all dates into one field. Publication, adoption, effective, transition-end, enforcement, implementation, and review dates can differ materially. The register must preserve those distinctions because an adopted requirement may not yet be effective, and an effective requirement may have phased obligations or delayed enforcement.

## 5. Assign governance roles

Recommended roles include:

- **compliance governance owner** — approves methodology and reporting;
- **source steward** — verifies official sources, versions, and status;
- **legal or subject-matter reviewer** — evaluates meaning and applicability;
- **obligation owner** — remains accountable for the requirement;
- **control owner** — maintains implementation and evidence;
- **regulatory change coordinator** — manages horizon scanning and change intake;
- **records custodian** — retains decisions, versions, approvals, and evidence;
- **independent reviewer** — challenges unsupported conclusions and closure claims.

## 6. Set inventory boundaries

Document which entities, jurisdictions, products, services, systems, data types, facilities, workforce populations, suppliers, licenses, contracts, and business processes are included. An organization-wide inventory may contain local obligations, but local requirements must retain their scope and should not be generalized automatically.

## 7. Create obligation identifiers

Assign a stable organization identifier to each obligation while preserving the official source reference. Use parent-child relationships when a source provision contains multiple independently actionable duties. Do not invent identifiers that could be mistaken for official citations.

## 8. Minimum entry criteria

An obligation should not enter the controlled register without:

- an identifiable source and current status;
- a traceable official reference or documented reason why one is unavailable;
- a preliminary scope and applicability status;
- an accountable owner;
- a review date;
- an explicit statement of uncertainty when interpretation is incomplete.

## 9. Prohibited claims

Do not state that inclusion in the register proves applicability, that assignment to a control proves implementation, or that evidence presence proves compliance. Each conclusion requires its own review and supporting facts.

## 10. Governance evidence

Retain the methodology, source inventory, role assignments, approval records, review calendar, change history, superseded entries, unresolved questions, exceptions, and escalation records.
