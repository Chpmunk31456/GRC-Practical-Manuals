# Chapter 109 — Deficiency Classification

## Purpose

This chapter establishes a consistent method for classifying AI control deficiencies, determining severity, assigning ownership, escalating material issues, and tracking remediation.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should classify AI control deficiencies using documented criteria that consider legal obligations, affected people, safety, fundamental rights, privacy, cybersecurity, business impact, duration, detectability, recurrence, and control dependency. Classification should drive escalation, remediation priority, reporting, and deployment decisions.

## Plain-language explanation

A deficiency is more than a failed control test. It is a weakness that may allow an AI-related obligation, risk, or business objective to remain unmet. A minor documentation gap and a failure that could expose people to unlawful discrimination should not be managed in the same way.

## Deficiency categories

Common categories include:

- design deficiency;
- operating-effectiveness failure;
- missing control;
- incomplete or unreliable evidence;
- unauthorized exception;
- overdue remediation;
- repeated or systemic failure;
- monitoring or detection gap;
- third-party control dependency failure;
- legal or regulatory nonconformity;
- inaccurate management reporting.

## Severity factors

Evaluate:

- whether a binding obligation is affected;
- whether the issue involves a prohibited practice;
- actual or plausible harm to people;
- safety and fundamental-rights impact;
- data sensitivity and scale;
- number of systems, users, or decisions affected;
- likelihood and detectability;
- duration and recurrence;
- ability to contain or reverse impact;
- dependency on the failed control;
- availability of compensating controls;
- regulatory, financial, operational, and reputational exposure.

## Suggested severity levels

### Critical

A critical deficiency may involve unlawful deployment, prohibited conduct, severe safety or rights impact, inability to stop a harmful system, material evidence destruction, or a control failure requiring immediate suspension.

### High

A high deficiency creates substantial exposure, affects a key control, lacks effective compensation, or could materially impair compliance, safety, privacy, fairness, or resilience.

### Moderate

A moderate deficiency has limited scope or impact but requires timely correction to prevent escalation or recurrence.

### Low

A low deficiency has minimal immediate impact and does not undermine a key obligation or control objective, but still requires documented correction.

## Escalation and action

Define for each severity:

- notification recipients;
- target remediation date;
- interim controls;
- deployment restriction or suspension rules;
- retesting requirements;
- executive or board reporting;
- regulator or customer notification assessment;
- risk-acceptance authority.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay identifies that its recruitment-screening tool has been operating for two months without the approved human-oversight review because a workflow integration failed. The issue affects employment decisions and a key control. GlobalWay classifies it as high, suspends automated recommendations, preserves affected records, conducts retrospective review, implements manual approval, corrects the workflow, and requires independent retesting before restart.

## Control activities

- Maintain approved deficiency-classification criteria.
- Require multidisciplinary severity assessment for material issues.
- Link severity to escalation, deadlines, and deployment decisions.
- Document compensating controls and residual risk.
- Reassess severity when facts change.
- Track repeat and systemic failures.
- Require validated closure evidence.

## Evidence

- deficiency register;
- severity assessments;
- legal, privacy, security, and rights analyses;
- escalation records;
- interim-control evidence;
- remediation plans;
- retest results;
- closure approvals;
- management and board reports.

## Audit tests

1. Sample deficiencies and verify severity criteria were applied consistently.
2. Confirm critical and high issues triggered required escalation and deployment action.
3. Review whether affected people, legal obligations, and control dependencies were considered.
4. Trace overdue items to escalation and risk decisions.
5. Verify closure was supported by retesting and evidence.
6. Identify repeated issues and assess whether root causes were addressed.

## Metrics

- deficiencies by severity and domain;
- overdue critical and high issues;
- average remediation time;
- repeat deficiencies;
- deficiencies involving key controls;
- deployments suspended or restricted;
- deficiencies closed without retest;
- aging by severity.

## Management checklist

- Are severity criteria clear and consistently applied?
- Can a critical issue stop deployment or operation?
- Are legal and affected-person impacts considered?
- Are compensating controls real and tested?
- Are repeat failures treated as systemic problems?
