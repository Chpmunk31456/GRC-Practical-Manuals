# Chapter 112 — Audit Planning

## Purpose

This chapter explains how to plan a risk-based audit of AI governance, EU AI Act readiness, control design, operating effectiveness, technical safeguards, evidence, and management oversight.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

AI audits should be planned using documented objectives, scope, criteria, risk assessment, competence requirements, sampling strategy, evidence standards, timelines, and reporting expectations. Planning should reflect the organization’s AI roles, jurisdictions, system classifications, lifecycle stages, incidents, changes, and reliance on third parties.

## Plain-language explanation

A useful AI audit does not begin with a generic checklist. It begins by determining which systems matter, which obligations apply, what could cause harm or noncompliance, which controls are critical, and what evidence can demonstrate that those controls actually work.

## Audit universe

Maintain an audit universe that includes:

- AI systems and use cases;
- business services and legal entities;
- provider, deployer, importer, distributor, authorized-representative, and manufacturer roles;
- prohibited-practice screening;
- high-risk systems;
- GPAI and systemic-risk responsibilities;
- transparency obligations;
- data, models, infrastructure, and vendors;
- governance processes and control domains;
- jurisdictions and sectors;
- incidents, complaints, exceptions, and findings.

## Risk-based prioritization

Prioritize based on:

- legal classification and applicability;
- potential safety or fundamental-rights harm;
- scale and sensitivity of decisions;
- novelty and complexity;
- autonomy and human-oversight dependence;
- data sensitivity;
- cyber and resilience exposure;
- third-party concentration;
- recent material change;
- incidents, complaints, or poor control history;
- regulatory deadlines and examinations;
- time since prior assurance.

## Audit objectives and criteria

Define whether the audit will assess:

- governance and accountability;
- legal applicability and classification;
- control design;
- operating effectiveness;
- technical performance and validation;
- documentation and evidence;
- conformity readiness;
- post-market monitoring;
- incident and corrective-action processes;
- vendor and supply-chain controls;
- implementation-roadmap progress.

Criteria may include binding law, official guidance, internal policy, contracts, approved standards, control-library requirements, and documented risk decisions. Clearly distinguish mandatory criteria from recommended practices.

## Scope and boundaries

Document:

- systems, models, versions, and environments;
- entities and jurisdictions;
- business processes;
- time period;
- lifecycle stages;
- vendors and fourth parties;
- exclusions and rationale;
- known limitations;
- reliance on other assurance work.

## Team competence

The audit team may require legal, privacy, security, data-science, model-risk, accessibility, human-factors, sector, and internal-audit expertise. Identify conflicts of interest and use specialists where general audit competence is insufficient.

## Evidence and sampling plan

Determine:

- populations and data sources;
- sample method and size;
- key controls requiring full-population testing;
- system-generated evidence validation;
- interviews and walkthroughs;
- technical test requirements;
- third-party evidence reliance;
- evidence retention and chain of custody;
- handling of confidential and personal data.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay’s annual audit plan prioritizes its recruitment-screening system, disruption assistant, and fraud-detection platform. The recruitment tool is selected because it may be high-risk and affects employment decisions. The disruption assistant is selected because of recent model and vendor changes. The audit plan combines legal classification review, control testing, bias and oversight testing, vendor evidence, and technical validation.

## Control activities

- Maintain a complete AI audit universe.
- Perform documented risk assessment.
- Define objectives, criteria, scope, and exclusions.
- Assign competent and independent auditors.
- Design evidence and sampling methods before fieldwork.
- Coordinate with legal, privacy, security, model-risk, and business owners.
- Address access, confidentiality, and technical-environment needs.
- Approve and communicate the audit plan.

## Evidence

- audit universe;
- risk assessment;
- annual and engagement plans;
- scope memorandum;
- criteria register;
- competence and independence assessment;
- sampling plan;
- evidence request list;
- specialist work plans;
- reliance assessments;
- planning approvals.

## Audit tests

1. Verify the audit universe includes material AI systems, roles, obligations, and vendors.
2. Trace engagement selection to documented risk factors.
3. Confirm objectives and criteria distinguish legal duties from guidance.
4. Review scope exclusions and limitations.
5. Assess team competence and independence.
6. Evaluate whether sampling and technical testing are appropriate to risk.
7. Confirm access to required systems, evidence, and personnel was planned.

## Metrics

- audit-universe coverage;
- high-risk systems audited;
- overdue assurance reviews;
- engagements requiring specialists;
- scope limitations;
- audits delayed by evidence or access problems;
- repeat high-severity findings;
- planned versus completed audits.

## Management checklist

- Does the audit universe include every material AI use case?
- Are the highest-risk systems prioritized?
- Are legal and technical criteria explicit?
- Does the team have the necessary competence and independence?
- Is the evidence and sampling plan sufficient?
- Are scope limitations transparent?
