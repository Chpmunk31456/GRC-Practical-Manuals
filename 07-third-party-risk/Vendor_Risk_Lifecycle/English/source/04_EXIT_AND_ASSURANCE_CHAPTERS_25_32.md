# Manual 08 — Vendor and Third-Party Risk Lifecycle
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 25–32

> Original third-party-risk implementation guidance. This material supports controlled TPRM and does not eliminate supplier risk.

## Chapter 25 — Performance and service-risk review

Operational performance should be evaluated alongside control evidence. Persistent service failures, missed commitments, support degradation, or repeated incidents can change residual risk even when formal assurance documents remain current.

## Chapter 26 — Financial and strategic dependency

Where material to service continuity, the organization should consider financial viability, ownership changes, acquisition risk, strategic dependency, and the feasibility of replacing the supplier.

These considerations should be handled proportionately and with appropriate specialist input.

## Chapter 27 — Data return and deletion

Exit planning should define what data, configurations, logs, artifacts, models, prompts, or other information must be returned, transferred, retained, or deleted. Completion should be evidenced where contractual or regulatory requirements apply.

## Chapter 28 — Access termination

Offboarding should revoke accounts, credentials, API keys, network access, privileged access, support access, integrations, certificates, and other supplier connectivity that is no longer required.

## Chapter 29 — Transition and continuity

Critical suppliers should have a realistic transition plan that considers replacement services, data migration, technical dependencies, knowledge transfer, timing, and interim controls.

An exit plan that cannot be executed within business tolerances should be treated as a current risk.

## Chapter 30 — Residual obligations

After service termination, some obligations may survive, including confidentiality, record retention, data deletion, incident cooperation, audit support, intellectual-property terms, or legal holds. These should be tracked until complete.

## Chapter 31 — TPRM assurance limitations

A controlled TPRM process reduces uncertainty but cannot certify a supplier as risk-free. Conclusions should identify the evidence, scope, time period, assumptions, and limitations on which they depend.
