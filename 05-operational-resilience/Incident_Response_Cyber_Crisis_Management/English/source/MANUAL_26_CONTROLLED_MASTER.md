# Manual 26 — Incident Response & Cyber Crisis Management Controlled Implementation

Source baseline: NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 (final, April 2025) supersedes Rev. 2 and aligns incident response with CSF 2.0. CISA and jurisdiction-specific notification guidance are supporting/overlay sources and must not be treated as universally binding. Recheck current source state before release.

## 01. Purpose, scope, and assurance boundary
Define a practical incident-response and cyber-crisis operating model without implying universal legal sufficiency, breach reportability, or audit opinion.

## 02. Source hierarchy and version control
Separate NIST guidance, CISA guidance, regulatory notification rules, contractual obligations, sector overlays, insurer requirements, and internal policy.

## 03. Governance and executive accountability
Assign executive sponsor, incident commander, crisis leader, legal/privacy roles, communications authority, business owners, and evidence accountability.

## 04. Incident response policy
Define policy scope, authority, severity thresholds, escalation, evidence, communications, recovery, lessons learned, and governance requirements.

## 05. Organizational preparation
Maintain readiness across people, process, tooling, logging, contact data, access, backups, forensic capability, vendors, and decision support.

## 06. Asset, service, and dependency context
Link incident response to critical services, assets, identities, applications, cloud services, suppliers, facilities, and business processes.

## 07. Threat and scenario planning
Maintain scenario libraries for ransomware, data theft, account compromise, cloud abuse, supply-chain events, destructive malware, insider misuse, and service disruption.

## 08. Detection and event intake
Define monitored sources, alert triage, user/vendor reporting, escalation channels, minimum intake data, and evidence preservation.

## 09. Triage and initial analysis
Assess credibility, scope, affected services, indicators, likely impact, uncertainty, and immediate containment needs.

## 10. Incident declaration and severity
Use documented declaration criteria and severity levels tied to impact, spread, legal/regulatory triggers, customer harm, safety, and executive attention.

## 11. Incident command structure
Define operational command, specialist workstreams, decision authority, handoffs, battle rhythm, and command-log requirements.

## 12. Crisis-management integration
Escalate from incident handling to enterprise crisis governance when business, legal, safety, reputational, geopolitical, or executive thresholds are met.

## 13. Investigation and evidence preservation
Preserve logs, images, artifacts, timelines, chain-of-custody records, analyst notes, and decision evidence proportionate to legal and operational needs.

## 14. Containment strategy
Select short- and long-term containment actions using impact, attacker persistence, business criticality, safety, customer harm, and recovery constraints.

## 15. Eradication and root-cause analysis
Remove malicious presence, correct exploited weaknesses, invalidate compromised credentials, remediate persistence, and document causal factors.

## 16. Recovery planning
Define restoration order, clean-state criteria, security validation, business-owner acceptance, monitoring, rollback, and residual-risk decisions.

## 17. Identity and access recovery
Address credential reset, token revocation, privileged access, break-glass controls, federation, service accounts, and identity-provider compromise.

## 18. Ransomware and extortion response
Define decision governance for encryption, data theft, extortion, law enforcement, insurer, legal, communications, restoration, and sanctions-related review without prescribing payment decisions.

## 19. Data breach and privacy incident coordination
Coordinate privacy, legal, security, records, customer, regulator, and jurisdiction-specific assessment while preserving distinct reportability tests.

## 20. Regulatory and contractual notification
Maintain a jurisdiction- and obligation-aware notification matrix with trigger, timing, authority, owner, content, evidence, and change-control fields.

## 21. Third-party and supply-chain incidents
Define supplier notification, evidence, containment coordination, alternate service, contractual escalation, assurance, and concentration-risk response.

## 22. Cloud and SaaS incidents
Address shared responsibility, provider logs, tenant isolation, identity, API abuse, region impacts, evidence access, provider escalation, and recovery dependencies.

## 23. OT/ICS and safety-sensitive incidents
Preserve safety, reliability, engineering authority, process constraints, evidence, vendor coordination, and sector-specific escalation over purely IT-centric actions.

## 24. Communications and stakeholder management
Define internal communications, customer notices, media response, spokesperson authority, message approval, rumor control, and executive briefings.

## 25. Decision logging and crisis records
Maintain time-stamped decisions, assumptions, evidence considered, approvers, alternatives, residual risks, and follow-up actions.

## 26. Legal privilege and investigation boundaries
Coordinate counsel-led decisions where appropriate without assuming privilege automatically applies; separate operational facts from legal conclusions.

## 27. Business continuity and disaster recovery integration
Link incident response to BCMS activation, disaster recovery, alternate operations, supplier continuity, and crisis-management thresholds.

## 28. Exercises and simulations
Run tabletop, technical, executive, supplier, ransomware, cloud, communications, and integrated crisis exercises with measurable objectives.

## 29. Metrics and performance management
Track detection, declaration, containment, eradication, recovery, recurrence, exercise findings, notification readiness, issue aging, and evidence completeness.

## 30. Post-incident review and remediation
Perform lessons learned, root-cause validation, control-gap analysis, action tracking, residual-risk acceptance, and closure verification.

## 31. Assurance and management review
Provide independent review and leadership oversight over readiness, incidents, exercises, metrics, findings, resources, and improvement priorities.

## 32. Release, evidence, and implementation roadmap
Package Essential / Structured / Enhanced implementation paths with source verification, localization, accessibility, provenance, checksums, workflow-security, exact candidate identity, and sequential release controls.
