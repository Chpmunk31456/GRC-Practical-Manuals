## Current authoritative state cross-check

Official HHS/OCR material was rechecked on 2026-08-26:

- HHS's Security Rule summary states that it addresses the Security Rule **currently in effect** and separately links to the proposed cybersecurity modifications.
- HHS continues to identify the HIPAA Security Rule at 45 CFR Part 160 and Part 164, Subparts A and C.
- HHS's current Business Associate guidance confirms BAA and subcontractor obligations and direct applicability of specified HIPAA requirements to business associates.
- HHS's Breach Notification guidance continues to require covered entities and business associates, as applicable, to follow the current notification framework for breaches of unsecured PHI.
- HHS states that the June 18, 2025 federal court order vacated most of the 2024 reproductive-health Privacy Rule. The surviving NPP modifications remained in effect, with a February 16, 2026 compliance date; revised HHS model notices were published in February 2026.

The source-verification record has been hardened to preserve that partial-vacatur/NPP distinction.

## Technical/editorial checks completed

- Entity-role and applicability decisions are explicitly treated as organization-specific determinations requiring evidence and qualified human review.
- PHI and ePHI are differentiated, and Security Rule implementation is scoped to ePHI rather than all PHI.
- The minimum-necessary principle is qualified with “where applicable” rather than represented as universally applicable.
- Risk analysis is presented as an evidence-based assessment rather than a checklist or vulnerability scan.
- Administrative, physical, and technical safeguards remain distinct but coordinated.
- Technical control descriptions are framed as risk- and environment-dependent, avoiding unsupported claims that a particular technology is universally mandated by the current Security Rule.
- Breach assessment is routed to qualified human/legal/compliance review; repository automation is expressly prohibited from making a reportability determination.
- Business-associate agreements are treated as required evidence where applicable but not as proof of operating effectiveness.
- Documentation/evidence retention is tied to applicable requirements rather than overgeneralized across every artifact.
- Current-law and proposed-rule material are separated throughout the master.
- Audit/readiness/repository QA boundaries are preserved and do not imply certification or formal HIPAA compliance.
- Material changes reopen affected review gates.
