# Chapter 105 — Article-to-Control Mapping

## Purpose

This chapter defines how to map EU AI Act requirements and related obligations to practical controls, owners, evidence, tests, and remediation.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should maintain traceable mappings from applicable legal and regulatory requirements to implemented controls. The mapping should show why each control exists, which systems and roles it applies to, what evidence demonstrates operation, and how gaps are managed.

## Plain-language explanation

A legal requirement may be broad, while a control must be specific and testable. Article-to-control mapping connects the two. It prevents obligations from being overlooked, shows where several requirements rely on the same control, and enables efficient audit and regulatory response.

## Mapping structure

For each requirement, record:

- source and citation;
- requirement summary;
- applicability conditions;
- regulated role;
- systems and processes in scope;
- control identifiers;
- control owners and performers;
- frequency or trigger;
- required evidence;
- test procedure;
- implementation status;
- gaps, exceptions, and remediation;
- last review and next review date.

## Mapping principles

Mappings should be:

- based on current authoritative sources;
- specific enough to support testing;
- separated by role and obligation where needed;
- linked to one or more controls without unnecessary duplication;
- version controlled;
- reviewed after regulatory, system, role, or process change;
- explicit about partial coverage and residual gaps.

## Many-to-many relationships

One requirement may need several controls, and one control may support several requirements. Maintain both views:

- requirement-to-control coverage;
- control-to-requirement dependency.

Do not infer full compliance merely because one control is mapped to several obligations. Confirm that scope, evidence, frequency, and effectiveness satisfy each obligation.

## Coverage status

Use clear statuses such as:

- not applicable;
- applicable and implemented;
- partially implemented;
- planned;
- exception approved;
- ineffective;
- remediation overdue;
- retired or superseded.

Each non-implemented or partial status should have an accountable owner, target date, and risk decision.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay maps its high-risk recruitment-screening system to requirements for risk management, data governance, technical documentation, logging, transparency, human oversight, accuracy, cybersecurity, conformity readiness, and post-market monitoring. The deployment-approval control supports several obligations, but separate mappings identify the exact evidence required for each article.

## Control activities

- Maintain an authoritative requirement register.
- Define a standard mapping methodology.
- Map each applicable requirement to controls and evidence.
- Identify unmapped or partially covered obligations.
- Review mappings after change or incident.
- Preserve version history and approvals.
- Report coverage gaps to accountable management.

## Evidence

- requirement register;
- article-to-control matrix;
- applicability assessments;
- control-library references;
- evidence indexes;
- gap and remediation records;
- review approvals;
- version history;
- regulatory-change assessments.

## Audit tests

1. Sample applicable requirements and trace them to controls, evidence, and tests.
2. Confirm mappings reflect the organization’s actual regulated role.
3. Review partial or missing coverage and related remediation.
4. Test whether shared controls satisfy each mapped obligation.
5. Verify regulatory and system changes trigger mapping review.
6. Confirm retired controls were replaced where obligations remain.

## Metrics

- applicable requirements mapped;
- unmapped requirements;
- partial-coverage items;
- overdue remediation;
- mappings not reviewed after change;
- controls supporting multiple critical obligations;
- requirements dependent on ineffective controls.

## Management checklist

- Can every applicable obligation be traced to a control?
- Can each control be traced back to its requirements?
- Are gaps and partial coverage visible?
- Are role, scope, evidence, and testing explicit?
- Is the mapping current and version controlled?
