# Regulatory Horizon Scanning and Change Intake

## 1. Define the monitoring universe

Horizon scanning should cover the jurisdictions, regulators, legislatures, standards bodies, courts, contractual channels, licensing authorities, industry programs, and official gazettes relevant to the organization. Document which sources are monitored, by whom, how often, and what constitutes a reportable change.

## 2. Prioritize authoritative channels

Use official publications, regulator notices, legislative records, court or administrative sources, contractual notices, and standards-body records as authoritative inputs. Newsletters, law-firm alerts, trade associations, vendors, and media may support discovery but require verification before a controlled change record is created.

## 3. Capture change status

Distinguish:

- consultation or request for comment;
- proposal or draft;
- adopted or enacted change;
- published final instrument;
- effective requirement;
- phased or transitional requirement;
- enforcement guidance or priority;
- stayed, delayed, suspended, amended, withdrawn, superseded, or expired requirement.

A proposal should not be recorded as an effective obligation.

## 4. Preserve critical dates

Record discovery, publication, adoption, effective, transition-end, enforcement, implementation, and review dates separately. Where dates vary by provision, entity type, threshold, geography, or product, create linked records or explicit phased milestones.

## 5. Perform initial triage

Initial triage should identify:

- potentially affected entities, products, services, processes, systems, data, people, suppliers, and contracts;
- likely applicability;
- materiality and urgency;
- whether qualified review is required;
- known deadlines and uncertainty;
- immediate preservation, communication, or risk actions;
- accountable reviewer and owner.

## 6. Rate materiality and urgency separately

Materiality reflects potential significance to legal exposure, customers, operations, finances, safety, privacy, security, licensing, reputation, or strategy. Urgency reflects the time available to decide and act. A high-materiality change may have a long transition period; a lower-materiality notice may require immediate response.

## 7. Avoid duplicate and fragmented intake

Link related publications, corrections, guidance, FAQs, implementing acts, technical standards, enforcement notices, and judicial decisions to a common change record when appropriate. Preserve each authoritative source while maintaining a coherent change history.

## 8. Establish escalation rules

Escalate when a change may threaten a license, create criminal or civil exposure, require customer or regulator notification, affect fundamental rights, materially alter products or contracts, require major technology changes, conflict across jurisdictions, or cannot be implemented before the required date.

## 9. Record negative decisions

When a change is determined not applicable or not material, retain the facts, rationale, reviewer, approval, date, and reassessment trigger. A closed negative decision should remain traceable.

## 10. Intake evidence

Retain the official publication, discovery record, source verification, initial assessment, reviewer assignment, decision, communications, and links to affected obligations, risks, controls, projects, and exceptions.
