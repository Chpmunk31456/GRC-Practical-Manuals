# Manual 11 — GDPR Privacy and Data Protection Controlled Implementation
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 17–24

> Original implementation and training guidance for DPIA, security, breach response, processors, and transfers. Legal sufficiency requires context-specific competent review.

## Chapter 17 — Records of processing and data inventory

Maintain records that connect processing purpose, data categories, data subjects, systems, recipients, processors, transfers, retention, security measures, owners, lawful basis, and related DPIAs or notices where applicable.

The ROPA should operate as a living governance record rather than an annual spreadsheet disconnected from architecture and business change.

## Chapter 18 — DPIA screening and initiation

Screen new and changed processing for factors that may indicate high risk, including scale, sensitivity, systematic monitoring, profiling, automated decisions, vulnerable individuals, novel technology, data combination, tracking, and barriers to exercising rights.

Record screening rationale, reviewer, date, evidence, and escalation. A “no DPIA required” conclusion should be explainable and revisited after material change.

## Chapter 19 — DPIA analysis and treatment

Where a DPIA is required, describe processing, necessity and proportionality, risks to individuals, sources of harm, likelihood/plausibility, safeguards, residual risk, owners, and approval. Integrate security, privacy, legal, product, engineering, and business evidence.

DPIA templates can support consistency but do not automatically establish adequacy.

## Chapter 20 — Security of processing

Implement security measures appropriate to risk, considering confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience, recovery, access control, encryption or pseudonymisation where appropriate, vulnerability management, monitoring, backup, testing, supplier security, and human factors.

Security claims should be supported by implementation and operating evidence, not policy statements alone.

## Chapter 21 — Personal-data breach detection and triage

Define how suspected personal-data breaches are identified, escalated, contained, investigated, and distinguished from other security or privacy events. Preserve timelines, affected data, populations, systems, causes, controls, recipients, and evidence.

The incident record should support risk-to-individuals assessment and later supervisory or data-subject notification decisions.

## Chapter 22 — Breach notification decision process

Establish a controlled workflow for assessing notification obligations, timing, content, supervisory-authority engagement, individual notification, documentation of non-notification decisions, and coordination with processors or other controllers.

Automation may calculate deadlines or assemble facts, but it must not make an unreviewed legal determination that notification is or is not required.

## Chapter 23 — Processor and subprocessor governance

Select processors using sufficient guarantees appropriate to risk and maintain required contractual and operating controls. Track instructions, confidentiality, security, subprocessors, rights assistance, DPIA/breach support, return/deletion, audit information, locations, transfers, and material service changes.

Contracts should align with actual processing and be supported by evidence of implementation.

## Chapter 24 — International transfers and fail-closed gate

Inventory transfers and remote-access scenarios involving personal data leaving the relevant protected area. Document the transfer mechanism, recipient, country, processor chain, safeguards, supplementary measures where needed, data flows, and review triggers.

Do not pass the gate when DPIA decisions are unsupported, breach workflows cannot meet required decision timing, processors are materially ungoverned, or international transfers lack a documented lawful mechanism and competent review.
