# Chapter 131 — Months 4–6

## Purpose

This chapter provides a practical implementation plan for months 4 through 6, emphasizing control maturity, technical validation, evidence quality, remediation, vendor governance, and independent challenge.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

By the end of month 6, the organization should have functioning lifecycle controls for material AI systems, completed remediation of urgent gaps, technical and operational validation for priority systems, current evidence repositories, and an assurance programme capable of identifying control failures.

## Plain-language explanation

Months 4–6 are where governance must prove it works. Policies, inventories, and classifications should now be tested against real systems, releases, incidents, vendors, and business decisions. Weak controls should be corrected before they become normalized.

## Month 4: deepen system-level implementation

For priority systems:

- finalize intended-purpose and use restrictions;
- complete risk, privacy, security, and fundamental-rights assessments;
- map legal obligations to controls;
- complete technical-documentation indexes;
- define human-oversight procedures;
- establish logging, monitoring, and incident triggers;
- confirm vendor evidence and contractual rights;
- document residual risk and approval.

## Month 5: validate and test

Perform proportionate testing of:

- control design and operation;
- accuracy and error rates;
- subgroup performance and bias;
- robustness and cybersecurity;
- prompt injection and misuse resistance;
- transparency notices;
- human-review effectiveness;
- fallback, suspension, and recovery;
- data quality and lineage;
- evidence completeness and reproducibility.

Require failed acceptance criteria to trigger remediation, restriction, or suspension.

## Month 6: institutionalize assurance

- establish recurring control testing;
- perform internal-audit or independent reviews of selected systems;
- validate evidence repositories and retention;
- conduct a regulatory-examination simulation;
- test incident and legal-hold procedures;
- review supplier concentration and exit readiness;
- report themes and systemic deficiencies to executives and the board;
- refresh the roadmap based on findings.

## Remediation discipline

Each material deficiency should include:

- clear root cause;
- affected systems and obligations;
- severity and exposure;
- interim controls;
- accountable owner;
- target date;
- validation method;
- escalation path;
- closure evidence.

Repeated extensions or exceptions should receive independent challenge.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay completes detailed assessments for its recruitment, fraud, disruption-assistance, and dynamic-pricing systems. Technical validation identifies weak abstention behavior in the disruption assistant and inconsistent subgroup performance in the fraud model.

GlobalWay restricts both features, implements remediation, retests them, and submits the results to an independent review team. It also completes a mock regulatory request and discovers that vendor version records are difficult to reconcile, leading to a new contractual and evidence-control requirement.

## Control activities

- Complete system-level assessments and control mappings.
- Validate priority systems technically and operationally.
- Test key controls independently.
- Maintain disciplined deficiency and corrective-action management.
- Strengthen vendor monitoring, evidence access, and exit readiness.
- Exercise incident, continuity, regulatory, and preservation processes.
- Escalate systemic themes to executives and the board.
- Refresh priorities based on evidence and findings.

## Evidence

- completed system assessments;
- technical-documentation indexes;
- validation plans and results;
- control-test workpapers;
- independent-review reports;
- remediation records;
- vendor monitoring and contract evidence;
- exercise and simulation results;
- legal-hold and incident tests;
- executive and board reporting;
- updated roadmap.

## Audit tests

1. Select priority systems and verify assessments and controls are complete and approved.
2. Confirm validation criteria were defined before testing.
3. Trace failed tests to restrictions, remediation, and retesting.
4. Review evidence quality and version reconciliation.
5. Test whether vendor monitoring and exit controls are operating.
6. Inspect independent assurance for competence and scope.
7. Verify systemic findings changed governance or roadmap priorities.

## Metrics

- priority systems fully assessed;
- validation completion rate;
- failed acceptance criteria;
- high-severity findings open and overdue;
- repeat control failures;
- systems with complete evidence indexes;
- critical vendors with current assurance;
- exercises completed;
- time to remediate and retest;
- exceptions repeatedly renewed.

## Management checklist

- Are priority systems fully documented and controlled?
- Do technical tests reflect realistic failure and misuse scenarios?
- Can failed criteria block or restrict deployment?
- Are findings corrected and independently validated?
- Can evidence be produced quickly and reconciled to production?
- Are vendor and continuity risks adequately tested?
