# Manual 35 — Singapore AI & Privacy Governance Release-Time Source State

**Verified:** 2026-08-31  
**State:** controlled source decision for publication preparation; not itself a publication approval.

## Binding privacy baseline

Singapore's Personal Data Protection Act 2012 (PDPA) and applicable regulations remain the binding privacy layer. Current PDPC materials identify the core data-protection obligations, including accountability, notification, consent, purpose limitation, accuracy, protection, retention limitation, transfer limitation, access/correction, and mandatory data-breach notification.

Current PDPC breach guidance states that once an organisation determines a breach is notifiable, notification to the PDPC is required as soon as practicable and no later than three calendar days after that determination. Applicability, significant-harm/significant-scale thresholds, affected-individual notification, exceptions, and sector overlays must be evaluated under current law and guidance rather than reduced to a universal incident-severity rule.

PDPC continues to mark the Data Portability Obligation as taking effect only when the necessary regulations are issued. Manual 35 therefore treats Data Portability as change-watch and must not describe it as operative unless release-time verification changes that status.

## AI governance baseline

Singapore's AI governance resources are treated as governance and assurance guidance, not standalone statutes. The controlled AI layer includes:

- the Model AI Governance Framework for Traditional AI (second edition, 2020);
- the final Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI released in 2024;
- AI Verify and associated implementation/testing resources; and
- the Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI launched in January 2026 and updated on 20 May 2026.

The May 2026 Agentic AI update adds practical case studies and best practices including multi-agent systems, third-party-agent risk, and automation-bias safeguards while maintaining human accountability, bounded authority, lifecycle controls, transparency, and user responsibility as central governance themes.

## Controlled legal-status boundary

Every chapter must keep distinct: (a) binding PDPA/statutory or regulatory obligations, (b) PDPC advisory/enforcement guidance, (c) voluntary IMDA/PDPC/AI Verify AI governance and testing frameworks, (d) sector-specific or contractual overlays, and (e) organisation-specific implementation choices. Following a voluntary AI framework does not by itself establish PDPA compliance, regulatory approval, certification, or legal sufficiency.
