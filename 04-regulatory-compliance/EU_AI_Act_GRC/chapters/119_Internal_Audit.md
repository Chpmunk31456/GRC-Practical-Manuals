# Chapter 119 — Internal Audit

## Purpose

This chapter explains how internal audit should provide independent, risk-based assurance over AI governance, EU AI Act readiness, control effectiveness, technical risk, management oversight, and remediation.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Internal audit should include material AI risks and obligations within its audit universe, maintain sufficient independence and competence, coordinate appropriately with other assurance functions, and report significant findings to senior management and the board or audit committee.

## Plain-language explanation

Management owns AI risk and controls. Compliance, legal, privacy, security, model-risk, and quality functions may provide oversight. Internal audit independently evaluates whether the governance framework and controls are designed appropriately, operate effectively, and produce reliable evidence.

## Internal-audit mandate

The audit charter and planning process should authorize review of:

- AI governance and accountability;
- legal applicability and classification;
- prohibited-practice screening;
- high-risk and GPAI obligations;
- risk and impact assessments;
- data, model, security, privacy, and resilience controls;
- human oversight and transparency;
- third-party dependencies;
- conformity and post-market readiness;
- incidents, complaints, exceptions, and remediation;
- management and board reporting.

## Independence and objectivity

Internal audit should not design, own, approve, or operate the controls it later audits. Where auditors previously advised on a programme, safeguards should address self-review threats through reassignment, independent review, cooling-off periods, or external support.

## Competence

The function should assess whether it has sufficient expertise in:

- EU AI Act obligations and role allocation;
- internal-control and assurance methods;
- data governance and privacy;
- cybersecurity and resilience;
- model performance and validation;
- bias, accessibility, and fundamental rights;
- human factors and oversight;
- sector-specific requirements.

Specialists should be used where necessary, while internal audit retains responsibility for scope, conclusions, and reporting.

## Three-lines coordination

Coordinate without diluting independence:

- first line owns AI systems, risks, and controls;
- second line sets frameworks, challenges risk decisions, and monitors compliance;
- third line independently assesses governance and controls.

Map assurance coverage to reduce duplication and identify unreviewed risk areas.

## Engagement execution

Internal-audit work should include:

- documented objectives, scope, criteria, and risk assessment;
- walkthroughs and control design evaluation;
- operating-effectiveness testing;
- technical specialist testing where required;
- evidence validation;
- issue rating and root-cause analysis;
- management-response challenge;
- follow-up and closure validation.

## Reporting

Reports should clearly distinguish:

- legal noncompliance;
- control-design weakness;
- operating failure;
- technical limitation;
- evidence gap;
- management-risk acceptance;
- scope limitation;
- advisory observation.

Material issues should be escalated promptly rather than held until the final report.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay internal audit reviews its AI governance programme. The audit finds that the inventory is incomplete, vendor model changes are not consistently reassessed, and board reporting excludes unresolved high-severity exceptions.

Internal audit rates the combined issue high because incomplete inventory undermines classification, monitoring, and reporting. Management establishes a remediation programme, and internal audit validates closure through inventory reconciliation, sample testing, and board-report review.

## Control activities

- Include AI in the audit universe and risk assessment.
- Approve a risk-based assurance plan.
- Protect internal-audit independence.
- Obtain qualified technical and legal support.
- Coordinate assurance coverage across the three lines.
- Report significant issues promptly.
- Track management actions and overdue remediation.
- Independently validate closure.

## Evidence

- audit charter;
- AI audit universe;
- annual risk assessment and plan;
- engagement work programmes;
- competence and independence assessments;
- specialist agreements;
- workpapers and technical evidence;
- audit reports;
- management responses;
- escalation records;
- follow-up and closure testing.

## Audit tests

1. Verify material AI risks are included in the audit universe.
2. Confirm audit selection reflects legal, operational, and rights-based risk.
3. Review internal-audit independence and conflicts.
4. Assess team competence and specialist use.
5. Inspect workpapers for sufficient evidence and challenge.
6. Trace high-severity findings to executive or board reporting.
7. Confirm closure was independently validated.

## Metrics

- material AI areas without assurance coverage;
- planned versus completed AI audits;
- high-severity findings;
- overdue management actions;
- repeat findings;
- engagements requiring specialists;
- scope limitations;
- time from issue identification to escalation;
- closure rejections after validation.

## Management checklist

- Is AI included in the internal-audit universe?
- Does internal audit remain independent from control ownership?
- Does the team have the required legal and technical competence?
- Are findings reported at the right level?
- Are overdue actions challenged?
- Is closure independently verified?
