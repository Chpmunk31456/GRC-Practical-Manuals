# Manual 46 — Enterprise AI Governance Training

**Status:** Controlled build — not published

## Purpose

This manual is a practitioner and senior-leadership training resource for designing, operating, assessing, and explaining an enterprise AI governance program. It is training material, not legal advice and not a substitute for authoritative standards or regulatory text.

## Learning outcomes

A learner completing this manual should be able to:

1. Explain AI governance to executives, technical teams, risk functions, auditors, and regulators.
2. Establish an enterprise AI governance operating model with defined accountability and decision rights.
3. Build and maintain an AI-system inventory and lifecycle governance process.
4. Classify AI use cases by regulatory, safety, privacy, security, operational, and reputational risk.
5. Apply the EU AI Act using role- and risk-based analysis rather than treating every AI system identically.
6. Explain and operationalize ISO/IEC 42001 as an AI management system.
7. Apply NIST AI RMF GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE functions and the NIST Generative AI Profile.
8. Integrate AI governance with ISO/IEC 27001, privacy, cybersecurity, third-party risk, incident management, and enterprise GRC.
9. Govern generative AI, RAG, vector databases, foundation-model services, and agentic AI.
10. Define evidence, metrics, monitoring, testing, exception, change-management, and retirement requirements.
11. Assess AI vendors and model/service dependencies.
12. Respond effectively to senior-manager AI-governance interview scenarios using implementation-focused answers.

## Core operating model

The manual will teach the following lifecycle as a reusable governance pattern:

**Discover → Inventory → Classify → Assess → Approve → Build/Acquire → Test → Deploy → Monitor → Change/Incident Management → Reassess → Retire**

Each gate must identify accountable ownership, applicable obligations, required evidence, approval/exception authority, monitoring requirements, and escalation criteria.

## Evidence model

Training will distinguish policy statements from auditable evidence. Example evidence includes AI inventory records, system/model cards, impact assessments, risk assessments, DPIAs where applicable, data lineage, evaluation results, security testing, red-team findings, vendor due diligence, contracts, approval records, exceptions, monitoring logs, incidents, model/version changes, human-oversight records, metrics, and retirement evidence.

## Source-control rule

Before factual regulatory deadlines, legal obligations, standard-specific requirements, or jurisdictional assertions are frozen for publication, they must be verified against current authoritative sources. Secondary summaries may support learning but must not override primary sources.

Priority authoritative source families include:

- European Union / European Commission / EUR-Lex for the EU AI Act.
- ISO for ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 23894 scope and official standard metadata; copyrighted ISO text must not be reproduced beyond permitted use.
- NIST for AI RMF 1.0 and NIST AI 600-1.
- Singapore IMDA / PDPC for Singapore AI governance frameworks.
- Applicable data-protection authorities and legislation for privacy claims.
