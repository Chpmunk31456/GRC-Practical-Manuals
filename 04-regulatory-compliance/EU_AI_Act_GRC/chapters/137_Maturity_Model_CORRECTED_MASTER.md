# Chapter 137 — AI Governance Maturity Model

> **Legal status:** Corrected English master for consolidation. This file controls over conflicting earlier Chapter 137 draft language.

## Requirement

An AI governance maturity model is a voluntary management tool, not a substitute for legal compliance. Organizations should use it to assess whether governance, risk, compliance, technical, operational, and assurance capabilities are consistently designed, implemented, evidenced, measured, and improved.

## Plain-English explanation

Legal duties apply regardless of an organization’s maturity score. A maturity model helps management identify weak or inconsistent capabilities, prioritize investment, and track progress, but it must not be used to justify noncompliance, postpone mandatory controls, or average away critical failures.

## Maturity levels

Use five levels:

1. **Initial** — activities are informal, reactive, owner-dependent, and poorly evidenced;
2. **Developing** — key policies and inventories exist, but implementation is incomplete or inconsistent;
3. **Defined** — approved processes, roles, controls, and evidence standards operate across material systems;
4. **Managed** — performance, risk, control effectiveness, incidents, and remediation are measured and governed;
5. **Optimizing** — continuous improvement, automation, independent assurance, horizon scanning, and lessons learned are embedded.

## Assessment domains

Assess at minimum:

- governance and accountability;
- inventory, classification, and legal-role management;
- risk and fundamental-rights assessment;
- data governance and privacy;
- secure development, validation, and change control;
- human oversight and transparency;
- supplier and dependency governance;
- monitoring, incidents, and corrective action;
- documentation, evidence, and recordkeeping;
- conformity, regulatory readiness, and internal assurance;
- AI literacy and competence;
- multijurisdictional governance and continuous improvement.

## GlobalWay example

GlobalWay rates each domain using documented criteria and evidence. It does not allow a strong overall average to conceal a critical weakness in recruitment-system oversight. The board receives domain-level ratings, high-risk gaps, remediation owners, target dates, and evidence of improvement.

## Control activity

Maturity assessments must use approved criteria, objective evidence, qualified reviewers, documented scoring rationale, and management challenge. Mandatory legal gaps must be reported separately from maturity scores and escalated according to severity.

## Evidence

- approved maturity framework and scoring criteria;
- domain assessments and evidence references;
- reviewer competence and independence records;
- management challenge and approval;
- legal-gap register;
- prioritized improvement roadmap;
- reassessment history and trend reporting.

## Audit test

Review the maturity programme. Confirm that ratings are evidence-based, legal obligations are not treated as optional maturity enhancements, critical gaps cannot be hidden by averaging, improvement actions are tracked, and reported progress is independently supportable.

## Primary legal references

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended: applicable governance, quality-management, risk-management, documentation, monitoring, competence, corrective-action, and assurance duties.
- The maturity levels and scoring methodology in this chapter are recommended management practices and are not prescribed by the EU AI Act.
