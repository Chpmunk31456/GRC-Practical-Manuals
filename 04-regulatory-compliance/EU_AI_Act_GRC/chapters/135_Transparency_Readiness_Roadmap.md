# Chapter 135 — Transparency Readiness Roadmap

## Purpose

This chapter provides a practical roadmap for identifying, designing, implementing, testing, and maintaining transparency obligations for AI systems.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should identify AI interactions and generated content subject to transparency duties, provide notices at the correct time and in an understandable form, support accessibility and language needs, maintain technical marking or disclosure controls where applicable, and preserve evidence that transparency measures operate as intended.

## Plain-language explanation

Transparency is not satisfied by placing a generic statement in a privacy policy. People should receive useful information when it matters: before or during an AI interaction, when content has been artificially generated or manipulated, or when they are exposed to systems that may influence decisions or perceptions.

## Phase 1: inventory transparency scenarios

Identify:

- systems that interact directly with natural persons;
- emotion-recognition or biometric-categorization uses;
- synthetic audio, image, video, and text generation;
- deepfake and manipulated-content scenarios;
- public-interest text and editorial responsibility;
- high-risk system information obligations;
- employee, candidate, traveler, customer, and supplier interactions;
- channels, languages, jurisdictions, and accessibility needs.

## Phase 2: determine applicable disclosures

For each use case, document:

- the legal and policy basis;
- who must receive the notice;
- when it must appear;
- the required content;
- exceptions or limitations;
- technical marking requirements;
- responsible owner and approver;
- evidence and monitoring requirements.

## Phase 3: design notices and markings

Transparency measures should be:

- clear and concise;
- distinguishable from unrelated terms;
- presented early enough to influence user choice;
- appropriate to the audience and context;
- accessible to persons with disabilities;
- available in relevant languages;
- consistent across interfaces and channels;
- supported by an effective human-contact or escalation path where appropriate.

## Phase 4: implement technical controls

Implement as applicable:

- visible AI-interaction notices;
- persistent or readily accessible disclosure indicators;
- machine-readable marking of synthetic content;
- metadata and provenance controls;
- deepfake labels;
- human escalation options;
- logging of notice presentation and acceptance where lawful;
- release controls that prevent deployment without approved disclosures.

## Phase 5: test effectiveness

Test:

- notice timing and visibility;
- comprehension by intended audiences;
- accessibility and assistive-technology compatibility;
- language quality;
- mobile, web, voice, and embedded-channel presentation;
- technical marking persistence after normal processing;
- user ability to obtain human support;
- consistency between actual system behavior and the notice.

## Phase 6: monitor and maintain

Reassess after:

- model or interface changes;
- new content-generation functions;
- expansion to new countries or languages;
- complaints or evidence of user confusion;
- new provider or platform capabilities;
- changes in legal guidance;
- reclassification or substantial modification.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay’s traveler chatbot displays an AI notice before the first response and provides a visible path to a human consultant. Its marketing team uses generative AI for destination images and maintains an approval process for required synthetic-content disclosures. Accessibility testing reveals that one notice is not announced correctly by screen readers, so release is paused until corrected.

## Control activities

- Maintain a transparency-use-case register.
- Approve notices and technical markings before release.
- Test timing, comprehension, accessibility, and language quality.
- Reconcile disclosures with actual system functions and limitations.
- Monitor complaints and user confusion.
- Trigger reassessment after material change.
- Preserve approved versions and implementation evidence.

## Evidence

- transparency applicability assessments;
- approved notice library;
- user-interface and content examples;
- accessibility and language tests;
- marking and provenance test results;
- release approvals;
- human-escalation tests;
- monitoring reports;
- complaints and corrective actions;
- version and change records.

## Audit tests

1. Select AI systems and verify transparency applicability was assessed.
2. Confirm notices appear at the required point and in the correct channel.
3. Compare notice statements with actual system behavior and limitations.
4. Review accessibility, language, and comprehension testing.
5. Test synthetic-content marking and disclosure persistence where applicable.
6. Verify human escalation functions.
7. Confirm changes trigger disclosure review and approval.

## Metrics

- systems requiring transparency measures;
- approved notices implemented;
- accessibility or language defects;
- disclosure failures;
- user complaints or confusion;
- synthetic-content marking failures;
- systems changed without notice reassessment;
- overdue remediation;
- human-escalation success rate.

## Management checklist

- Have all applicable AI interactions and synthetic-content uses been identified?
- Are notices timely, clear, accessible, and accurate?
- Do disclosures match actual system behavior?
- Are machine-readable markings implemented where required?
- Can users obtain meaningful human support?
- Are transparency controls retested after change?
