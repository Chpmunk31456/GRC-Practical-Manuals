# Manual 08 — Vendor and Third-Party Risk Lifecycle
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 09–16

> Original third-party-risk implementation guidance. This material does not certify suppliers or replace organization-specific legal, procurement, privacy, security, resilience, or financial review.

## Chapter 09 — Evidence quality

Evidence should be evaluated for relevance, currency, scope, independence, and applicability to the actual service. A document can be valid yet still irrelevant if it covers a different product, environment, entity, or period.

## Chapter 10 — Contract control requirements

Material risks identified during due diligence should be reflected in contract terms where appropriate. Topics may include security obligations, privacy/data handling, audit rights, incident notification, subcontracting, service levels, resilience, change notification, data return/deletion, and exit support.

## Chapter 11 — Right to audit and assurance access

Right-to-audit language should be usable in practice. The organization should understand whether assurance will be provided through reports, certifications, evidence packages, interviews, targeted reviews, onsite work, or other mechanisms.

## Chapter 12 — Subprocessors and fourth parties

Material subprocessors and fourth parties should be visible enough to understand concentration, data handling, operational dependency, and change risk. The level of visibility should be proportional to exposure and criticality.

## Chapter 13 — Concentration and dependency risk

Risk assessment should consider whether multiple critical processes rely on one supplier, region, platform, model provider, cloud service, or upstream dependency. Concentration may be hidden when different vendors share the same underlying service.

## Chapter 14 — Resilience and recovery evidence

For critical services, due diligence should examine recovery capability, dependency assumptions, test evidence, recovery objectives, communication processes, and whether the organization has a viable fallback or exit path.

## Chapter 15 — Incident notification and coordination

Contracts and operating procedures should establish how material supplier incidents are reported, what information is expected, who receives notification, and how joint containment, investigation, recovery, and regulatory or customer communications are coordinated.

## Chapter 16 — Approval package

The approval package should summarize service scope, criticality, inherent risk, due-diligence evidence, material findings, contractual treatments, open exceptions, residual risk, monitoring tier, exit considerations, and human approval.

Approval must correspond to the actual service and scope reviewed.
