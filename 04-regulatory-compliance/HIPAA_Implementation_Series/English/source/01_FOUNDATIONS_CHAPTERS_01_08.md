# Manual 06 — HIPAA Implementation and Audit
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 01–08

> Educational implementation guidance based on the controlled current-law HIPAA baseline. This material does not provide legal advice, determine covered-entity or business-associate status, establish compliance, or determine whether an incident is a reportable breach. Proposed Security Rule changes are readiness-only unless and until HHS issues a final rule and the controlled baseline is updated.

## Chapter 01 — Scope, entity role, and accountability

Begin by determining which organizational units, systems, services, and relationships fall within the HIPAA implementation scope. Record the basis for covered-entity, health-plan, health-care-provider, clearinghouse, business-associate, or subcontractor treatment where applicable, and identify accountable privacy, security, legal, compliance, operations, and business owners.

The implementation record should distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions requiring legal or regulatory interpretation. Scope decisions should be dated, evidence-backed, and reassessed after material organizational, service, data-flow, acquisition, or regulatory change.

## Chapter 02 — PHI and ePHI inventory

Organizations should maintain an inventory of protected health information and electronic protected health information that identifies where information is created, received, maintained, transmitted, stored, backed up, exported, or disposed of.

Records should identify systems, applications, endpoints, databases, cloud services, interfaces, removable media, paper processes where relevant, owners, custodians, data classifications, retention requirements, and external recipients. Unknown repositories and unmanaged exports should be treated as control gaps requiring investigation.

## Chapter 03 — Data-flow and boundary mapping

Map how PHI and ePHI move between workforce members, applications, facilities, cloud services, vendors, business associates, patients, health plans, providers, and other authorized recipients. Include trust boundaries, authentication points, interfaces, APIs, file transfers, messaging, remote access, and backup or archival paths.

Data-flow maps support risk analysis, minimum-necessary review, access-control design, incident investigation, business-associate governance, and breach assessment. Maps should be versioned and linked to the systems and processes they represent.

## Chapter 04 — Privacy governance and minimum necessary

Privacy governance should define who may use or disclose PHI, for what purpose, under what authority, and with what approval or verification. Where the minimum-necessary standard applies, processes should limit access, use, disclosure, and requests to the amount reasonably needed for the permitted purpose.

Role design, workflow approvals, reports, exports, analytics, support access, and administrative privileges should be reviewed for unnecessary exposure. Exceptions should identify rationale, authority, scope, owner, duration, and compensating controls.

## Chapter 05 — Security Rule risk analysis

Risk analysis should be accurate and thorough for the organization’s ePHI environment. It should identify assets, data flows, threats, vulnerabilities, existing safeguards, likelihood, potential impact, and resulting risk using a repeatable method appropriate to organizational size, complexity, capabilities, technical infrastructure, and risk context.

A risk analysis is not complete merely because a checklist was filled in or a vulnerability scan was run. Evidence should show the systems and ePHI considered, assumptions, methods, findings, limitations, responsible reviewers, and date of analysis.

## Chapter 06 — Risk management and treatment

Risk management should convert identified risks into accountable treatment decisions. For each material risk, record the planned safeguard or corrective action, owner, target date, dependencies, interim controls, residual risk, approval, and evidence of completion.

High-risk items should not remain indefinitely open without escalation or documented acceptance by an authorized risk owner. Completed remediation should be validated rather than closed solely on management assertion.

## Chapter 07 — Administrative safeguards

Administrative safeguards should translate HIPAA security responsibilities into operational governance. Relevant controls include assigned security responsibility, workforce authorization and supervision, information-access management, security awareness and training, security-incident procedures, contingency planning, evaluation, and business-associate arrangements where applicable.

Implementation should show who performs each control, how often it occurs, what evidence is retained, what exceptions exist, and how failures are escalated.

## Chapter 08 — Fail-closed implementation gate

A HIPAA implementation or audit package should fail closed when material scope is unresolved, required current-law analysis is missing, ePHI boundaries are unknown, significant risk-analysis gaps remain, evidence cannot support claimed safeguards, or required human/legal/compliance review is incomplete.

Automated repository QA may confirm structure, source-state records, links, and publication evidence. It cannot determine legal status, control effectiveness, compliance, or breach-reporting obligations. Material changes reopen affected review gates.
