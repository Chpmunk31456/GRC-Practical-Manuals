## Purpose

Use this plan to define how qualified people will supervise, understand, challenge, override, suspend, and escalate the operation of an AI system.

## 2. Oversight objectives

Define what human oversight is expected to prevent or reduce, including:

- unsafe or unlawful outcomes;
- automation bias;
- over-reliance on inaccurate outputs;
- discrimination or unfair treatment;
- inappropriate use outside intended purpose;
- failure to recognize uncertainty or model limitations;
- delayed response to incidents or drift.

## 5. Information available to reviewers

Confirm reviewers receive:

- intended purpose and limitations;
- input and output context;
- confidence or uncertainty indicators where meaningful;
- relevant source information;
- known bias and performance limitations;
- applicable policies and decision criteria;
- prior overrides, incidents, and complaints;
- instructions for challenge, override, suspension, and escalation.

## 6. Authority and independence

Document whether reviewers can:

- disregard or reverse the output;
- request additional evidence;
- defer a decision;
- obtain specialist advice;
- stop or suspend the process;
- escalate without retaliation or inappropriate performance pressure.

## 7. Automation-bias controls

Use controls such as:

- independent review before displaying the AI recommendation;
- mandatory rationale for acceptance and override;
- randomized quality review;
- reviewer rotation;
- alerts for unusually high acceptance rates;
- scenario-based training;
- separation of production targets from oversight quality.

## 9. Override and escalation procedure

1. Identify the questionable output or condition.
2. Preserve relevant inputs, outputs, and context.
3. Apply the approved alternative process.
4. Record the reviewer’s decision and rationale.
5. Escalate material, repeated, or systemic issues.
6. Trigger incident, risk, or change management where required.
7. Verify corrective action and communicate outcomes.

## 10. Monitoring

Track:

- acceptance and override rates;
- reviewer disagreement;
- time to review;
- escalation frequency;
- quality defects;
- subgroup differences;
- complaints and appeals;
- reviewer workload and fatigue;
- training and competence completion.

## 11. Failure and fallback

Document:

- manual fallback process;
- system suspension criteria;
- emergency contacts;
- continuity arrangements;
- evidence preservation;
- communication to affected persons.

## 12. Review triggers

Reassess after model, workflow, staffing, threshold, intended-purpose, legal, or incident changes.
