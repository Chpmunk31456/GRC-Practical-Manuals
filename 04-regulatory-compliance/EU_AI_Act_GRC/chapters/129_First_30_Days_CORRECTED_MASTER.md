# Chapter 129 — First 30 Days

> **Legal status:** Corrected English master for consolidation. This file controls over conflicting earlier Chapter 129 draft language.

## Requirement

The first 30-day roadmap is implementation guidance, not a standalone EU AI Act requirement. Organizations should use this period to establish legal applicability, actor roles, immediate prohibitions and deadlines, governance ownership, inventory completeness, risk triage, and urgent remediation priorities.

## Plain-English explanation

The first month should create control over scope and urgent exposure. It is not the time to declare full compliance. The organization should identify what AI exists, which legal rules apply, who is accountable, where evidence is missing, and which systems require immediate restriction, suspension, or escalation.

## First-30-day actions

Complete at minimum:

1. appoint executive, legal, compliance, technical, privacy, security, HR, procurement, and audit owners;
2. confirm the current legal baseline and application dates, including Regulation (EU) 2026/1744;
3. establish an authoritative AI-system and GPAI-model inventory;
4. identify provider, deployer, importer, distributor, authorised-representative, and other relevant roles;
5. screen for prohibited practices and immediately applicable duties;
6. triage potential high-risk, transparency, GPAI, and systemic-risk obligations;
7. identify systems affecting workers, applicants, customers, travelers, children, or other vulnerable persons;
8. preserve critical evidence and suspend unsupported deletion;
9. review serious incidents, complaints, known bias, security findings, and unresolved supplier issues;
10. impose interim controls or stop-use decisions where risk is unacceptable;
11. establish decision, escalation, and notification pathways;
12. approve a documented 90-day remediation and validation plan.

## GlobalWay example

During its first 30 days, GlobalWay inventories recruitment, traveler-assistance, fraud, pricing, and internal productivity systems. It identifies actor roles, screens for prohibited practices, freezes one unsupported recruitment workflow, preserves evidence for an open complaint, and assigns accountable owners for high-risk readiness and supplier remediation.

## Control activity

The accountable executive must approve the first-30-day scope, owners, urgent decisions, unresolved legal questions, and the transition into the 31-to-90-day plan. Progress metrics must distinguish completed evidence from planned work.

## Evidence

- governance appointments;
- current legal-baseline record;
- initial inventory and role assessments;
- prohibited-practice screening;
- risk-triage register;
- stop-use and interim-control decisions;
- incident and complaint review;
- supplier escalation records;
- approved 90-day plan.

## Audit test

Review the first-30-day programme. Confirm that it established a supportable inventory, identified urgent legal and operational risks, assigned accountable owners, documented interim safeguards, and did not misrepresent planned work as completed compliance.

## Primary legal references

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 where applicable.
- Applicable AI Act provisions depend on actor role, system classification, application date, intended purpose, and use context.
- This chapter is implementation guidance and does not replace legal analysis of exact statutory deadlines and duties.
