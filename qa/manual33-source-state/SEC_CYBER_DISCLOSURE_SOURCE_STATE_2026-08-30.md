# Manual 33 — SEC Cybersecurity Governance and Disclosure source-state decision

## Controlled baseline

Manual 33 uses the SEC's July 26, 2023 cybersecurity disclosure rules as the current federal securities-disclosure baseline for Exchange Act registrants, together with current SEC forms, compliance guidance, and interpretive material.

Primary authoritative sources:
- SEC adopting release, Cybersecurity Risk Management, Strategy, Governance, and Incident Disclosure, Release Nos. 33-11216; 34-97989.
- Regulation S-K Item 106 for annual cybersecurity risk-management, strategy, and governance disclosure.
- Form 8-K Item 1.05 for material cybersecurity incident disclosure by domestic registrants.
- Form 6-K and Form 20-F cybersecurity disclosure provisions for foreign private issuers.
- Current SEC Division of Corporation Finance compliance and disclosure interpretations and current SEC small-entity compliance guidance.

## Current rule state and applicability boundary

The controlled manual must preserve the distinction between binding Commission rules/form requirements and staff guidance, statements, compliance guides, petitions for rulemaking, enforcement activity, and organization-specific implementation practice.

Current rule mechanics to preserve include:
- Item 1.05 Form 8-K disclosure is tied to the registrant's determination that a cybersecurity incident is material, not merely to discovery of the incident.
- The materiality determination must be made without unreasonable delay and applies the federal securities-law materiality standard from the perspective of a reasonable investor.
- A required Item 1.05 Form 8-K is generally due within four business days after the materiality determination, subject to the rule's national-security/public-safety delay process.
- Regulation S-K Item 106 requires annual disclosure regarding cybersecurity risk-management processes, material effects or reasonably likely material effects from cybersecurity threats, board oversight, and management's role.
- Comparable annual disclosure requirements apply to foreign private issuers through Form 20-F, and material cybersecurity incident information may trigger Form 6-K furnishing requirements.
- Inline XBRL tagging requirements are already in effect for the covered cybersecurity disclosures.

The SEC currently has a 2025 public petition seeking amendments that would rescind certain incident-reporting requirements. A petition is not an adopted rule and must not be treated as changing the current binding baseline unless and until the Commission takes effective rulemaking action.

## Controlled architecture implications

The 32-chapter architecture should cover applicability and registrant types, materiality governance, incident escalation and determination, Form 8-K/Form 6-K workflows, annual Item 106/Form 20-F governance disclosure, board and management roles, risk-management process evidence, third-party incidents, related incidents, disclosure controls and procedures, legal/securities coordination, national-security/public-safety delay handling, Inline XBRL, evidence retention, testing, remediation, auditability, change monitoring, and cross-regime coordination without conflating SEC disclosure duties with cybersecurity-control frameworks or breach-notification statutes.

## Release boundary

This source-state record does not provide legal advice, make a materiality determination, determine issuer/registrant status, or assert compliance. Current SEC rule text, forms, interpretations, and any rulemaking developments must be rechecked before English freeze and again before publication. Localization, rendered/accessibility review, provenance, workflow security, exact-hash substantive human review where required, durable artifacts, predecessor publication, and final release reconciliation remain fail-closed.

Publication remains sequential behind Manuals 18–32.
