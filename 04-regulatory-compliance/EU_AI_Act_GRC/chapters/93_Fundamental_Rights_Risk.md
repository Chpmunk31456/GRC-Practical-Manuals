# Chapter 93 — Fundamental-Rights Risk

## Purpose

This chapter establishes a practical method for identifying, assessing, treating, and monitoring risks that an AI system may create for fundamental rights.

## Requirement

Organizations should assess rights impacts across the full AI lifecycle, especially where systems affect access to employment, education, essential services, mobility, safety, benefits, credit, insurance, healthcare, migration, law enforcement, or other consequential decisions.

## Plain-language explanation

An AI system can be technically accurate and still create unfair, inaccessible, intrusive, or harmful outcomes. Fundamental-rights risk assessment asks who may be affected, what rights may be impaired, how severe the impact could be, and what controls are required before use.

## Rights-risk domains

Assess, as applicable:

- equality and non-discrimination;
- dignity and autonomy;
- privacy and data protection;
- freedom of expression and information;
- access to effective remedy;
- rights of children, older persons, and persons with disabilities;
- worker and employment rights;
- consumer protection;
- access to essential services;
- due process and procedural fairness;
- safety and physical integrity.

## Assessment method

1. Define the system, purpose, users, and affected persons.
2. Identify decisions, recommendations, or actions the system influences.
3. Map potentially affected rights and vulnerable groups.
4. Identify plausible harm scenarios and misuse.
5. Score likelihood, severity, scale, reversibility, and detectability.
6. Evaluate existing safeguards and evidence.
7. Define additional controls, owners, and deadlines.
8. Determine residual risk and accountable approval.
9. Reassess after material changes, incidents, or new evidence.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay evaluates an AI disruption assistant that prioritizes rebooking options. The assessment identifies risk that wheelchair users, older travelers, and travelers with medical needs could receive unsuitable itineraries if accessibility data is incomplete. GlobalWay adds mandatory accessibility fields, human review for protected-need cases, supplier-data validation, exception monitoring, and an escalation route to a human travel consultant.

## Control activities

- Maintain a documented rights-risk methodology.
- Include affected-person and vulnerable-group analysis.
- Test representative scenarios and edge cases.
- Involve legal, privacy, accessibility, security, ethics, and business owners.
- Require documented approval before consequential deployment.
- Track mitigation actions and residual-risk decisions.
- Provide complaint, escalation, and remedy channels.

## Evidence

- fundamental-rights impact assessment;
- affected-person map;
- vulnerable-group analysis;
- scenario and misuse tests;
- accessibility and fairness test results;
- mitigation plan;
- residual-risk approval;
- complaint and remedy records;
- reassessment history.

## Audit tests

1. Select consequential AI systems and verify a rights-risk assessment exists.
2. Confirm the assessment identifies affected persons and vulnerable groups.
3. Review whether plausible harm scenarios were tested.
4. Trace high-risk findings to controls and accountable owners.
5. Verify residual risk was formally approved.
6. Confirm complaints, incidents, and material changes trigger reassessment.

## Metrics

- systems assessed for rights risk;
- high-severity rights risks identified;
- overdue mitigation actions;
- complaints and remedy requests;
- accessibility failures;
- repeat rights-risk findings;
- residual risks accepted by level.

## Management checklist

- Who may be adversely affected?
- Which rights could be impaired?
- Are vulnerable groups specifically considered?
- Can affected persons challenge or appeal outcomes?
- Are mitigations tested and evidenced?
- Is residual risk explicitly approved?
