# Chapter 123 — Market-Surveillance Authorities

## Purpose

This chapter explains how organizations should prepare for oversight by market-surveillance authorities responsible for evaluating whether AI systems placed on the market or put into service comply with applicable requirements.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should maintain procedures, evidence, and accountable contacts that support cooperation with market-surveillance authorities, including information production, inspections, technical evaluation, corrective action, withdrawal, recall, restriction, and follow-up where applicable.

## Plain-language explanation

Market surveillance focuses on whether systems available or operating in the market meet legal requirements. Authorities may request documents, inspect systems, examine incidents, require corrective action, or restrict a system. Readiness depends on current evidence and clear operational authority.

## Readiness scope

Identify:

- systems placed on the market or put into service;
- relevant legal entities and economic-operator roles;
- Member States and sectors;
- high-risk and product-safety dependencies;
- authorized representatives, importers, distributors, and providers;
- registration and conformity records;
- post-market monitoring and incident data;
- responsible internal and external contacts.

## Evidence expected

Maintain proportionately:

- intended-purpose and classification records;
- technical documentation;
- conformity-assessment and declaration records;
- quality-management evidence;
- instructions for use and transparency materials;
- logging and post-market monitoring records;
- validation and performance results;
- incident and complaint records;
- corrective-action and change records;
- supply-chain and economic-operator documentation.

## Inspection and information requests

Procedures should cover:

- authentication of the authority;
- scope and deadline analysis;
- secure evidence collection;
- version reconciliation;
- interviews and demonstrations;
- access to systems and premises where legally required;
- confidentiality and personal-data controls;
- legal and technical review;
- documented submission approval.

## Corrective measures

Prepare for actions such as:

- correcting documentation or labeling;
- restricting functionality or use;
- strengthening oversight or monitoring;
- suspending deployment;
- notifying customers or partners;
- withdrawing or recalling a system;
- replacing a model or component;
- conducting additional testing;
- reporting completion to authorities.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

A market-surveillance authority asks GlobalWay to demonstrate that a high-risk recruitment system matches its technical documentation and human-oversight plan. GlobalWay verifies the production version, produces conformity and testing evidence, demonstrates the override process, and identifies one outdated instruction document. It corrects the document, notifies affected users, and validates closure.

## Control activities

- Maintain a market-surveillance readiness plan.
- Identify systems and economic-operator roles by jurisdiction.
- Keep conformity, technical, and post-market evidence current.
- Reconcile evidence to the deployed version.
- Define authority for restriction, suspension, withdrawal, and recall.
- Track corrective measures through verified closure.
- Conduct readiness simulations for material systems.

## Evidence

- market and jurisdiction inventory;
- economic-operator role map;
- technical and conformity files;
- declarations and registrations;
- inspection procedures;
- request and production logs;
- corrective-action records;
- withdrawal or recall plans;
- simulation results;
- authority correspondence.

## Audit tests

1. Select systems in the EU market and confirm economic-operator roles are documented.
2. Verify technical and conformity evidence matches deployed versions.
3. Review inspection and information-request procedures.
4. Test authority for suspension, withdrawal, and recall.
5. Trace prior corrective measures to validated closure.
6. Review post-market information for unreported or unresolved concerns.

## Metrics

- systems without current market-surveillance files;
- version mismatches;
- overdue authority requests;
- unresolved corrective measures;
- simulations completed;
- time to restrict or suspend a system;
- withdrawal or recall readiness gaps.

## Management checklist

- Can we identify every system subject to market surveillance?
- Does the evidence match the actual deployed version?
- Can we demonstrate conformity and human oversight?
- Who can restrict, suspend, withdraw, or recall the system?
- Are corrective measures validated and reported promptly?

## Figure specification — Market-Surveillance Response Path

Create a workflow from authority request or market signal through authentication, evidence production, inspection, technical evaluation, finding, corrective measure, restriction or withdrawal where needed, validation, reporting, and closure.
