# Manual 55 — AI Third-Party, Vendor & Supply-Chain Governance

**Canonical stage:** 0 — authoritative-source baseline and third-party control architecture  
**Date:** 1 September 2026

## Purpose

Establish a practical governance model for external AI models, APIs, platforms, agents, tools, datasets, hosting, orchestration, plugins, integrators and other supply-chain dependencies across selection, contracting, onboarding, operation, change, incident response and exit.

## Initial control domains

1. AI third-party inventory and ownership;
2. service/model/provider classification and materiality;
3. due diligence and evidence sufficiency;
4. provider security and privacy posture;
5. training-data and prompt/output use restrictions;
6. model/provider/version transparency;
7. subprocessors and fourth parties;
8. hosting, residency and cross-border data flows;
9. identity, access, tools and delegated authority;
10. model/data/software supply-chain integrity;
11. contractual control requirements and audit/evidence access;
12. change notification and revalidation triggers;
13. performance, robustness and service-level dependencies;
14. incident notification, investigation and evidence preservation;
15. business continuity, concentration and substitution risk;
16. regulatory and policy applicability allocation;
17. ongoing monitoring and risk scoring;
18. exceptions, residual risk and conditional approval;
19. termination, portability, deletion and credential revocation;
20. post-exit assurance and lessons learned.

## Relationship to prior manuals

Manual 51 provides agent identity, authorization and action-boundary controls. Manual 52 provides adversarial security and supply-chain threat controls. Manual 53 provides privacy/data-governance requirements. Manual 54 provides independent model/system validation. Manual 55 consolidates those dependencies into the third-party lifecycle and contract/evidence model rather than duplicating them.

## Stage-0 completion criterion

Stage 0 is complete when source families, actor/provider taxonomy, lifecycle phases, minimum evidence classes, contract-control domains, concentration/continuity risks and exit requirements are controlled well enough to begin detailed source-to-control mapping and scenario construction.
