# Manual 09 — NIST CSF 2.0 Controlled Implementation
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 09–16

> Controlled implementation guidance for IDENTIFY and PROTECT. Organizations should select implementation methods according to context, risk, resources, dependencies, and applicable obligations.

## Chapter 09 — Asset and service inventory

Maintain sufficient visibility of systems, applications, data, identities, facilities, cloud services, operational technology, suppliers, and business services to support risk decisions. Inventories should identify owners, criticality, dependencies, lifecycle state, and unknowns.

Unmanaged or unidentified assets should be treated as risk requiring investigation rather than assumed to be outside scope.

## Chapter 10 — Cybersecurity risk assessment

Identify threats, vulnerabilities, exposure, likelihood or plausibility, consequences, existing safeguards, uncertainty, and affected objectives using a repeatable method appropriate to the organization.

Assessment evidence should distinguish facts, assumptions, estimates, and limitations. Risk scores without traceable reasoning should not be treated as sufficient evidence.

## Chapter 11 — Risk prioritization and improvement

Convert assessment results, incidents, tests, audits, business changes, and threat information into prioritized improvement actions. Record owners, target dates, dependencies, interim measures, residual risk, and escalation.

Improvement should be continuous and risk-driven, not limited to periodic compliance cycles.

## Chapter 12 — Identity and access management

Manage identities, credentials, authentication, authorization, privileged access, service accounts, and access lifecycle according to risk and business need.

Evidence should support joiner/mover/leaver controls, privileged-access governance, periodic review, exception handling, and timely removal or restriction of unnecessary access.

## Chapter 13 — Awareness and workforce resilience

Define cybersecurity knowledge and behavior expectations for the workforce and relevant third parties. Training should reflect role, access, threat exposure, and responsibilities.

Track assignment, completion, overdue items, role-specific requirements, lessons from incidents, and whether training changes behavior or reduces risk where measurable.

## Chapter 14 — Data security

Protect data according to sensitivity, business value, legal or contractual needs, lifecycle state, and threat exposure. Address access, encryption or equivalent protection where appropriate, integrity, backup, retention, transfer, disposal, and leakage risk.

Data protection claims should be supported by inventories, configurations, monitoring, tests, or other relevant evidence.

## Chapter 15 — Platform and configuration security

Establish risk-based secure configuration, vulnerability management, patching, malware defense, endpoint/server/cloud controls, software integrity, and lifecycle management.

Exceptions, unsupported technology, compensating controls, and remediation ownership should be visible rather than hidden by aggregate compliance percentages.

## Chapter 16 — Infrastructure resilience and protective gate

Design networks, services, facilities, cloud dependencies, backups, capacity, segmentation, redundancy, and recovery-enabling safeguards according to criticality and risk.

PROTECT is incomplete when critical safeguards are unknown, material vulnerabilities lack accountable treatment, privileged access is uncontrolled, data protections cannot be evidenced, or resilience assumptions remain untested.
