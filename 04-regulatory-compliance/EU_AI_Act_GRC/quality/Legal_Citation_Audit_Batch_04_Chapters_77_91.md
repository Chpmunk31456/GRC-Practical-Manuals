# Legal and Citation Audit — Batch 04 (Chapters 77–91)

## Scope

This batch governs the legal and citation review of Chapters 77–91 of the English EU AI Act GRC manual. It is a controlling review record pending consolidation of corrected chapter masters into the publication source set.

## Controlling legal baseline

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended.
- Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 and any other amendments applicable as of publication.
- Current consolidated EUR-Lex text controls over summaries, earlier drafts, and secondary commentary.
- European Commission guidelines, templates, codes of practice, standards, and FAQs must be identified by legal status and must not be presented as binding law unless they have binding effect.

## Review objectives

For each chapter in this batch, confirm:

1. the correct regulated actor or actors;
2. the exact trigger, scope, exception, and safeguard;
3. effective date and transitional treatment;
4. evidence and recordkeeping expectations;
5. value-chain dependencies and contractual consequences;
6. operational controls that are proportionate and auditable;
7. alignment with the recurring GlobalWay Travel Services case study;
8. distinction between legal requirements, recommended practices, and non-binding guidance.

## Mandatory corrections and quality gates

### Governance and accountability

- Distinguish board, executive, management, control-function, and operational ownership.
- Avoid implying that internal governance titles replace statutory actor obligations.
- Require documented authority, escalation, conflict management, and decision records.

### Inventory, classification, and lifecycle controls

- Link every control to a unique system or model inventory record and production version.
- Reassess after intended-purpose changes, material technical changes, new jurisdictions, new actor roles, or new legal requirements.
- Preserve classification rationale, assumptions, reviewer identity, approval date, and next-review trigger.

### Policies, standards, and procedures

- Separate mandatory policy requirements from implementation guidance.
- Require version control, approval, training, exceptions, monitoring, and periodic review.
- Ensure procedures define accountable owners, inputs, outputs, timelines, and retained evidence.

### Training and competence

- Distinguish Article 4 AI-literacy duties from broader recommended role-based competence programs.
- Require training content to reflect role, context, risk, technical knowledge, experience, education, and affected persons.
- Avoid treating completion certificates alone as proof of effective competence.

### Metrics, monitoring, and reporting

- Require metrics to be tied to legal duties, risks, controls, decisions, thresholds, and escalation.
- Distinguish activity metrics from effectiveness and outcome metrics.
- Prevent dashboards from presenting estimates as verified compliance facts.

### Internal audit and assurance

- Preserve independence, scope, competence, evidence, issue grading, management response, remediation, and closure validation.
- Do not imply that internal audit replaces conformity assessment, authority review, or notified-body involvement where legally required.
- Require sampling to cover systems, models, actors, jurisdictions, vendors, changes, incidents, and complaints.

### Management review and continuous improvement

- Require review of compliance status, incidents, complaints, post-market data, regulatory change, risk trends, audit findings, resources, and unresolved decisions.
- Document decisions, owners, deadlines, risk acceptance, and follow-up verification.
- Ensure improvement actions update affected policies, controls, technical documentation, training, monitoring, and contracts.

## Evidence expectations

Each corrected chapter should identify evidence such as:

- governance charters and decision-rights matrices;
- AI inventory and classification records;
- policies, standards, procedures, and exception records;
- role-based training plans and competence assessments;
- metrics catalogues, dashboards, thresholds, and escalation records;
- internal-audit plans, workpapers, findings, and closure validation;
- management-review packs, minutes, decisions, and action tracking;
- regulatory-change assessments and implementation records.

## Audit approach

For each chapter:

1. trace each legal statement to the current consolidated regulation;
2. identify non-binding guidance explicitly;
3. test whether the GlobalWay example matches the legal trigger and actor role;
4. verify that the control can be operated and evidenced;
5. verify that the audit test can independently establish design and operating effectiveness;
6. record unresolved legal interpretation for counsel review rather than guessing.

## Publication status

Chapters 77–91 remain subject to corrected-master consolidation, editorial review, graphics review, citation verification, and final DOCX/PDF quality assurance before release.
