# Chapter 131 — Months 4–6

> **Legal status:** Corrected English master for consolidation. This chapter is a recommended implementation roadmap, not a statutory timetable. It controls over conflicting earlier Chapter 131 language.

## Requirement

Organizations should use months 4–6 to stabilize the AI governance operating model, close high-priority gaps, expand assurance, improve evidence quality, and prepare affected systems for applicable legal and conformity obligations.

## Plain-English explanation

This phase moves the programme from initial implementation to repeatable operation. The EU AI Act does not require completion of these activities within six months. Internal milestones must remain aligned to the organization’s actual legal roles, applicable effective dates, system classifications, and sector obligations.

## Months 4–5 priorities

1. complete overdue applicability, role, and classification assessments;
2. validate prohibited-practice screening and high-risk determinations;
3. remediate material data, documentation, oversight, security, transparency, and supplier-control gaps;
4. formalize change-management and substantial-modification review;
5. expand AI-literacy activities by role and risk;
6. establish post-market monitoring and incident workflows where applicable;
7. test inventory, intake, approval, logging, and evidence-retention controls;
8. define escalation thresholds and closure criteria.

## Month 6 priorities

1. conduct targeted design and operating-effectiveness testing;
2. assess readiness for conformity, registration, declaration, marking, and authority access where applicable;
3. validate management and board reporting;
4. review vendor dependencies, audit rights, incident notification, and exit planning;
5. approve residual-risk decisions and compensating controls;
6. refresh the implementation roadmap based on findings, legal changes, and business priorities.

## GlobalWay example

GlobalWay completes delayed classifications, remediates missing supplier evidence, tests human-oversight and logging controls, and conducts readiness reviews for systems that may fall within high-risk categories. Management postpones one release because technical documentation and post-market monitoring evidence are incomplete.

## Control activity

The AI governance body must review progress at least monthly, distinguish statutory obligations from programme targets, escalate overdue material actions, and require evidence-based closure before risk restrictions or release blocks are removed.

## Evidence

- updated inventory and classification register;
- remediation plans and closure evidence;
- control-test workpapers;
- supplier assurance and contract records;
- training and competence records;
- readiness assessments;
- residual-risk approvals;
- executive and board reporting.

## Audit test

Confirm that programme milestones were risk-based, material legal and control gaps were prioritized, closure decisions were supported by evidence, readiness reviews did not substitute for legally required conformity processes, and management reporting reflected actual status.
