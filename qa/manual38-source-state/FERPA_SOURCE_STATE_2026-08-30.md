# Manual 38 — FERPA Authoritative Source State

**Manual:** 38 — FERPA Controlled Implementation  
**State:** downstream authoritative-source gate only; publication state unchanged  
**Verified:** 2026-08-30/31 UTC against current U.S. Department of Education Student Privacy Policy Office materials and the current FERPA regulatory baseline

## Controlled baseline

The current federal baseline is the **Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), 20 U.S.C. § 1232g**, implemented by **34 CFR Part 99**. The U.S. Department of Education Student Privacy Policy Office (SPPO) administers FERPA guidance and compliance activity.

Manual 38 must preserve the distinction between statutory requirements, binding regulations, Department guidance/FAQs, enforcement findings or letters, other federal/state student-privacy laws, and organization-specific implementation practices.

## Scope and applicability boundaries

FERPA generally applies to educational agencies and institutions that receive funds under a program administered by the U.S. Department of Education. The manual must not imply universal coverage of every private school, vendor, education-adjacent entity, or record type. Applicability must be evaluated against the statute, 34 CFR Part 99, the identity of the educational agency or institution, the status of the record as an education record, and any relevant exception or overlay.

FERPA rights generally belong to parents and transfer to the student when the student reaches age 18 or attends a postsecondary institution at any age, subject to the regulation and applicable exceptions.

## Source-layer boundaries

Manual 38 must separately identify:

1. FERPA statutory requirements under 20 U.S.C. § 1232g;
2. binding regulations in 34 CFR Part 99;
3. current Department/SPPO official guidance and FAQs;
4. enforcement findings, letters, or case-specific materials;
5. other applicable federal or state student-privacy requirements;
6. contracts, data-sharing agreements and school/vendor implementation controls;
7. organization-specific policies, procedures, risk decisions and evidence.

Guidance must not be presented as if it were the statutory or regulatory text. Case-specific enforcement material must not be generalized beyond its supported scope.

## Core control boundaries

The controlled manual must preserve and correctly scope at least the following areas:

- parent and eligible-student rights to inspect and review education records;
- amendment/challenge procedures;
- the general consent rule and documented exceptions;
- school-official and legitimate-educational-interest determinations;
- directory-information designation, notice and opt-out controls;
- audit/evaluation and studies exceptions, including written-agreement requirements where applicable;
- record-of-disclosure and redisclosure requirements where applicable;
- annual FERPA rights notification;
- reasonable methods to authenticate and authorize access;
- vendor/service-provider governance where a third party acts under a permitted FERPA exception;
- retention, destruction and data-minimization controls where driven by agreement, exception, policy or another applicable law;
- incident and unauthorized-disclosure handling without inventing a FERPA-specific universal breach-notification rule.

No blanket claim may be made that consent is always required, that every educational-data disclosure is prohibited, that every third party is directly regulated as an educational institution, or that FERPA itself creates a universal breach-reporting deadline.

## Current source confirmation

Current Department materials continue to identify the FERPA statute as 20 U.S.C. § 1232g and the regulations as 34 CFR Part 99. Current SPPO materials also continue to describe the 45-day inspection right, amendment right, consent framework, annual notice requirement, and regulatory exceptions such as school-official, audit/evaluation, studies and directory-information disclosures.
