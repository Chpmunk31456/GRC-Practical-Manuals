# Chapter 127 — Regulatory Notification

## Purpose

This chapter explains how organizations should identify, assess, approve, and deliver regulatory notifications concerning serious incidents, noncompliance, corrective actions, market restrictions, and other reportable AI matters.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should maintain a documented notification process that identifies applicable authorities, legal triggers, reporting deadlines, required content, approval authority, communication channels, preservation requirements, and follow-up commitments. Potential notification duties should be assessed immediately when a material AI incident or compliance failure is identified.

## Plain-language explanation

A notification obligation may depend on the organization’s role, the system classification, the type and severity of harm, where the system is used, and which authority has jurisdiction. The organization must move quickly without submitting unverified or misleading information. Initial reports may be preliminary, but they should clearly distinguish known facts, reasonable assessments, and unresolved questions.

## Potential notification triggers

Assess whether notification may be required after:

- a serious incident;
- death, serious injury, or significant risk to health or safety;
- serious and irreversible disruption of critical infrastructure;
- infringement of obligations intended to protect fundamental rights;
- suspected prohibited AI practice;
- material noncompliance involving a high-risk AI system;
- corrective action, restriction, suspension, recall, or withdrawal;
- cybersecurity compromise affecting AI safety, integrity, or compliance;
- authority request or investigation;
- substantial modification affecting regulatory status;
- material vendor or component failure;
- related privacy, consumer-protection, product-safety, employment, or sector notification duties.

## Notification assessment

Document:

- date and time of detection;
- event timeline;
- system, model, version, and environment;
- organization role and accountable owner;
- affected jurisdictions and authorities;
- people, rights, and services affected;
- known and potential consequences;
- legal triggers and deadlines;
- evidence available and missing;
- containment and corrective actions;
- whether other notification regimes apply;
- approval and submission status.

## Authority and jurisdiction mapping

Maintain a current register of:

- competent national authorities;
- market-surveillance authorities;
- notifying authorities and notified bodies where relevant;
- data-protection, product-safety, consumer, employment, sector, and cybersecurity authorities;
- authority contact channels;
- language and format requirements;
- legal-entity responsibilities;
- escalation and external-counsel contacts.

## Notification content

A notification should include, as applicable:

- reporting organization and contact information;
- organization role in the AI value chain;
- affected AI system and intended purpose;
- system and model versions;
- event date, detection date, and timeline;
- incident or noncompliance description;
- affected persons, locations, and services;
- known or potential harm;
- immediate containment;
- technical and organizational analysis;
- vendors and dependencies involved;
- corrective-action plan;
- evidence limitations and open questions;
- planned updates and responsible contact.

## Timeliness and staged reporting

Where full facts are not available before a deadline, submit an approved preliminary notification when legally appropriate and provide verified updates. Do not delay solely to achieve a perfect report. At the same time, do not speculate or make unsupported admissions.

## Coordination across regimes

An AI incident may also trigger obligations under data protection, cybersecurity, product safety, employment, consumer protection, contracts, insurance, or sector regulation. Coordinate messages and facts so that submissions are consistent while respecting each regime’s scope and deadline.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay discovers that its AI fraud-detection platform has incorrectly blocked a group of travelers because a vendor update changed a geographic risk feature. The legal team determines that the event may involve fundamental-rights and consumer-protection concerns in several EU jurisdictions.

GlobalWay restricts the feature, preserves the affected version and logs, identifies the relevant authorities, and submits an approved preliminary notification containing the known facts, containment measures, affected population estimate, and planned investigation. Follow-up reports provide validated impact figures, root cause, remediation, and evidence of safe restart.

## Control activities

- Maintain a notification-trigger matrix.
- Identify authorities and deadlines by jurisdiction and role.
- Activate legal and executive review immediately after material events.
- Preserve system versions, logs, and decision evidence.
- Use controlled templates and approval workflows.
- Coordinate related notification regimes.
- Track acknowledgements, questions, updates, and commitments.
- Validate corrective action before closure.

## Evidence

- notification policy and trigger matrix;
- authority and jurisdiction register;
- incident assessments;
- legal analyses;
- draft and submitted notifications;
- approval records;
- delivery confirmations;
- regulator correspondence;
- follow-up reports;
- corrective-action evidence;
- commitment and deadline trackers;
- closure decisions.

## Audit tests

1. Select material incidents and verify notification duties were assessed promptly.
2. Confirm authority, role, jurisdiction, and deadline determinations were documented.
3. Compare submitted facts with source evidence and incident timelines.
4. Verify preliminary reports identified uncertainty and follow-up obligations.
5. Review consistency across AI, privacy, cybersecurity, product, and sector notifications.
6. Trace regulator questions and commitments through verified closure.
7. Confirm late or missed notifications were escalated and remediated.

## Metrics

- material incidents assessed for notification;
- time from detection to legal assessment;
- notifications submitted on time;
- late or missed notifications;
- preliminary reports awaiting update;
- inconsistent cross-regime submissions;
- overdue regulator commitments;
- notifications requiring correction;
- time to validated closure.

## Management checklist

- Which authority and legal entity are responsible?
- What deadline applies, and when did the clock begin?
- Are known facts separated from assumptions and unknowns?
- Have all relevant notification regimes been considered?
- Are containment and corrective actions clearly documented?
- Are follow-up commitments owned and tracked?
