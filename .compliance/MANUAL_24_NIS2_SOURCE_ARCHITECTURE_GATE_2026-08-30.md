# Manual 24 — NIS2 Source and Controlled Architecture Gate

**Series order:** 24  
**State:** source/architecture preparation; not publication authorization  
**Predecessor:** Manual 23 — DORA

## Source boundary

Use Directive (EU) 2022/2555 (NIS2) as the primary EU legal baseline and keep it distinct from national transposition measures, Commission implementing acts, ENISA guidance, sector-specific law, contractual requirements, and organization-specific implementation practices. Before controlled-English freeze, reverify the current EUR-Lex consolidated text, relevant Commission implementing acts, ENISA materials, and applicable Member State transposition requirements for the intended use case.

## Controlled architecture

Prepare a 32-chapter implementation architecture covering applicability and entity classification; essential/important entity distinctions; management-body accountability; governance; risk management; asset and service scope; incident handling; business continuity and crisis management; supply-chain security; secure acquisition/development/maintenance; vulnerability handling/disclosure; cryptography; access control; MFA/secure communications; workforce security; logging/monitoring; detection; incident classification; significant-incident reporting; early warning/notification/final reporting; evidence retention; supervisory cooperation; registration/data requirements; sector overlays; cross-border considerations; supplier evidence; testing; metrics; exceptions; remediation; source watch; localization; provenance; accessibility; and release controls.

Every substantive chapter must identify source layer, applicability, accountable owner, operating procedure, evidence object/location, review/test method, exception/remediation path, and reassessment trigger.

## Fail-closed boundaries

- Do not present the Directive text as identical to any Member State's transposition law.
- Do not generalize sector-specific or national requirements across the EU.
- Preserve distinctions among legal obligations, implementing acts, ENISA guidance, contractual requirements, and internal controls.
- Treat incident-reporting timelines and classification thresholds as source-controlled items requiring release-time reverification.
- Do not claim legal advice, regulator approval, or certification.
