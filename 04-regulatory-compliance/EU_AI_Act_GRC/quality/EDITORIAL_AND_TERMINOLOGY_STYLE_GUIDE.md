# EU AI Act Manual — Editorial and Terminology Style Guide

## Purpose

This guide controls terminology, tone, structure, spelling, formatting, and cross-language consistency for the English, Spanish, and Brazilian Portuguese editions.

## English master

- Use clear international English with consistent professional terminology.
- Use sentence case for headings unless a defined title requires otherwise.
- Use “organization” in general prose unless quoting an official source that uses another spelling.
- Use “programme” only where established in the existing manual; do not mix “program” and “programme” within the same context.
- Use “AI system” as the default term; do not substitute “model” unless the text specifically concerns a model.
- Use “provider” and “deployer” only in their EU AI Act legal sense.
- Distinguish “must” for binding requirements, “should” for recommended controls, and “may” for options or possibilities.
- Do not use absolute statements where legal exceptions, conditions, or jurisdictional overlays apply.

## Chapter structure

Each substantive chapter should retain this sequence where applicable:

1. Purpose
2. Requirement
3. Plain-language explanation
4. GlobalWay Travel Services example
5. Control activities
6. Evidence
7. Audit tests
8. Metrics
9. Management checklist
10. Figure specification and alt text

Additional legal or technical sections may appear before the example when needed.

## Citations and legal references

- Write article references as “Article 10” and annex references as “Annex III.”
- Use article ranges only when every article in the range supports the statement.
- Do not cite a recital as the sole basis for a binding obligation when an operative article exists.
- Separate legal citations from references to standards or guidance.
- Preserve official regulation numbers, dates, and titles exactly.

## Tables and checklists

- Keep column headings concise and parallel.
- Use blank cells only in templates intended for completion.
- Avoid tables so wide that they become unreadable in DOCX or PDF.
- Repeat header rows across pages in final production.
- Do not split a short checklist item across pages when avoidable.

## Accessibility

- Every informative graphic requires meaningful alt text.
- Avoid relying on color alone to convey legal status or risk.
- Use descriptive link text in publication files.
- Maintain logical heading order.
- Ensure tables have clear headers and reading order.

## Translation controls

- Translate the approved English master only after legal freeze.
- Preserve legal-role distinctions and defined terms.
- Maintain an approved multilingual glossary.
- Do not translate official names, regulation numbers, article numbers, or product names incorrectly.
- Require a second-pass review for false cognates, gender agreement, regional usage, punctuation, and legal nuance.
- Record intentional localization separately from literal translation.

## Editorial completion criteria

A file passes when terminology, capitalization, hyphenation, legal modality, chapter structure, table formatting, accessibility text, and cross-references are internally consistent and suitable for translation.
