# Manual 19 — FedRAMP / FISMA Current Source State

**Series order:** 19  
**Verification date:** 2026-08-30  
**State:** controlled source decision for English construction; not publication authorization

## Current decision

FedRAMP is in an active transition from the traditional Rev. 5 authorization process toward FedRAMP 20x. Current official FedRAMP materials identify Phase 3 as active for FY26 Q3-Q4 and state that the Class A pipeline opened August 3, 2026 and the Class B/Class C 20x pipelines open August 31, 2026. FedRAMP currently targets June 11, 2027 as the end date for accepting new Rev. 5 certifications. The 2026 Consolidated Rules timeline identifies optional early adoption beginning July 4, 2026 and mandatory adoption beginning January 1, 2027, subject to rule-specific applicability/effective dates.

This manual therefore must not encode a single timeless FedRAMP process. It must use a transition-aware decision model that distinguishes current Rev. 5 paths, current 20x certification classes, agency authorization responsibilities, and future transition milestones.

NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 remains the current NIST security/privacy control catalog baseline for this manual's NIST control relationship. NIST controls are guidance/control catalog material, not statutory FISMA text. FISMA statutory obligations, OMB policy, CISA directives, NIST RMF/control guidance, FedRAMP program requirements, and agency-specific authorization requirements must remain separate source layers.

## Controlled boundaries

1. Do not equate FedRAMP certification with an agency's authorization decision or with blanket FISMA compliance.
2. Do not treat Rev. 5 process artifacts as permanent 20x requirements.
3. Do not treat public-preview, RFC, pilot, or future-phase material as current mandatory requirements unless officially effective for the relevant path.
4. Keep agency-specific overlays and authorization conditions separate from generally applicable FedRAMP program requirements.
5. Preserve assessor independence/competence boundaries and distinguish evidence support from authorization judgment.
6. Reopen affected source, semantic, localization, artifact, and provenance gates after any material source-state change.
