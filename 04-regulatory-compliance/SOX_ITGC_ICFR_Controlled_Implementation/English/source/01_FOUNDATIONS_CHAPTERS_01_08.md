# Manual 13 — SOX ITGC / ICFR Controlled Implementation

Controlled English source. Original implementation guidance; do not treat this manual as legal advice, an audit opinion, or a substitute for SEC/PCAOB requirements.

## Chapter 1 — Purpose and operating boundary
This manual translates SOX/ICFR technology-control obligations into a practical governance and evidence model. It distinguishes binding law and SEC rules, PCAOB auditor requirements, regulator guidance, and optional implementation frameworks. Use the manual to design, operate, test, evidence, remediate, and reassess technology controls that support internal control over financial reporting (ICFR). Do not use it to make an organization-specific materiality, filer-status, exemption, or legal conclusion without competent judgment.

## Chapter 2 — SOX Sections 302 and 404 context
Section 302 executive certifications and Section 404 management assessment/reporting create different but related governance expectations. Establish a responsibility map showing management certification support, control ownership, disclosure-process inputs, ICFR evaluation responsibilities, and escalation routes. Technology teams should understand which systems, reports, data flows, and automated controls materially support financial reporting and which cybersecurity activities are outside ICFR scope unless they affect those dependencies.

## Chapter 3 — Management versus external-auditor responsibilities
Management owns the design, operation, assessment, and documentation of ICFR. The external auditor independently evaluates ICFR when an integrated audit or attestation is required. Keep management testing distinct from auditor procedures. Evidence prepared by management should be reproducible and sufficiently complete to support management conclusions without being tailored merely to satisfy an expected audit sample.

## Chapter 4 — Scope and applicability
Define the reporting entity, relevant accounts and disclosures, significant processes, financially relevant applications, interfaces, infrastructure, service organizations, reports, spreadsheets, and data stores. Document why each technology component is in or out of ICFR scope. Reassess scope after acquisitions, system migrations, major releases, cloud moves, new outsourcing arrangements, material process redesigns, or significant deficiencies.

## Chapter 5 — Financial-statement risk and assertion linkage
Technology controls should trace to financial-reporting risks rather than generic control catalogs. Link business-process risks to relevant assertions, significant accounts/disclosures, automated controls, reports, interfaces, and IT dependencies. Each ITGC should explain how failure could affect completeness, accuracy, authorization, valuation, occurrence, cutoff, presentation, or another relevant financial-reporting objective.

## Chapter 6 — Governance and accountability
Assign executive accountability, process owners, control owners, system owners, evidence custodians, testers, reviewers, and remediation owners. Define segregation-of-duties expectations between control operation and independent evaluation. Establish escalation thresholds for failed controls, repeated exceptions, overdue remediation, unresolved access conflicts, failed jobs, unauthorized changes, and unreliable reports.

## Chapter 7 — Entity-level technology controls
Entity-level controls include governance over access, change, operations, security, risk assessment, monitoring, and financial-system oversight. Evaluate whether these controls operate with enough precision to reduce risks at lower levels. Broad policy statements alone are not sufficient evidence of operating effectiveness; the organization should demonstrate governance actions, monitoring, decisions, and follow-through.

## Chapter 8 — ITGC domains and ICFR relevance
Organize ITGCs around access to programs and data, program changes, program development/acquisition, and computer operations, while adding interfaces, IPE/reports, and modern service dependencies where needed. A control belongs in ICFR only when it supports a financially relevant dependency. Avoid expanding SOX scope to every security control merely because it is good practice.
