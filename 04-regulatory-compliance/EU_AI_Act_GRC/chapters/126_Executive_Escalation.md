# Chapter 126 — Executive Escalation

## Purpose

This chapter defines how material AI risks, incidents, compliance failures, regulatory matters, and unresolved control deficiencies should be escalated to senior management and the board.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should establish documented escalation criteria, decision rights, communication channels, response timelines, and evidence requirements for material AI matters. Escalation should occur early enough for authorized leaders to restrict, suspend, remediate, disclose, or accept risk before harm or noncompliance increases.

## Plain-language explanation

Escalation is not simply forwarding an email. It is a controlled transfer of a material decision to a person with the authority, information, independence, and resources to act. Delayed or incomplete escalation can turn a manageable control issue into a serious incident or enforcement matter.

## Escalation triggers

Escalate proportionately when there is:

- suspected prohibited AI practice;
- serious incident or credible risk of significant harm;
- material fundamental-rights, safety, discrimination, privacy, or cybersecurity concern;
- high-risk classification uncertainty affecting deployment;
- failed conformity or technical-validation criteria;
- inability to provide required human oversight;
- significant model drift or unexpected behavior;
- material vendor failure or unapproved change;
- regulatory inquiry, investigation, inspection, or urgent information request;
- missed regulatory deadline or notification obligation;
- evidence destruction, concealment, falsification, or integrity concern;
- repeated or overdue high-severity findings;
- exception outside approved risk appetite;
- potential material financial or reputational exposure.

## Escalation levels

Define levels such as:

### Operational escalation

For issues that can be contained and resolved by the accountable product, technology, risk, or business owner within approved tolerances.

### Executive escalation

For material issues requiring cross-functional authority, additional resources, deployment restriction, customer remediation, legal strategy, or significant risk acceptance.

### Board escalation

For matters involving enterprise risk appetite, serious incidents, prohibited practices, major regulatory exposure, significant financial impact, repeated management failure, or strategic decisions about continuation or withdrawal.

## Required escalation package

Provide:

- concise issue statement;
- system, model, version, owner, and jurisdictions;
- triggering event and timeline;
- people, rights, and business services affected;
- known facts, uncertainties, and evidence gaps;
- legal and policy obligations;
- current risk rating and potential consequences;
- containment already performed;
- decision options and trade-offs;
- recommended action;
- accountable owner and required deadline;
- communication and notification implications.

## Decision options

Authorized leaders may:

- continue with enhanced monitoring;
- restrict users, data, geography, or functionality;
- require additional human review;
- suspend deployment or operation;
- roll back to a known-good version;
- notify affected people, customers, partners, insurers, or authorities;
- commission independent validation or investigation;
- terminate or replace a vendor;
- accept residual risk within authority;
- withdraw the system from service.

## Communication discipline

Escalation records should be factual, concise, traceable, and candid about uncertainty. Avoid minimizing the issue, overstating certainty, or presenting a preferred outcome as the only option. Sensitive communications should follow legal, confidentiality, and privilege requirements.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay’s disruption assistant begins recommending invalid travel alternatives after a vendor model update. Monitoring shows a growing error rate during severe weather, and consultants cannot reliably identify affected recommendations.

The issue exceeds operational tolerance and is escalated to the executive AI risk committee. The committee restricts the feature, requires manual verification, directs legal and customer-impact review, and authorizes rollback. Because travelers may have relied on incorrect recommendations, the matter is also reported to the board risk committee.

## Control activities

- Define materiality thresholds and escalation levels.
- Assign decision rights and alternates.
- Maintain 24-hour or emergency pathways for severe matters.
- Require a standardized escalation package.
- Record decisions, rationale, conditions, and dissent.
- Track required actions and deadlines.
- Escalate overdue or ineffective remediation.
- Review escalation performance after incidents and exercises.

## Evidence

- escalation policy and thresholds;
- authority and decision-rights matrix;
- escalation records and packages;
- meeting minutes and decisions;
- restriction, suspension, or rollback approvals;
- executive and board reports;
- action and commitment trackers;
- communications and notification decisions;
- post-incident reviews;
- escalation exercises and training records.

## Audit tests

1. Review material incidents and confirm escalation occurred within required timelines.
2. Verify decision makers had appropriate authority and received sufficient information.
3. Trace decisions to containment, remediation, notification, and closure evidence.
4. Confirm high-severity overdue findings were escalated.
5. Review whether board reporting met established criteria.
6. Test emergency escalation paths through simulation or records.
7. Confirm escalation lessons resulted in threshold, process, or training improvements.

## Metrics

- time from detection to executive escalation;
- time from escalation to decision;
- material issues not escalated on time;
- overdue executive actions;
- systems restricted, suspended, or withdrawn;
- repeat issues after executive decision;
- board-reportable AI matters;
- emergency escalation exercises completed;
- escalations with incomplete evidence packages.

## Management checklist

- Are materiality thresholds clear and understood?
- Can urgent issues reach an authorized executive immediately?
- Does the escalation package distinguish facts, assumptions, and unknowns?
- Are leaders given realistic alternatives, including suspension?
- Are decisions and conditions documented and tracked?
- Does the board receive matters that exceed management authority or risk appetite?
