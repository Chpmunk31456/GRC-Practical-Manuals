# Chapter 132 — Months 7–12

## Purpose

This chapter provides a practical implementation plan for months 7 through 12, focusing on enterprise-wide adoption, regulatory readiness, sustainable assurance, maturity improvement, and integration into normal business operations.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

By the end of month 12, AI governance should operate as a sustainable enterprise capability rather than a temporary project. Material systems should be inventoried, classified, controlled, monitored, tested, and supported by reliable evidence, with clear accountability and continuous improvement.

## Plain-language explanation

The final six months of the first-year roadmap are about scale and durability. Governance must work across business units, countries, technologies, and vendors. Management should be able to demonstrate that the programme is not dependent on a few individuals or one-time remediation efforts.

## Months 7–8: scale and standardize

- extend the control baseline across business units and legal entities;
- apply role, risk, sector, jurisdiction, and technology overlays;
- integrate intake and approval into procurement and development tooling;
- standardize evidence repositories and naming conventions;
- automate inventory, monitoring, and evidence collection where reliable;
- establish recurring business-unit attestations;
- expand role-based training and competence assessment.

## Months 9–10: strengthen monitoring and assurance

- operationalize continuous compliance monitoring;
- define drift, incident, complaint, and change triggers;
- expand internal audit coverage;
- perform thematic reviews across multiple systems;
- assess control dependencies and concentration risk;
- test board and executive reporting quality;
- conduct regulatory, incident, continuity, and vendor-exit exercises;
- validate corrective-action closure.

## Months 11–12: demonstrate readiness and improve maturity

- perform enterprise readiness assessment;
- confirm high-risk, GPAI, and transparency obligations are addressed;
- reconcile system inventories, documentation, deployed versions, and vendor records;
- review regulatory deadlines and unresolved legal interpretations;
- assess programme maturity and residual risk;
- approve year-two priorities and budget;
- refresh policies, controls, training, and assurance plans;
- report programme effectiveness and remaining exposure to the board.

## Sustainable operating model

The target model should include:

- accountable executive and governing committee;
- central policy and control ownership;
- distributed business and system accountability;
- embedded legal, privacy, security, data, procurement, HR, and audit participation;
- reliable inventory and classification processes;
- lifecycle gates and change controls;
- technical validation and monitoring;
- issue, exception, and incident management;
- independent assurance;
- regulatory-response capability;
- funded continuous improvement.

## Year-end readiness criteria

Confirm that:

- all material systems have accountable owners;
- prohibited-practice screening is complete;
- high-risk and transparency classifications are current;
- mandatory documentation and evidence are retrievable;
- human oversight and incident procedures are tested;
- critical vendors are monitored and contractually controlled;
- material changes trigger reassessment;
- findings and exceptions are actively governed;
- executives and the board receive reliable reporting;
- year-two priorities address remaining gaps.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

By month 12, GlobalWay has integrated AI intake into procurement and development workflows, implemented a centralized evidence register, expanded monitoring across all critical systems, and completed internal audits of recruitment and fraud-detection tools.

A year-end readiness review identifies remaining weaknesses in regional vendor documentation and multilingual transparency notices. These items become funded year-two priorities, while the board approves continued operation subject to documented interim controls and quarterly reporting.

## Control activities

- Scale governance across entities and business units.
- Standardize and automate reliable processes.
- Expand monitoring and independent assurance.
- Exercise regulatory, incident, continuity, and vendor-exit readiness.
- Perform enterprise year-end readiness and maturity assessment.
- Reconcile documentation and deployed-system versions.
- Approve year-two resources and priorities.
- Report effectiveness, residual risk, and unresolved exposure to the board.

## Evidence

- enterprise control deployment records;
- business-unit attestations;
- automated inventory and monitoring reports;
- training and competence records;
- thematic and internal-audit reports;
- exercise results;
- readiness assessment;
- maturity assessment;
- year-end risk and exception reports;
- board materials and approvals;
- year-two roadmap and budget.

## Audit tests

1. Confirm governance and controls operate across material entities and business units.
2. Test automated inventory or monitoring outputs for completeness and reliability.
3. Review thematic assurance and closure validation.
4. Reconcile selected production systems to approved documentation and vendor versions.
5. Verify readiness exercises identified and corrected realistic weaknesses.
6. Assess year-end maturity conclusions against supporting evidence.
7. Confirm year-two priorities address residual legal, technical, and control risk.

## Metrics

- enterprise inventory coverage;
- controls operating by entity and business unit;
- automated evidence coverage;
- high-risk systems with current readiness evidence;
- monitoring alerts investigated on time;
- audit findings and repeat findings;
- regulatory and continuity exercises passed;
- overdue exceptions and remediation;
- maturity score by domain;
- year-two actions funded and assigned.

## Management checklist

- Has AI governance become part of normal operations?
- Do controls scale across entities and jurisdictions?
- Can we demonstrate readiness with current evidence?
- Are monitoring and assurance identifying real problems?
- Does the board understand residual risk and unresolved gaps?
- Is the year-two programme funded and prioritized?
