# Manual 11 — GDPR Privacy and Data Protection Controlled Implementation
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 01–08

> Original implementation and training guidance aligned to the GDPR. This material does not reproduce the Regulation and does not provide organization-specific legal advice.

## Chapter 01 — GDPR purpose, scope, and accountability

Start by identifying why personal data is processed, whose data is involved, which entities participate, where processing occurs, and which legal and operational relationships matter. GDPR implementation should connect legal obligations to accountable owners, systems, data flows, contracts, controls, evidence, and review.

Applicability conclusions should be documented with scope, assumptions, jurisdictional facts, responsible reviewers, and unresolved questions.

## Chapter 02 — Material and territorial scope

Assess whether activities involve personal data and whether GDPR territorial rules apply, including establishment-based processing and relevant offering/monitoring scenarios involving individuals in the Union.

Do not rely on headquarters location alone. Record entities, establishments, markets, data subjects, processing locations, services, monitoring behavior, and representative requirements where applicable.

## Chapter 03 — Personal data and identifiability

Identify direct and indirect identifiers, online identifiers, device and location data, account data, behavioral information, inferred attributes, pseudonymous records, and combinations of data that may relate to an identifiable natural person.

Anonymisation conclusions require evidence that re-identification risk has been reduced to an appropriate level in context; pseudonymised data generally remains personal data.

## Chapter 04 — Controller, processor, and joint-controller roles

Determine roles from actual purposes and means of processing rather than contract labels alone. Document who decides why processing occurs, essential means, instructions, independent uses, shared decisions, and processor/subprocessor chains.

Role analysis should be revisited when services, data uses, AI functionality, contracts, or decision rights change.

## Chapter 05 — Data-protection principles

Operationalize lawfulness, fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity/confidentiality, and accountability as design and operating requirements.

Each material processing activity should show how principles affect collection, use, access, sharing, retention, deletion, notices, security, and evidence.

## Chapter 06 — Accountability and evidence

Accountability requires more than policy statements. Maintain evidence of decisions, ownership, notices, records of processing, contracts, DPIAs, rights handling, security controls, training, incident decisions, transfer mechanisms, reviews, and corrective actions.

Evidence should be attributable, current, scoped to the actual processing, and retained according to a controlled evidence model.

## Chapter 07 — Governance and privacy operating model

Define decision rights across privacy, legal, security, data governance, engineering, product, procurement, HR, marketing, operations, audit, and business leadership. Establish escalation paths for high-risk processing, rights conflicts, incidents, transfers, supplier issues, and unresolved legal interpretation.

The operating model should distinguish advisory, approval, implementation, challenge, and oversight responsibilities.

## Chapter 08 — Applicability and governance fail-closed gate

Do not proceed as if GDPR obligations are fully mapped when scope is unresolved, roles are unclear, material processing is not inventoried, accountable owners are missing, or significant legal questions have no assigned reviewer.

Automation may assist discovery and classification, but it cannot make final organization-specific applicability or role determinations without competent human review.
