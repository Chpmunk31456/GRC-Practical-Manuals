# Chapter 122 — AI Office and National Authorities

## Purpose

This chapter explains how organizations should understand, document, and operationalize their interactions with the European AI Office and relevant national competent authorities.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should identify the authorities relevant to their AI roles, systems, jurisdictions, and sectors; maintain current contact and responsibility maps; and establish procedures for regulatory communication, cooperation, evidence production, escalation, and follow-up.

## Plain-language explanation

The EU AI Act is supervised through a network of European and national authorities. An organization should not wait for an inquiry to determine which authority matters, who will respond, or where the required evidence is stored.

## Authority mapping

Document:

- legal entity and establishment;
- provider, deployer, importer, distributor, authorized representative, or product-manufacturer role;
- Member States where systems are placed on the market or used;
- whether GPAI or systemic-risk obligations apply;
- sector regulators and market-surveillance authorities;
- data-protection, consumer, employment, product-safety, and cybersecurity authorities where relevant;
- lead internal contacts and escalation routes.

## European AI Office

The organization should understand when the AI Office may be relevant, particularly for matters involving general-purpose AI, systemic-risk models, codes of practice, technical guidance, evaluations, information requests, and coordination at Union level.

Internal procedures should support:

- verified receipt of communications;
- legal and technical triage;
- coordinated responses;
- preservation of evidence;
- executive escalation;
- tracking of commitments and deadlines.

## National competent authorities

National authorities may have supervisory, enforcement, registration, inspection, or coordination responsibilities. The authority map should identify the responsible bodies for each Member State and sector in which the organization operates.

## Cooperation principles

Regulatory engagement should be:

- timely;
- accurate;
- complete;
- evidence based;
- consistent across jurisdictions;
- transparent about uncertainty and gaps;
- protected for confidentiality and privilege where lawful;
- centrally tracked.

## Cross-functional response team

Include as appropriate:

- legal;
- compliance;
- privacy;
- cybersecurity;
- data science and engineering;
- product and business owners;
- internal audit;
- communications;
- local-country leadership;
- vendor management.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay maps the national authorities relevant to its recruitment, fraud-detection, and traveler-assistance systems in each EU market. It also identifies the AI Office as potentially relevant to its use of a third-party GPAI model. A central regulatory-response lead coordinates local counsel, system owners, and technical specialists.

## Control activities

- Maintain a current authority and jurisdiction register.
- Assign accountable regulatory-response owners.
- Authenticate communications and deadlines.
- Coordinate legal, technical, and business review.
- Preserve and index supporting evidence.
- Track commitments, remediation, and follow-up.
- Review the authority map after legal, organizational, or deployment change.

## Evidence

- authority register;
- jurisdiction map;
- role and system classification records;
- response-team roster;
- regulatory correspondence;
- response approvals;
- evidence indexes;
- commitment trackers;
- training and simulation records;
- periodic review logs.

## Audit tests

1. Verify the organization has identified relevant Union and national authorities.
2. Trace selected systems to applicable jurisdictions and regulators.
3. Review response ownership and escalation paths.
4. Inspect prior communications for timeliness, consistency, and approval.
5. Confirm commitments and requests were tracked through closure.
6. Test whether authority maps are updated after material change.

## Metrics

- jurisdictions without confirmed authority mapping;
- overdue regulatory responses;
- unresolved commitments;
- conflicting submissions;
- response-team training completion;
- evidence-production delays;
- authority-map review status.

## Management checklist

- Do we know which authorities may supervise each material AI system?
- Are Union, national, and sector responsibilities distinguished?
- Can we respond consistently across countries?
- Are legal and technical experts available quickly?
- Are commitments to authorities centrally tracked?
