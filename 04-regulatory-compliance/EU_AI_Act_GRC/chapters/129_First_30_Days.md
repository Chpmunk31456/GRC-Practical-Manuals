# Chapter 129 — First 30 Days

## Purpose

This chapter provides a practical first-30-day implementation plan for establishing control, visibility, accountability, and immediate risk reduction across an organization’s AI activities.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

The first 30 days should establish governance, stop clearly unacceptable exposure, create an initial AI inventory, identify accountable leaders, classify urgent use cases, preserve evidence, and define a controlled implementation programme.

## Plain-language explanation

The first month is not the time to build a perfect compliance system. It is the time to gain control. Management should know which AI systems exist, which uses may be prohibited or high risk, who owns them, what data they use, which vendors are involved, and where immediate restrictions are required.

## Days 1–5: mobilize

- appoint an executive sponsor;
- name a programme lead and cross-functional steering group;
- define legal, risk, privacy, security, technical, procurement, HR, audit, and business participation;
- approve an interim AI governance mandate;
- establish escalation and decision authority;
- identify applicable legal entities, jurisdictions, and sectors;
- preserve critical existing records and decisions.

## Days 6–10: discover

Launch a rapid inventory covering:

- production AI systems;
- pilots, proofs of concept, and shadow AI;
- embedded vendor AI features;
- employee use of generative AI;
- high-impact automated decisions;
- models, data sources, providers, and critical dependencies;
- business and technical owners;
- affected people and jurisdictions.

Use interviews, procurement records, software inventories, cloud logs, expense records, and business-unit attestations to improve completeness.

## Days 11–15: triage

Prioritize immediate review of:

- suspected prohibited practices;
- employment, education, credit, insurance, essential-service, biometric, safety, law-enforcement, migration, and justice use cases;
- systems affecting vulnerable people;
- public-facing generative AI;
- sensitive or special-category data;
- systems without human review;
- systems with recent incidents, complaints, or material changes;
- vendor services lacking documentation or contract protections.

## Days 16–20: stabilize

Apply interim measures such as:

- suspend clearly unacceptable uses;
- restrict functionality or population;
- require human review;
- disable sensitive-data input;
- add transparency notices;
- preserve logs;
- block unapproved tools;
- require vendor escalation;
- freeze material changes pending review;
- define safe-stop and incident contacts.

## Days 21–25: establish minimum governance

Approve interim versions of:

- AI policy;
- intake and approval process;
- inventory standard;
- prohibited-practice screen;
- risk-classification method;
- incident escalation procedure;
- vendor due-diligence requirements;
- evidence-retention expectations;
- AI-literacy and acceptable-use guidance.

## Days 26–30: plan the programme

Create a prioritized roadmap that identifies:

- legal and control gaps;
- system-specific remediation;
- resource and competence needs;
- owners and target dates;
- regulatory deadlines;
- dependency and vendor constraints;
- assurance and testing needs;
- reporting cadence;
- budget and technology requirements.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay begins with a rapid inventory and discovers 42 AI use cases, including 11 not previously known to central governance. It immediately suspends one employee emotion-analysis pilot, restricts a recruitment tool pending classification and bias review, adds AI notices to two traveler chatbots, and freezes a vendor model upgrade until change assessment is complete.

By day 30, GlobalWay has an executive sponsor, programme office, triaged inventory, interim policy, urgent remediation tracker, and approved 12-month roadmap.

## Control activities

- Establish executive sponsorship and programme governance.
- Create a rapid but evidence-based inventory.
- Screen urgently for prohibited and high-impact uses.
- Apply interim restrictions where risk is unclear or unacceptable.
- Preserve critical evidence and logs.
- Define minimum policies and lifecycle gates.
- Assign owners, deadlines, and escalation paths.
- Report material exposure to executives and the board.

## Evidence

- programme charter;
- steering-group membership;
- rapid inventory;
- business-unit attestations;
- triage criteria and results;
- suspension and restriction decisions;
- interim policies and procedures;
- urgent remediation register;
- executive and board reports;
- approved 12-month roadmap.

## Audit tests

1. Verify executive sponsorship and decision authority were formally established.
2. Review whether inventory discovery used multiple reliable sources.
3. Test urgent-use-case triage for prohibited and high-impact exposure.
4. Confirm interim restrictions were implemented and monitored.
5. Verify material risks were escalated promptly.
6. Review roadmap ownership, priorities, dependencies, and target dates.
7. Confirm records created during mobilization are retained and traceable.

## Metrics

- AI systems identified;
- newly discovered shadow-AI use cases;
- urgent systems triaged;
- systems suspended or restricted;
- systems without accountable owners;
- critical evidence gaps;
- overdue first-month actions;
- high-risk or prohibited-practice reviews pending;
- business units completing attestations.

## Management checklist

- Who is accountable for the programme?
- Do we know where AI is being used?
- Which use cases require immediate suspension or restriction?
- Are critical logs and records preserved?
- Are owners and deadlines assigned?
- Does the board understand the most material exposure?
