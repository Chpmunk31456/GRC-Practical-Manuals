# Impact Assessment, Implementation, and Escalation

## 1. Convert intake into a controlled decision

A verified change should move through documented applicability, impact, decision, implementation, validation, and closure stages. The decision record should identify who decided, what facts and sources were considered, what remains uncertain, and when reassessment is required.

## 2. Assess impact across the operating model

Evaluate potential effects on:

- governance, accountability, and reporting;
- policies, standards, procedures, and records;
- products, services, customer journeys, and disclosures;
- contracts, procurement, and supplier obligations;
- systems, architecture, configurations, logging, and data flows;
- privacy, security, safety, accessibility, and human rights;
- workforce roles, training, monitoring, and labor conditions;
- finance, tax, insurance, licensing, and capital requirements;
- audit, certification, assessment, and regulatory engagement.

## 3. Identify implementation deliverables

Translate the change into specific deliverables with owners, dependencies, acceptance criteria, evidence, and deadlines. Examples include revised policies, configuration changes, contract amendments, notices, risk assessments, training, new controls, monitoring rules, reports, registrations, or testing.

## 4. Build a deadline hierarchy

Use the earliest binding date unless qualified review supports another interpretation. Distinguish internal target dates from legal or contractual dates. Include time for design, approval, procurement, development, testing, communication, training, deployment, evidence collection, and contingency.

## 5. Manage dependencies

Record dependencies on legal interpretation, regulator guidance, technology releases, suppliers, budget, staffing, works councils, customer approvals, data migration, or other projects. A dependency does not remove accountability; it should trigger monitoring and escalation.

## 6. Use controlled implementation status

Recommended states include not started, analysis, approved, planned, in progress, blocked, testing, implemented, validation pending, closed, exception approved, and cancelled with rationale.

## 7. Escalate threatened deadlines

Escalate when the organization may miss a binding date, when scope or interpretation is unresolved near the decision deadline, when required resources are unavailable, when implementation creates material operational risk, or when responsible owners do not accept accountability.

## 8. Manage interim measures

When final implementation cannot be completed immediately, document temporary safeguards, limitations, monitoring, communications, residual risk, approval, and expiration. Interim measures should not be labeled compliant without qualified approval.

## 9. Validate implementation

Validation should confirm that deliverables exist, affected scope is covered, controls operate as designed where required, evidence is retained, communications and training occurred, contracts and records were updated, and residual issues are visible.

## 10. Closure authority

Closure should require accountable business acceptance and appropriate compliance, legal, security, privacy, audit, or other specialist review based on the obligation. Project completion alone is insufficient.
