# Chapter 124 — Investigations and Information Requests

## Purpose

This chapter explains how organizations should manage investigations and formal information requests concerning AI systems while preserving evidence, meeting deadlines, protecting confidentiality, and ensuring truthful, consistent responses.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should maintain a controlled investigation-response process covering request authentication, legal triage, preservation, collection, review, production, interviews, technical demonstrations, remediation, and commitment tracking.

## Plain-language explanation

An investigation may move quickly and involve legal, technical, operational, and human-rights questions at the same time. Poor coordination can lead to incomplete answers, inconsistent statements, lost evidence, or missed deadlines. A disciplined process reduces those risks.

## Trigger events

The process may be activated by:

- formal authority requests;
- inspections or examinations;
- serious incidents;
- complaints or whistleblower reports;
- suspected prohibited practices;
- product-safety concerns;
- data-protection or discrimination allegations;
- media or public-interest scrutiny;
- vendor or supply-chain failures;
- internal audit escalation.

## Initial response

Immediately:

1. authenticate the request or triggering event;
2. preserve the original communication;
3. identify jurisdiction, authority, scope, deadlines, and legal basis;
4. appoint an investigation lead;
5. issue evidence-preservation instructions where appropriate;
6. identify relevant systems, versions, people, vendors, and records;
7. establish secure communication and tracking.

## Investigation governance

Define:

- accountable executive;
- legal and compliance lead;
- technical investigation lead;
- evidence custodian;
- privacy and confidentiality review;
- communications control;
- interview coordination;
- regulator liaison;
- decision authority for suspension or restriction.

## Evidence handling

Preserve and document:

- system and model versions;
- prompts, configurations, and code;
- logs and monitoring records;
- training, validation, and production data where lawful;
- approvals and risk assessments;
- vendor communications;
- incidents, complaints, and changes;
- relevant emails, messages, and meeting records;
- chain of custody and access history.

Do not alter, backdate, selectively delete, or create misleading records.

## Response preparation

Each response should:

- address the exact request;
- cite supporting evidence;
- identify assumptions and limitations;
- distinguish facts from analysis;
- reconcile system and document versions;
- disclose known gaps honestly;
- undergo legal, technical, and factual review;
- be approved by authorized personnel;
- be delivered securely and on time.

## Interviews and demonstrations

Prepare participants to explain actual practice, identify supporting evidence, avoid speculation, disclose limitations, and escalate questions beyond their expertise. Demonstrations should use controlled environments and preserve records of what was shown.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

An authority investigates whether GlobalWay’s fraud-detection system unfairly disadvantages travelers from a particular region. GlobalWay preserves the relevant model, data, logs, overrides, complaints, and vendor communications. It conducts subgroup testing, documents a data-quality issue, restricts the affected feature, and provides the authority with evidence, interim controls, and a remediation timeline.

## Control activities

- Maintain an investigation-response procedure.
- Authenticate requests and control deadlines.
- Preserve evidence promptly.
- Assign legal, technical, and executive accountability.
- Reconcile evidence to deployed versions.
- Review all submissions for accuracy and consistency.
- Track interviews, demonstrations, commitments, and remediation.
- Escalate evidence gaps and suspected misconduct.

## Evidence

- investigation-response plan;
- intake and authentication records;
- preservation notices;
- evidence inventory and chain of custody;
- request tracker;
- interview records;
- technical analyses;
- response drafts and approvals;
- submissions and correspondence;
- remediation and commitment logs;
- closure and lessons-learned reports.

## Audit tests

1. Review whether requests are authenticated and centrally tracked.
2. Verify preservation actions were timely and complete.
3. Trace selected statements to supporting evidence.
4. Confirm system versions were reconciled.
5. Review interview and demonstration controls.
6. Inspect known gaps for transparent disclosure and remediation.
7. Confirm commitments were completed and independently validated.

## Metrics

- response deadlines missed;
- preservation delays;
- evidence gaps;
- corrected or inconsistent submissions;
- unresolved investigation commitments;
- time to restrict affected functionality;
- repeat investigation issues;
- personnel trained for investigation response.

## Management checklist

- Can we preserve the right evidence immediately?
- Are legal and technical responsibilities clear?
- Do our answers match the deployed system and retained records?
- Are uncertainties and gaps disclosed honestly?
- Can we restrict or suspend the system when necessary?
- Are all commitments tracked through verified closure?
