## Chapter design requirements

Every chapter must explicitly distinguish: (a) FFIEC interagency examination guidance, (b) binding statutory or regulatory requirements, (c) member-agency-specific rules, orders, statements, or supervisory expectations, (d) institution-specific examination findings or commitments, and (e) organization implementation practice. FFIEC handbook material must never be represented as a standalone federal statute or as a substitute for OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC, NCUA, CFPB, state, or other applicable requirements.

Applicability depends on institution type, charter, regulator, services, technology, and risk profile. The controlled English master must not infer a legal or supervisory conclusion from generic facts and must not present an implementation example as a regulator-approved design.

## Evidence-object model

The controlled build should produce reusable evidence objects including: regulator/applicability matrix; board and management oversight record; information-security program charter; risk assessment; architecture inventory; asset register; access review; authentication-control record; data-protection decision; logging/monitoring evidence; vulnerability/patch evidence; secure-development record; cloud/emerging-technology risk assessment; third-party/TSP due-diligence record; outsourcing/concentration-risk analysis; incident record; regulatory-notification decision record; business-continuity plan and test record; recovery evidence; physical/environmental interface record; training evidence; independent test/audit evidence; examination evidence index; issue-remediation record; risk-acceptance record; metrics/reporting pack; release-source verification record.

## Localization and review boundary

Controlled es-419 and pt-BR localizations must preserve U.S. banking-regulatory terminology, institution/regulator distinctions, supervisory-vs-binding-law boundaries, evidence expectations, and non-legal/non-examination-opinion disclaimers. Legal/regulatory meaning, localization semantics, editorial quality, and exact-PDF rendered accessibility/visual review must be planned before final candidate reconciliation. Automated QA may support but must not replace any documented genuine-human judgment requirement.
