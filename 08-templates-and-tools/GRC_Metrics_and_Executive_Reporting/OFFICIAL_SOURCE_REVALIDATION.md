# Official Source Revalidation

**Review date:** 1 August 2026  
**Status:** PASS for the core baseline sources listed below

## NIST SP 800-55 Volume 1

- Official publisher: National Institute of Standards and Technology
- Current title: *Measurement Guide for Information Security: Volume 1 — Identifying and Selecting Measures*
- Status: Final
- Publication date: December 2024
- Supersession: Replaces NIST SP 800-55 Revision 1
- Review conclusion: Appropriate primary source for measure definition, selection, prioritization, qualitative and quantitative analysis, and usefulness evaluation.

## NIST SP 800-55 Volume 2

- Official publisher: National Institute of Standards and Technology
- Current title: *Measurement Guide for Information Security: Volume 2 — Developing an Information Security Measurement Program*
- Status: Final
- Publication date: December 2024
- Supersession: Replaces NIST SP 800-55 Revision 1
- Review conclusion: Appropriate primary source for measurement-program governance, implementation, communication, aggregation, review, and lifecycle management.

## ISO 31000:2018

- Official publisher: International Organization for Standardization
- Current title: *Risk management — Guidelines*
- Edition: 2
- Status: Published; last reviewed and confirmed in 2023 and still identified by ISO as current on the review date
- Development note: ISO/CD 31000 Edition 3 is under development. It is not a published replacement and is not treated as the current standard.
- Review conclusion: Appropriate baseline source for risk context, governance integration, monitoring, communication, and decision-making principles.

## Superseded-source safeguard

NIST SP 800-55 Revision 1 was withdrawn on 4 December 2024. It must not be represented as the current NIST measurement guide. Historical references may be retained only when clearly identified as superseded.

## Review boundary

This revalidation confirms publisher identity, publication status, supersession status, and high-level relevance. It does not reproduce copyrighted standards, establish legal or regulatory applicability, validate organization-specific formulas or thresholds, or provide audit assurance.
