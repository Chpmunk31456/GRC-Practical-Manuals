# Manual 11 — GDPR Privacy and Data Protection Controlled Implementation
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 25–32

> Original implementation and training guidance for governance, DPO, enforcement readiness, emerging technology, assurance, and continuous improvement.

## Chapter 25 — Retention, deletion, minimisation, and data quality

Define retention and deletion rules by purpose, legal need, business requirement, data category, system, processor, and evidence requirement. Implement defensible deletion, archival, legal-hold exceptions, and verification where appropriate.

Data minimisation and accuracy should be monitored throughout the lifecycle, including derived, duplicated, cached, analytics, backup, and AI-related data where relevant.

## Chapter 26 — DPO governance and independence

Where a Data Protection Officer is required or voluntarily appointed, define reporting lines, access to senior management, independence, resources, expertise, involvement in high-risk matters, conflict controls, and evidence of advice and escalation.

Do not treat appointment of a DPO as transfer of accountability away from controllers or processors.

## Chapter 27 — Training, awareness, and role competence

Provide privacy training proportional to role, data access, decision authority, and risk. Product, engineering, marketing, HR, procurement, security, incident response, customer support, legal/privacy, and executive roles require different practical competencies.

Track completion, role-specific content, recurring errors, lessons from incidents and rights cases, and whether training changes behavior where measurable.

## Chapter 28 — Supervisory-authority and enforcement readiness

Maintain an organized evidence package for significant processing decisions, notices, ROPA, DPIAs, processor contracts, transfer decisions, rights cases, incidents, security controls, DPO advice, training, governance, and remediation.

Regulatory engagement should be coordinated by accountable legal/privacy leadership and supported by accurate, preserved, reviewable evidence.

## Chapter 29 — AI, web scraping, and automated processing

Assess AI and data-intensive processing for purpose, lawful basis, transparency, data sourcing, minimisation, data-subject rights, profiling/automated-decision concerns, model inputs/outputs, retention, security, provenance, vendor relationships, and high-risk indicators.

Current EDPB work on generative-AI web scraping and other emerging topics should be monitored with clear draft-versus-final status controls.

## Chapter 30 — Anonymisation, pseudonymisation, research, and novel technology

Evaluate anonymisation claims in context and distinguish them from pseudonymisation. For research, blockchain, synthetic data, privacy-enhancing technologies, and novel architectures, document purpose, identifiability, reversibility, data linkage, governance, rights implications, security, and specialist review.

Emerging-technology labels do not change the need to analyze actual processing and risk.

## Chapter 31 — Privacy assurance, metrics, and corrective action

Assess whether privacy governance and controls are designed appropriately, implemented, operating, evidenced, and improved. Use metrics tied to meaningful outcomes such as rights performance, stale ROPA records, unresolved DPIA actions, processor evidence, retention execution, incident quality, and recurring control failures.

Formal legal opinions, certifications, or independent assurance conclusions require competent professionals and sufficient appropriate evidence.

## Chapter 32 — Manual release and continuous-improvement boundary

Manual 11 closes only when authoritative-source verification, the controlled 32-chapter English master, technical/privacy/legal editorial review, `es-419` and `pt-BR` localization with human semantic review, graphics/accessibility review, DOCX/PDF publication QA, repository/workflow security review, manifest/checksums/provenance, exact-head changed-scope reconciliation, and Final Human Release Approval are complete for the same exact candidate.

Completion establishes a reviewed training and implementation baseline. It does not provide organization-specific legal advice, guarantee GDPR compliance, predict enforcement outcomes, or replace competent privacy/legal professionals or supervisory authorities.
