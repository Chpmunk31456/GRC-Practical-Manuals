**State:** authoritative-source gate prepared; publication state unchanged

## Current federal baseline

As of 2026-08-30, the controlled federal private-sector baseline remains the **Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA)**, overseen by the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC). The OPC's current 2025–2026 reporting continues to describe and enforce PIPEDA as Canada's federal private-sector privacy law.

For breach controls under PIPEDA, the controlled implementation must preserve the statutory **real risk of significant harm (RROSH)** threshold. Organizations subject to PIPEDA report qualifying breaches to the OPC and notify affected individuals as soon as feasible after determining the breach meets that threshold; breach records must also be maintained. Sensitivity of the information and probability of misuse are core RROSH factors.

## Applicability and source boundaries

Manual 42 must not present Canada as a single uniform private-sector privacy regime. The controlled architecture must separately determine:

1. whether PIPEDA applies to the organization/activity;
2. whether a substantially similar provincial private-sector privacy statute applies instead or alongside federal rules;
3. whether sector-specific or health-information legislation changes the control obligation;
4. whether the activity involves interprovincial or international personal-information flows that engage the federal regime;
5. whether a statement is binding legislation/regulation, regulator guidance, investigation finding, or organization-specific implementation practice.

Provincial law must be verified from the responsible legislature/regulator before a province-specific requirement is represented as binding. The manual must not generalize Quebec, Alberta, British Columbia, health-sector, public-sector, or employment-specific requirements across Canada.

## Controlled breach boundary

The manual must distinguish:

- breach detection and containment;
- RROSH assessment;
- OPC reporting;
- affected-individual notification;
- notification to another organization or government institution where applicable for harm mitigation;
- breach-record retention;
- provincial/sector-specific notification overlays.

No fixed universal Canadian breach deadline may be asserted where the controlling law instead uses an `as soon as feasible` standard or where a provincial/sector rule differs.

## Current-source evidence

Authoritative/current OPC materials checked 2026-08-30:

- OPC home and mandate materials: the OPC oversees the Privacy Act and PIPEDA and continues to identify PIPEDA as the federal private-sector privacy law.

- OPC parliamentary issue material: PIPEDA section 10.1(1) requires reporting when a breach creates a real risk of significant harm and reporting is required as soon as feasible after that determination.
- OPC PIPEDA compliance-help materials updated in August 2026 continue to provide current incident-management and compliance guidance.
