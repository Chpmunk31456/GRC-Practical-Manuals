# Manual 10 — NIST RMF and SP 800-53 Controlled Implementation
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 17–24

> Original implementation guidance for ASSESS and AUTHORIZE. Assessment evidence must support accountable risk decisions and must not be represented as automatic authorization.

## Chapter 17 — Assessment planning

Define assessment scope, objectives, selected controls, procedures, evidence sources, independence expectations, sampling, test environments, stakeholders, schedules, and limitations before fieldwork begins.

The plan should distinguish design, implementation, and operating-effectiveness questions and identify where inherited or common controls require separate evidence.

## Chapter 18 — Evidence sufficiency and quality

Evaluate evidence for relevance, reliability, currency, completeness, scope, provenance, and applicability to the control implementation being assessed. A valid artifact may still be insufficient if it covers the wrong environment, period, service, or responsibility boundary.

Document assumptions, unavailable evidence, contradictory evidence, and uncertainty rather than concealing them behind pass/fail labels.

## Chapter 19 — Assessment procedures and testing

Use appropriate examination, interview, and test procedures to determine whether controls are implemented correctly, operating as intended, and producing the desired outcome. Tailor procedures to technology, risk, system context, and available evidence.

Automated testing can improve coverage and repeatability, but assessor judgment remains necessary for interpretation, scope, limitations, and significance.

## Chapter 20 — Findings, weaknesses, and severity

Record findings with affected controls, system scope, evidence, condition, expected state, risk consequence, root cause where known, severity rationale, owner, remediation path, and validation requirements.

Do not reduce findings to checklist exceptions without explaining material risk or implementation impact.

## Chapter 21 — Assessment report and risk package

Prepare an assessment package that distinguishes tested scope, procedures performed, evidence reviewed, findings, limitations, inherited-control dependencies, unresolved questions, and assessor conclusions.

The package should support risk decisions without overstating assurance beyond the evidence collected.

## Chapter 22 — Authorization package

Assemble the information required for an accountable authorization decision, including system plans, control implementation evidence, assessment results, risk assessments, POA&M items, exceptions, common-control dependencies, residual-risk statements, and relevant operational constraints.

The package should expose material uncertainty and open risk rather than optimize for a clean appearance.

## Chapter 23 — Authorization decision and conditions

The authorizing official or equivalent accountable authority decides whether residual risk is acceptable for the defined mission, scope, environment, and period. Decisions may authorize, authorize with conditions, require remediation, restrict operation, defer, or deny authorization.

Record decision authority, date, scope, evidence considered, material risk, conditions, expiration or review triggers, and required follow-up.

## Chapter 24 — ASSESS/AUTHORIZE fail-closed gate

Do not treat a system as ready for authorization when assessment scope is materially incomplete, significant findings are unsupported or unresolved, evidence quality is inadequate, authorization responsibility is unclear, or residual risk is hidden from the decision-maker.

Automation may support assessment and package assembly, but no automated workflow may substitute for competent assessment judgment or the accountable human authorization decision.
