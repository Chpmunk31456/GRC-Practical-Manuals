# Chapter 134 — GPAI Readiness Roadmap

## Purpose

This chapter provides a practical roadmap for organizations developing, supplying, integrating, or deploying general-purpose AI models and systems.

AI may support human decision-making, but it must not remove human responsibility, judgment, or accountability.

## Requirement

Organizations should determine their role in the GPAI value chain, identify applicable obligations, maintain required technical and governance documentation, manage copyright and training-data transparency requirements, support downstream providers, assess systemic-risk implications where relevant, and operate post-market monitoring and incident processes.

## Plain-language explanation

GPAI readiness depends on role and use. A model provider, downstream system provider, enterprise deployer, and distributor may face different responsibilities. The organization must understand what it provides, how it modifies or integrates the model, whether it places products under its own name, and whether changes create additional obligations.

## Phase 1: map the GPAI value chain

Document:

- model and system names, versions, and providers;
- provider, downstream provider, deployer, importer, and distributor roles;
- open-source status and applicable limitations;
- jurisdictions and sectors;
- fine-tuning, retrieval, tool use, agents, and system integrations;
- branding, resale, and substantial-modification scenarios;
- users, affected persons, and critical business processes.

## Phase 2: determine applicability

Assess:

- whether the artifact is a GPAI model or an AI system using GPAI;
- which entity places the model or system on the EU market;
- whether an open-source exception is relevant;
- whether systemic-risk classification or designation applies;
- whether downstream high-risk or transparency obligations also apply;
- whether contractual arrangements accurately allocate responsibilities.

## Phase 3: establish documentation and information controls

Maintain proportionate:

- technical model and system documentation;
- version, capability, limitation, and evaluation records;
- integration and configuration documentation;
- information for downstream providers;
- copyright-compliance policy and implementation evidence;
- required training-content summaries;
- security, incident, and change records;
- contacts and response procedures for authorities and partners.

## Phase 4: evaluate and control risk

Address:

- model capability and misuse risk;
- hallucination and unreliable output;
- privacy and confidential-data leakage;
- prompt injection and tool abuse;
- cybersecurity and supply-chain exposure;
- bias and discriminatory outcomes;
- intellectual-property and content risk;
- autonomy and agentic action risk;
- concentration, continuity, and vendor dependency;
- downstream use in high-risk contexts.

## Phase 5: systemic-risk readiness where applicable

For GPAI models with systemic risk, prepare enhanced processes for:

- standardized model evaluations;
- adversarial testing;
- systemic-risk assessment and mitigation;
- incident tracking and reporting;
- cybersecurity protection;
- energy-use and resource information where applicable;
- cooperation with the AI Office and relevant authorities;
- independent challenge and executive oversight.

## Phase 6: downstream integration readiness

Before enterprise deployment:

- validate model and provider information;
- assess intended and foreseeable uses;
- define prohibited uses and technical restrictions;
- test prompts, retrieval, tools, and human escalation;
- implement logging and monitoring;
- establish version and change controls;
- verify transparency notices;
- assess high-risk and sector overlays;
- create vendor exit and continuity plans.

## GlobalWay Travel Services example

GlobalWay integrates a third-party GPAI model into its travel-consultant assistant. It documents its deployer and system-provider roles, evaluates the provider’s technical information, restricts use of sensitive traveler data, tests retrieval and tool calls, implements human approval for refunds and safety decisions, monitors model-version changes, and reassesses obligations before offering the assistant under GlobalWay branding to clients.

## Control activities

- Maintain a GPAI model and integration register.
- Document role and applicability assessments.
- Obtain and review provider documentation.
- Implement copyright, security, privacy, and transparency controls.
- Validate downstream integrations and high-impact use cases.
- Monitor model, vendor, and legal changes.
- Establish systemic-risk controls when applicable.
- Retain evidence supporting decisions and disclosures.

## Evidence

- GPAI inventory;
- role and applicability assessments;
- provider documentation;
- model and integration specifications;
- copyright policy and training-content summary evidence;
- evaluation and red-team results;
- risk assessments;
- contracts and downstream information;
- monitoring and incident records;
- systemic-risk documentation where applicable;
- change and release approvals.

## Audit tests

1. Verify GPAI models and integrations are completely inventoried.
2. Review role determinations and open-source assumptions.
3. Confirm required provider and downstream documentation is maintained.
4. Test copyright, security, privacy, and transparency controls.
5. Review evaluations for misuse, bias, reliability, and tool-action risk.
6. Confirm model-version changes trigger reassessment.
7. Verify enhanced systemic-risk controls where applicable.

## Metrics

- GPAI models and integrations inventoried;
- role assessments current;
- provider documentation gaps;
- high-impact integrations validated;
- model changes awaiting review;
- red-team and evaluation findings;
- incidents and misuse events;
- downstream information requests overdue;
- systemic-risk actions overdue.

## Management checklist

- Do we know every GPAI model and integration in use?
- Are our legal and supply-chain roles clear?
- Do we have reliable provider and technical documentation?
- Are copyright, privacy, security, and transparency controls operating?
- Have high-impact and agentic uses been technically validated?
- Do model and provider changes trigger timely reassessment?
