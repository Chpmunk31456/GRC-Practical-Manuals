# Manual 50 — Agentic AI Governance, Security, Identity & Human Accountability

**Status:** PRE-STAGE — SHARED CONTROLS/SECURITY LANE  
**Source baseline:** 31 August 2026

## Objective

Govern AI agents as actors capable of taking actions, using tools, accessing data, delegating tasks and changing system state—not merely as models that generate content.

## Controlled architecture

1. Agentic AI concepts and system boundaries.
2. Capability and autonomy classification.
3. Intended-purpose and prohibited-action definitions.
4. Agent identity, authentication and attribution.
5. Least privilege and authorization.
6. Tool/API allowlisting and capability control.
7. Credential, token and secret isolation.
8. Data-access boundaries and privacy.
9. Human approval checkpoints and meaningful accountability.
10. Separation of duties and dual-control patterns.
11. Transaction, cost, rate and resource limits.
12. Memory governance and retention.
13. Prompt/context and instruction-integrity controls.
14. Agent-to-agent delegation and trust boundaries.
15. Multi-agent orchestration risk.
16. Testing, simulation, red teaming and adversarial evaluation.
17. Runtime monitoring and behavioral anomaly detection.
18. Immutable/adequate audit trails and action provenance.
19. Emergency stop, suspension and containment.
20. Incident response and forensic readiness.
21. Change management and capability revalidation.
22. Third-party agent/provider governance.
23. Singapore Agentic AI MGF operational mapping.
24. NIST/ISO/EU relationship mapping.
25. Executive risk acceptance and board reporting.
26. Interview case studies and tabletop exercises.

## Core control proposition

For agentic systems, governance must answer five questions continuously:

1. **Who/what is the agent?**
2. **What is it permitted to do?**
3. **What data, tools and systems can it access?**
4. **Which actions require human authorization?**
5. **Can the organization reconstruct, contain and stop its actions?**
