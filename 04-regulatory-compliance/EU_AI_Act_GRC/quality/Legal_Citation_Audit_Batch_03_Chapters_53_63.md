# Legal and Citation Audit — Batch 03

**Scope:** Chapters 53–63 — general-purpose AI models, systemic-risk models, codes of practice, and related transparency governance.

**Status:** Initial authoritative-source review completed; direct chapter corrections and second-pass verification remain open.

## Controlling sources

1. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended.
2. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 where amendments affect timing, scope, or transition.
3. Current consolidated EUR-Lex text.
4. Official European Commission and EU AI Office GPAI guidance.
5. General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, identified as a voluntary compliance instrument rather than binding legislation.

## High-priority findings

### GPAI actor and scope precision

Chapters 53–55 must distinguish the provider of a GPAI model from providers and deployers of downstream AI systems. Model-level obligations do not automatically replace system-level duties, and one organization may hold multiple roles.

### Documentation and downstream information

Chapter 54 must map the provider’s technical-documentation and downstream-information duties to the applicable statutory provisions and clarify confidentiality, intellectual-property, and security safeguards without using them as a blanket basis to withhold required information.

### Copyright policy and training-content summary

Chapter 55 must distinguish the obligation to maintain a policy for compliance with Union copyright law from the obligation to publish a sufficiently detailed summary of training content. The manual must not imply that publication of complete datasets, copyrighted works, trade secrets, or personal data is required.

### Systemic-risk classification

Chapter 56 must distinguish automatic classification based on the statutory computational threshold from Commission designation based on equivalent capabilities or impact. Classification evidence must be version-specific and reassessed after material changes.

### Evaluations, adversarial testing, and systemic-risk mitigation

Chapters 57–59 must separate model evaluation, adversarial testing, systemic-risk assessment, mitigation, serious-incident handling, and cybersecurity obligations. The manual should avoid treating one red-team exercise as sufficient evidence for all duties.

### Energy and resource reporting

Chapter 60 must cite the exact model-level information obligations and avoid unsupported claims that every downstream deployer must independently calculate training energy use.

### Open-source treatment

Chapter 61 must state that open-source treatment is conditional and does not categorically remove obligations, especially for GPAI models with systemic risk and where statutory conditions are not met.

### Codes of practice

Chapters 62–63 must clearly label codes of practice as voluntary compliance pathways and implementation support. They must not be presented as legislation or as the sole lawful means of compliance.

### Timing and transitional rules

Every GPAI chapter must distinguish:

- obligations already applying to models placed on the market after the relevant date;
- transitional compliance for models placed on the market earlier;
- Commission enforcement timing;
- any amendment introduced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.
