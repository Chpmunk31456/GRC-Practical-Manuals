# Chapter 135 — Transparency Readiness Roadmap

> **Legal status:** Corrected English master for consolidation. This file controls over conflicting earlier Chapter 135 draft language.

## Requirement

Organizations must identify which AI transparency duties apply to each system, actor, use case, output, and deployment context, then implement controls so required notices, disclosures, markings, and instructions are accurate, timely, accessible, understandable, and consistent with actual system behaviour.

## Plain-English explanation

Transparency duties differ by role and use. They may include informing people that they are interacting with AI, marking AI-generated or manipulated content, disclosing deepfakes or certain public-interest text, informing exposed persons about emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation systems, and providing instructions for high-risk systems. A roadmap should translate those legal triggers into concrete implementation work.

## Roadmap requirements

The transparency-readiness programme should:

1. map systems and use cases to applicable Article 13, Article 26, Article 50, and related duties;
2. distinguish provider, deployer, importer, distributor, and product-manufacturer responsibilities;
3. document exceptions, exclusions, and effective dates;
4. define notice content, timing, channel, language, accessibility, and ownership;
5. implement machine-readable marking where legally required and technically feasible;
6. align notices with actual system purpose, limitations, data use, human oversight, and complaint routes;
7. test comprehension, accessibility, placement, persistence, and technical effectiveness;
8. manage supplier evidence and downstream information;
9. monitor changes that alter transparency obligations;
10. retain version-linked evidence of approval, deployment, testing, and correction.

## GlobalWay example

GlobalWay maps its traveler chatbot, recruitment tool, synthetic marketing content, and call-centre emotion-analysis pilot. It implements chatbot disclosure, applicant notices, machine-readable marking for generated images, deepfake escalation rules, accessible multilingual notices, and a review gate for supplier changes.

## Control activity

No AI system subject to a transparency duty may be released until the applicable notice, disclosure, marking, or instruction has been legally reviewed, technically implemented, tested in the production context, and linked to the approved system version.

## Evidence

- transparency applicability matrix;
- legal-role and use-case analysis;
- approved notice and disclosure text;
- accessibility and comprehension testing;
- machine-readable marking test results;
- supplier documentation;
- deployment and version records;
- change and reassessment history;
- complaint and remediation records.

## Audit test

Select systems subject to transparency duties. Confirm that the correct legal trigger and actor were identified, required information was delivered at the right time and through an effective channel, notices matched actual operation, technical markings functioned as intended, and material changes triggered reassessment.
