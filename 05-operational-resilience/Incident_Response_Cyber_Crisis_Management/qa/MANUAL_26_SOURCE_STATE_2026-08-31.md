# Manual 26 — Incident Response & Cyber Crisis Management release-time source state

Verified release baseline as of 2026-08-31:

- NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3, *Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management: A CSF 2.0 Community Profile*, is final and was published in April 2025.
- NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 supersedes NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 2.
- The Rev. 3 model integrates incident response recommendations across the six NIST CSF 2.0 Functions rather than treating incident handling as an isolated lifecycle.
- NIST guidance is not a universal legal notification rule. Jurisdiction-specific breach, cyber-incident, sector, contractual, insurer, law-enforcement, and customer notification requirements remain distinct overlays and must be evaluated separately.
- CISA and other government/sector guidance may support implementation but must not be generalized into universally binding requirements absent a controlling legal or contractual source.

Controlled boundaries:

- Separate operational incident severity from legal or regulatory reportability determinations.
- Preserve evidence, chain of custody, legal/privacy coordination, and decision records proportionate to the incident and governing requirements.
- Preserve OT/ICS and safety-sensitive constraints where safety, reliability, or engineering authority controls the response sequence.
- Localized editions are unofficial project localizations and must not be described as NIST-authorized translations.
- Recheck NIST and applicable reporting/notification sources again at the final publication reconciliation boundary.

This source decision supports controlled localization and publication-candidate generation. Publication state remains unchanged until localization, artifact, provenance, accessibility, workflow-security, exact-staging, predecessor-order, and final release-state gates are satisfied.
