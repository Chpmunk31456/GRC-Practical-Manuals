# Manual 25 — ISO 22301 Business Continuity Management Controlled Implementation

## 01. Purpose, scope, and controlled-use boundary
Establish a practical BCMS implementation model without reproducing ISO normative text or implying certification, legal compliance, or audit opinion.

## 02. Source hierarchy and version control
Maintain a source register separating ISO 22301:2019, Amendment 1:2024, ISO 22313 guidance, internal policy, contractual requirements, regulatory overlays, and change-watch material.

## 03. Organizational context
Identify internal and external issues affecting continuity capability, including dependencies, operating environment, stakeholders, climate-related context where relevant, and strategic constraints.

## 04. Interested parties and obligations
Maintain a controlled inventory of customer, regulatory, contractual, workforce, supplier, and public-sector continuity expectations with owners and evidence.

## 05. BCMS scope and boundaries
Define organizational units, services, sites, technologies, suppliers, data, facilities, and exclusions within the BCMS boundary.

## 06. Leadership and accountability
Assign executive sponsorship, policy ownership, decision rights, escalation authority, funding responsibility, and evidence accountability.

## 07. Business continuity policy
Maintain an approved policy aligned to organizational objectives, scope, obligations, resilience expectations, and continual-improvement commitments.

## 08. Roles, responsibilities, and competence
Define accountable roles for business continuity, crisis leadership, service owners, technology recovery, facilities, communications, legal, HR, procurement, and assurance.

## 09. Planning for risks and opportunities
Identify BCMS-level risks and opportunities that could affect the management system's intended outcomes and define tracked treatments.

## 10. Business impact analysis governance
Establish BIA methodology, review cadence, data quality controls, approval authority, and linkage to service inventories and recovery strategies.

## 11. Critical products and services
Identify priority products, services, activities, and value streams, including acceptable outage tolerances and stakeholder impacts.

## 12. Impact criteria and time dimensions
Define financial, legal, safety, customer, operational, regulatory, reputational, and mission impact criteria across time-based disruption intervals.

## 13. Dependencies and resource requirements
Map people, facilities, technology, data, utilities, suppliers, logistics, communications, and specialist dependencies needed for prioritized activities.

## 14. Risk assessment for disruption
Assess disruption scenarios, vulnerabilities, single points of failure, concentration risk, geographic exposure, cyber/physical dependencies, and cascading effects.

## 15. Continuity strategy selection
Select proportionate strategies based on BIA and risk results, balancing resilience, recovery capability, cost, complexity, and residual risk.

## 16. People and workforce continuity
Define staffing alternatives, succession, cross-training, remote-work capability, welfare, travel, access, safety, and workforce communication controls.

## 17. Facilities and physical resilience
Address alternate workspace, utilities, environmental controls, physical security, site access, geographic diversification, and facility recovery dependencies.

## 18. Technology and cyber recovery integration
Integrate BCMS objectives with disaster recovery, backup, restoration, cyber incident response, identity recovery, infrastructure resilience, and application dependencies.

## 19. Information and data continuity
Protect availability, integrity, recoverability, retention, confidentiality, and controlled access to information required during disruption and recovery.

## 20. Third-party and supply-chain continuity
Define supplier continuity requirements, due diligence, evidence, concentration-risk review, alternate sourcing, escalation, contract clauses, and reassessment.

## 21. Crisis management structure
Define strategic, tactical, and operational crisis roles, activation criteria, command structure, decision logging, handoffs, and executive escalation.

## 22. Incident and continuity response procedures
Maintain usable procedures for activation, stabilization, continuity, recovery, escalation, resource coordination, and stand-down.

## 23. Communications and stakeholder management
Define internal and external communications, spokesperson authority, message approval, regulator/customer notifications, contact maintenance, and rumor control.

## 24. Recovery objectives and restoration priorities
Translate approved business tolerances into operational recovery objectives, sequencing, minimum resource needs, and validated service restoration priorities.

## 25. Plan documentation and accessibility
Ensure plans are current, controlled, available under degraded conditions, appropriately protected, versioned, and understandable to intended users.

## 26. Training and awareness
Implement role-based awareness, competence development, onboarding, refresher training, crisis-leadership preparation, and evidence of completion.

## 27. Exercise and testing program
Maintain a risk-based exercise program using walkthroughs, simulations, technical recovery tests, supplier exercises, crisis exercises, and integrated scenarios.

## 28. Exercise findings and remediation
Record observations, root causes, action owners, due dates, residual risk, retest requirements, and closure evidence for exercise and incident findings.

## 29. Monitoring, measurement, and metrics
Define BCMS indicators for readiness, plan currency, recovery performance, exercise effectiveness, supplier resilience, issue aging, and objective achievement.

## 30. Internal audit and assurance
Plan independent, evidence-based assurance over BCMS design and operation, preserving auditor competence, scope, sampling, findings, and follow-up boundaries.

## 31. Management review and continual improvement
Provide leadership with evidence on performance, changes, incidents, exercises, audit results, risks, resources, objectives, and improvement actions.

## 32. Release, evidence, and implementation roadmap
Package an Essential / Structured / Enhanced implementation roadmap with source verification, control/evidence mapping, issue tracking, localization, accessibility, provenance, checksum, workflow-security, and release gates. Recheck ISO source status immediately before publication and treat ISO/CD 22301 as non-normative change-watch unless and until formally published.
