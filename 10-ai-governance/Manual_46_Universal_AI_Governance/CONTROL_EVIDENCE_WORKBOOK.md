# Manual 46 — Universal AI Governance Foundation
## Control and Evidence Workbook

**Status:** CONTROLLED DEVELOPMENT

This workbook converts the universal AI-governance model into operational control design and assurance evidence.

## 1. Control record template

For each control, record:
- Control ID
- Risk or requirement addressed
- Control objective
- Control activity
- Accountable owner
- Operator
- Trigger or frequency
- Systems/use cases in scope
- Evidence generated
- Test procedure
- Exception process
- Remediation owner/date
- Residual-risk decision

## 2. Minimum universal control domains

### GOV-01 — AI accountability
Objective: every governed AI use case has an accountable business owner and technical owner.
Evidence: inventory record, ownership acceptance, committee escalation path.

### INV-01 — AI inventory completeness
Objective: production and material pre-production AI systems are recorded in a governed inventory.
Evidence: inventory extract, reconciliation results, shadow-AI discovery records.

### CLS-01 — Risk and autonomy classification
Objective: governance intensity is determined from documented risk, impact and autonomy criteria.
Evidence: classification worksheet, tier decision, approver.

### RSK-01 — AI risk/impact assessment
Objective: material risks are identified, evaluated, treated and accepted before deployment.
Evidence: risk assessment, impact assessment, treatment plan, residual-risk acceptance.

### DAT-01 — Data and knowledge governance
Objective: AI data and knowledge sources are authorized, traceable and appropriate for intended use.
Evidence: lineage, provenance, source approvals, retention rules, RAG corpus register.

### SEC-01 — AI security review
Objective: AI-specific and conventional security threats are identified and controlled.
Evidence: threat model, architecture review, test results, remediation records.

### PRV-01 — Privacy assessment
Objective: personal and sensitive data use is reviewed and controlled according to applicable requirements.
Evidence: privacy assessment/DPIA where applicable, data-flow map, minimization and retention decisions.

### HUM-01 — Meaningful human oversight
Objective: required human intervention is practical, authorized and testable.
Evidence: oversight procedure, reviewer authority, training, override logs, stop/suspend procedure.

### VAL-01 — Testing and validation
Objective: the system is validated for intended use and foreseeable material failure modes before approval.
Evidence: test plan, validation report, findings, independent challenge where required.

### APP-01 — Deployment approval
Objective: production deployment occurs only after required governance gates are satisfied.
Evidence: approval record, conditions, exceptions, residual-risk decision.

### TPR-01 — Third-party AI governance
Objective: provider controls and dependencies are assessed and contractually governed.
Evidence: due diligence, assurance reports, contract clauses, provider change/incident obligations.

### MON-01 — Continuous monitoring
Objective: performance and risk indicators are monitored against defined thresholds.
Evidence: dashboards, alert logs, threshold definitions, escalations.

### CHG-01 — Material change and revalidation
Objective: material changes trigger proportionate reassessment and revalidation.
Evidence: change record, materiality assessment, regression/validation results, reapproval.

### INC-01 — AI incident management
Objective: AI incidents are detected, contained, investigated, escalated and remediated.
Evidence: incident record, preserved logs, impact assessment, notifications, corrective actions.

### AUD-01 — Governance evidence and assurance
Objective: AI governance decisions and control operation are independently testable.
Evidence: audit trail, control test results, findings, remediation and assurance reports.

### RET-01 — Retirement and decommissioning
Objective: retired AI systems are disabled, dependencies removed, data handled appropriately and evidence retained.
Evidence: retirement approval, access removal, archive/retention record, inventory closure.

## 3. Control design quality test

A control is not complete merely because a policy says it should happen. A testable control identifies:
**who does what, when, using what criteria, producing what evidence, and what happens when the result fails.**

## 4. Evidence sufficiency test

Evidence should support four questions:
1. Was the control designed appropriately?
2. Did it operate when required?
3. Was the result reviewed and exceptions handled?
4. Can an independent reviewer reconstruct the decision?

## 5. Exception record

Each exception should state:
- requested deviation;
- affected system/use case;
- reason;
- risk created;
- compensating controls;
- approving authority;
- expiration date;
- monitoring requirements;
- remediation/closure condition.

Open-ended exceptions without expiration or review are a governance anti-pattern.

## 6. Residual-risk acceptance

Residual-risk acceptance should identify the specific residual risk, affected business objective, evidence considered, risk owner, authority level, acceptance period and reassessment trigger.

## 7. Assurance test worksheet

For each sampled AI system:
1. Verify inventory completeness.
2. Verify ownership.
3. Reperform classification logic.
4. Confirm required assessments occurred.
5. Trace material risks to controls.
6. Inspect validation evidence.
7. Confirm approval authority.
8. Inspect monitoring evidence.
9. Inspect material changes and revalidation.
10. Inspect incidents/exceptions.
11. Confirm third-party obligations where applicable.
12. Confirm evidence retention and auditability.

## 8. Executive evidence pack

A concise executive evidence pack should include:
- inventory by risk tier;
- material/high-impact systems;
- approval/validation status;
- residual-risk acceptances;
- exceptions nearing expiry;
- unresolved high-severity findings;
- AI incidents and trends;
- third-party concentration;
- overdue revalidation;
- key risk indicators against thresholds.

## 9. Completion criterion

The learner should be able to take any AI use case and produce a traceable chain from business purpose through risk, controls, evidence, monitoring and residual-risk decision.
