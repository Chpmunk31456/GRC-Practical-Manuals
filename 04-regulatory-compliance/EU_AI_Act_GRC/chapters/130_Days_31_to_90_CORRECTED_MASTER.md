# Chapter 130 — Days 31–90

> **Legal status:** Corrected English master for consolidation. This chapter is an implementation roadmap, not a statutory timetable. It controls over conflicting earlier Chapter 130 language.

## Requirement

Organizations should use days 31–90 to convert initial AI triage into repeatable governance, risk assessment, control operation, remediation, training, and management reporting. The sequence must be adjusted to the organization’s legal roles, applicable effective dates, existing control maturity, and risk profile.

## Plain-English explanation

The EU AI Act does not require organizations to complete the activities in this chapter within 90 days. The roadmap is a recommended implementation method. Binding duties arise from the Act, as amended, and from other applicable law—not from this internal schedule.

## Days 31–45: validate scope and accountability

1. reconcile the AI inventory with procurement, cloud, software, data, security, HR, and business records;
2. confirm legal entities, systems, versions, intended purposes, owners, suppliers, jurisdictions, and affected populations;
3. document provider, deployer, importer, distributor, authorised-representative, and product-manufacturer roles where relevant;
4. establish recurring inventory attestations and quality checks;
5. restrict, suspend, or escalate ownerless and undocumented systems according to risk.

## Days 46–60: assess and classify

Perform proportionate assessments covering applicability, prohibited practices, high-risk classification, transparency duties, GPAI dependencies, privacy, fundamental rights, cybersecurity, resilience, human oversight, third-party risk, and substantial modification. Record uncertainty and require qualified legal or specialist review where conclusions are disputed.

## Days 61–75: establish baseline controls

Implement risk-based controls for intake, approval, inventory, data governance, documentation, validation, transparency, oversight, logging, monitoring, incidents, supplier governance, change management, evidence retention, exceptions, and residual-risk acceptance. Integrate them into existing business and technology workflows.

## Days 76–90: train, test, and report

Launch role-based AI-literacy activities, define risk and control indicators, begin control self-assessments, test selected high-priority controls, establish issue severity and remediation standards, and approve an assurance and resource plan.

## GlobalWay example

GlobalWay reconciles its inventory and identifies embedded AI features that were not captured during initial triage. It completes role and risk assessments for critical systems, establishes intake and release gates, trains accountable personnel, and begins monthly executive reporting. Its recruitment system remains restricted until bias, oversight, privacy, and supplier-evidence gaps are resolved.

## Control activity

Management must approve the roadmap, assign accountable owners, prioritize binding obligations and material risks, document deviations from the schedule, and prevent roadmap milestones from being represented as statutory deadlines.

## Evidence

- validated inventory and ownership records;
- role, applicability, and classification assessments;
- control library and workflow approvals;
- training and competence records;
- issue and remediation register;
- dashboards and governance minutes;
- approved assurance and resource plan.

## Audit test

Confirm that the organization distinguished statutory deadlines from internal milestones, validated inventory completeness, supported role and classification decisions, embedded controls into actual workflows, trained relevant personnel, and assigned accountable remediation for material gaps.
