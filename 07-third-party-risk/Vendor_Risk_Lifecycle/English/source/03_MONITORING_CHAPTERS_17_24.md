# Manual 08 — Vendor and Third-Party Risk Lifecycle
## Controlled English Source — Chapters 17–24

> Original third-party-risk implementation guidance. This material supports controlled TPRM operation and does not certify suppliers.

## Chapter 17 — Onboarding controls

Onboarding should verify that approved contract terms, access boundaries, data-handling requirements, security contacts, incident contacts, monitoring tier, and service ownership are implemented before production use.

## Chapter 18 — Continuous monitoring

Monitoring should focus on indicators linked to material risk: security events, service degradation, financial distress, ownership changes, compliance changes, subcontractor changes, model or platform changes, unresolved findings, and contract obligations.

## Chapter 19 — Evidence refresh

Evidence should be refreshed according to risk and expiration. Independent reports, certifications, policies, test evidence, insurance, privacy documents, and resilience artifacts should not remain credited indefinitely after they become stale.

## Chapter 20 — Change management

Material vendor changes should trigger impact analysis. Examples include new subprocessors, new hosting regions, architecture changes, changes in data use, mergers, service-model changes, AI/model changes, or changes to security or privacy commitments.

## Chapter 21 — Issue and remediation governance

Findings should have owners, severity, actions, dates, evidence, and escalation. Repeated missed remediation dates should affect risk treatment and may require service restrictions, additional controls, executive acceptance, or exit planning.

## Chapter 22 — Exception management

Exceptions should be time-bound and approved by appropriate authority. Monitoring should verify that compensating controls remain effective and that expiration dates are not bypassed through administrative renewal without reassessment.

## Chapter 23 — Periodic reassessment

Reassessment should consider current service scope, criticality, data use, control evidence, incidents, supplier changes, performance, concentration, fourth-party exposure, contract status, and exit readiness.

## Chapter 24 — Renewal decision

Renewal should be a risk decision, not an automatic procurement event. The approval record should identify unresolved findings, changed exposure, current evidence, residual risk, and whether contract terms or monitoring need to change.
