# Manual 27 — Data Governance & Privacy Engineering Controlled Implementation

**Controlled English master — release candidate source**  
**Series order:** 27  
**Published NIST framework baseline:** NIST Privacy Framework 1.0  
**Change-watch:** NIST Privacy Framework 1.1 Initial Public Draft / forthcoming final  
**Privacy-engineering reference:** NISTIR 8062  
**Boundary:** This manual is implementation guidance, not legal advice, regulator approval, certification, or a substitute for jurisdiction-specific law.

## 01. Purpose, scope, and boundaries
Define enterprise data-governance and privacy-engineering practices without conflating guidance with jurisdiction-specific legal obligations.

## 02. Source hierarchy and change control
Separate laws/regulations, regulator guidance, NIST Privacy Framework, privacy-engineering guidance, contracts, policy, and internal standards.

## 03. Data governance operating model
Define councils, stewards, custodians, owners, privacy, security, legal, records, architecture, and business-accountability roles.

## 04. Data inventory and lineage
Maintain authoritative inventories of data domains, systems, flows, stores, processors, transfers, and lineage relationships.

## 05. Classification and handling
Establish classification criteria, handling rules, access conditions, sharing limits, protection requirements, and evidence.

## 06. Purpose specification and use limitation
Record approved business purposes, compatible uses, restrictions, owners, review triggers, and exceptions.

## 07. Data minimization
Collect, create, retain, and expose only data proportionate to defined purposes and obligations.

## 08. Data quality and accuracy
Define quality dimensions, validation, correction, ownership, monitoring, and issue-management controls.

## 09. Retention and deletion
Implement retention schedules, legal holds, deletion, archival, disposal, exceptions, and evidence of execution.

## 10. Access governance
Apply least privilege, role/attribute-based controls, privileged access, periodic review, segregation, and revocation.

## 11. Data sharing and disclosure
Control internal and external sharing through authorization, agreements, purpose restrictions, minimization, logging, and review.

## 12. Cross-border and jurisdictional data flows
Maintain legal/contractual transfer assessments and avoid assuming a single global transfer mechanism applies everywhere.

## 13. Privacy risk assessment
Identify privacy harms, affected individuals, likelihood, severity, mitigations, residual risk, and reassessment triggers.

## 14. Privacy engineering objectives
Translate privacy goals into system properties such as predictability, manageability, disassociability, control, and transparency.

## 15. Privacy by design and default
Embed privacy requirements into architecture, SDLC, procurement, change management, and default configurations.

## 16. Consent and preference management
Where applicable, govern notices, choices, consent state, withdrawal, preference propagation, evidence, and exceptions.

## 17. Individual rights operations
Support access, correction, deletion, portability, objection, restriction, and other applicable rights with jurisdiction-aware workflows.

## 18. Identity and sensitive-data protection
Apply stronger governance for identifiers, credentials, health, financial, biometric, precise-location, children’s, and similarly sensitive data as applicable.

## 19. De-identification and pseudonymization
Define approved techniques, re-identification risk assessment, controls, testing, contracts, and use restrictions.

## 20. Privacy-enhancing technologies
Evaluate encryption, tokenization, secure computation, differential privacy, federated techniques, synthetic data, and other PETs proportionate to risk.

## 21. Logging, monitoring, and observability
Log access, sharing, deletion, consent state, rights requests, policy exceptions, and material data-flow changes.

## 22. Third-party data governance
Assess processors/vendors, instructions, sub-processing, evidence, security/privacy controls, retention, deletion, and incident obligations.

## 23. Cloud and data-platform governance
Govern data lakes, warehouses, SaaS, analytics platforms, object stores, APIs, notebooks, and cross-account sharing.

## 24. Analytics and secondary use
Review new analytics, enrichment, inference, profiling, and reuse for purpose compatibility, privacy risk, fairness, and transparency.

## 25. Records and evidence architecture
Maintain decisions, assessments, approvals, mappings, exceptions, notices, contracts, logs, issue records, and remediation evidence.

## 26. Incident and breach coordination
Integrate data governance with security/privacy incident detection, containment, assessment, notification, recovery, and lessons learned.

## 27. Metrics and indicators
Track inventory coverage, data quality, rights performance, retention execution, access review, privacy risk, vendor issues, and remediation aging.

## 28. Training and role competence
Provide role-based training for owners, stewards, engineers, analysts, privacy, security, procurement, and leadership.

## 29. Assurance and testing
Test governance design and operation through sampling, control validation, technical testing, rights-request review, and evidence inspection.

## 30. Management review
Review performance, regulatory change, incidents, privacy risk, metrics, exceptions, resources, and improvement priorities.

## 31. Continual improvement
Use findings, incidents, complaints, audit results, technology change, and regulatory change to drive controlled improvements.

## 32. Release and implementation roadmap
Package Essential / Structured / Enhanced implementation paths with source verification, localization, accessibility, provenance, checksums, workflow-security, human-review evidence where required, and sequential release controls.
