# Review, Supersession, Retirement, and Assurance Boundaries

## 1. Maintain the register as a controlled record

Obligations and regulatory change records require periodic and event-driven review. Assign review frequency based on source volatility, materiality, jurisdiction, business change, enforcement exposure, and confidence in the interpretation.

## 2. Verify current status

At each review, confirm the official source, version, amendments, corrections, effective dates, guidance, implementing measures, judicial or administrative developments, and known enforcement changes. Record the date and reviewer rather than overwriting the prior history.

## 3. Manage supersession

When a source is amended or replaced:

- preserve the prior record and its effective period;
- link the successor source and affected obligations;
- identify unchanged, revised, added, and removed duties;
- reassess applicability, controls, evidence, contracts, and action plans;
- avoid closing the prior obligation until transition and retention needs are understood.

## 4. Retire obligations carefully

Retirement may be appropriate when a source expires, is withdrawn, no longer applies to the organization, or is replaced. Document the reason, authority, effective retirement date, residual duties, record-retention obligations, open investigations or claims, and approval.

## 5. Preserve historical evidence

Historical records may remain necessary for audits, litigation, regulator inquiries, contractual disputes, certifications, incident investigations, or proof of compliance during an earlier period. Apply approved retention and access controls.

## 6. Monitor exceptions and overdue actions

Reports should identify expired exceptions, overdue implementation tasks, unreviewed applicability decisions, stale evidence, unresolved conflicts, missing owners, approaching effective dates, and changes with inadequate resources.

## 7. Perform quality review

Quality review should test source traceability, date accuracy, duplicate records, unsupported applicability decisions, unclear ownership, incomplete control linkage, missing evidence expectations, overdue reviews, and closure without approval.

## 8. Separate management reporting from assurance

A register can support management oversight, but it is not an audit opinion. Automated validation can confirm file integrity, required fields, structural consistency, and certain status rules. It cannot determine legal meaning, applicability, implementation effectiveness, or compliance.

## 9. Disclose limitations

Reports should state the source cut-off date, jurisdictions covered, excluded domains, unresolved interpretations, machine-assisted translations, evidence limitations, and whether qualified legal or regulatory review occurred.

## 10. Continuous improvement

Use incidents, audit findings, regulator feedback, missed deadlines, duplicate effort, stale records, and user feedback to improve sources, workflows, field definitions, escalation thresholds, training, and governance.
