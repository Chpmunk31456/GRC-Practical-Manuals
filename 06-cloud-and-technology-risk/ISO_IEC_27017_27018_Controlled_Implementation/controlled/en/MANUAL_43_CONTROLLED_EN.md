# Manual 43 — ISO/IEC 27017 & 27018 Cloud Security and Privacy Controlled Implementation

**Controlled baseline:** ISO/IEC 27017:2026 Edition 2 and ISO/IEC 27018:2025 Edition 3, with ISO/IEC 27001/27002 and applicable privacy law treated as separate related layers.  
**Copyright boundary:** this manual contains project-authored summaries and implementation guidance only; it does not reproduce protected ISO control text and is not an authorized ISO translation.  
**Release rule:** reverify edition status immediately before candidate freeze.

## Chapter 01 — Source and copyright boundary
Maintain authoritative edition metadata, purchased-standard access controls where applicable, project-authored summaries, copyright restrictions, citation records, and release-time version checks.

## Chapter 02 — Scope and standard relationships
Define the scope of ISO/IEC 27017 cloud security guidance and ISO/IEC 27018 public-cloud PII processing guidance, and document their relationship to ISO/IEC 27001, 27002, 27701, contracts, and law.

## Chapter 03 — Cloud service models
Classify IaaS, PaaS, SaaS and related managed-service models, documenting which responsibilities remain with provider, customer, tenant administrators, integrators, and subprocessors.

## Chapter 04 — Customer and provider accountability
Assign accountable owners for security, privacy, service management, architecture, incident response, vendor oversight, evidence, and contractual obligations on both sides of the service relationship.

## Chapter 05 — Shared responsibility
Create service-specific responsibility matrices that map control ownership, configuration duties, evidence sources, dependencies, exceptions, escalation paths, and verification frequency.

## Chapter 06 — Information-security governance
Integrate cloud risks into policy, risk assessment, control design, change management, assurance, management review, corrective action, and continual improvement.

## Chapter 07 — Asset and configuration governance
Inventory cloud accounts, subscriptions, projects, workloads, data stores, images, secrets, configurations, regions, dependencies, and owners; maintain approved secure baselines and drift monitoring.

## Chapter 08 — Identity and access
Govern identity federation, account lifecycle, authentication, authorization, service identities, conditional access, segregation, periodic review, and evidence of revocation.

## Chapter 09 — Privileged cloud administration
Protect administrative roles through least privilege, strong authentication, privileged access workflows, just-in-time elevation, logging, break-glass controls, review, and separation of duties.

## Chapter 10 — Virtualized and shared environments
Assess isolation, multi-tenancy, hypervisor or container boundaries, shared services, noisy-neighbor risks, metadata services, tenant separation, and provider assurance evidence.

## Chapter 11 — Administrative operations
Control provider and customer operational procedures, maintenance, support access, automation, consoles, emergency changes, remote administration, and operational evidence.

## Chapter 12 — Network and cloud architecture
Design segmentation, ingress/egress control, private connectivity, gateways, service endpoints, DNS, firewalls, load balancers, exposure management, and architecture review.

## Chapter 13 — Logging and monitoring
Define required cloud audit logs, workload telemetry, administrative events, security alerts, retention, time synchronization, integrity, centralized analysis, and investigation access.

## Chapter 14 — Cryptography and key management
Define encryption requirements, key ownership, provider-managed versus customer-managed keys, rotation, backup, access, revocation, HSM use, secrets handling, and evidence.

## Chapter 15 — Vulnerability and configuration management
Manage images, hosts, containers, services, dependencies, patches, configuration findings, remediation SLAs, exceptions, compensating controls, and continuous posture monitoring.

## Chapter 16 — Incident management
Define provider/customer incident roles, detection, triage, evidence access, communications, containment, legal/privacy assessment, notification support, lessons learned, and corrective action.

## Chapter 17 — Resilience and availability
Set resilience objectives, region/zone architecture, backups, recovery, capacity, dependency mapping, service continuity, testing, provider outage procedures, and evidence of recovery exercises.

## Chapter 18 — Portability and interoperability
Plan export formats, interfaces, dependencies, identity migration, data extraction, configuration transfer, lock-in risks, testing, exit criteria, and contractual support.

## Chapter 19 — Secure deletion and return
Define return, deletion, cryptographic erasure, residual copies, backups, media handling, tenant termination, provider attestations, exceptions, and verification evidence.

## Chapter 20 — Supplier and subprocessor governance
Inventory providers and subprocessors, assess security/privacy capability, contract requirements, location, changes, incidents, audit rights, assurance reports, remediation, and termination.

## Chapter 21 — Public-cloud PII processor scope
Determine when the relevant service acts in a public-cloud PII processor role and document instructions, responsibilities, data categories, processing purposes, and legal overlays without overstating ISO guidance as law.

## Chapter 22 — Customer instructions and purpose limits
Document authorized processing instructions, permitted purposes, prohibited secondary use, change control, support access, deletion, retention, and evidence of provider adherence.

## Chapter 23 — Disclosure and transparency
Maintain transparency about provider identity, subprocessors, locations, security/privacy capabilities, disclosure processes, service changes, and material risks relevant to customers.

## Chapter 24 — Breach support
Define provider obligations to detect and communicate relevant events, preserve evidence, support customer risk assessment and notification, coordinate subprocessors, and document remediation.

## Chapter 25 — Data location and transfer considerations
Track storage, replication, support access, remote administration, subprocessors, transfer mechanisms, localization requirements, contractual safeguards, and jurisdiction-specific risk decisions.

## Chapter 26 — Contractual controls
Translate cloud security/privacy responsibilities into service agreements, DPAs, security schedules, SLAs, incident clauses, audit rights, subprocessor terms, deletion, exit, and evidence commitments.

## Chapter 27 — Evidence and auditability
Define evidence for responsibility assignments, configurations, access, logs, incidents, supplier controls, deletion, resilience, privacy instructions, testing, and corrective actions; preserve traceability.

## Chapter 28 — Risk treatment
Assess cloud-specific threats, shared-control failures, provider dependencies, concentration, misconfiguration, data exposure, privacy risks, resilience, and exit constraints; track treatment and acceptance.

## Chapter 29 — Crosswalks to 27001, 27002, and 27701
Use project-authored crosswalks as implementation aids only. Document scope assumptions, non-equivalence, edition differences, and evidence mappings without reproducing protected standard text.

## Chapter 30 — Metrics and assurance limitations
Track cloud posture findings, privileged access, incident support, supplier issues, recovery tests, deletion evidence, privacy exceptions, remediation aging, and assurance coverage while stating evidence limitations.

## Chapter 31 — Localization, provenance, and copyright control
Freeze EN/es-419/pt-BR project editions, preserve the non-authorized-translation notice, bind DOCX/PDF candidates to SHA-256, and retain source-version, structure, parity, accessibility, render, workflow-security, and staging evidence.

## Chapter 32 — Edition-status reverification and release governance
Immediately before publication, reverify ISO/IEC 27017 and 27018 edition status and related-standard dependencies. Confirm predecessor Manual 42 publication, exact artifact identity, clean staged-head QA, copyright boundary, and registry reconciliation before release.
