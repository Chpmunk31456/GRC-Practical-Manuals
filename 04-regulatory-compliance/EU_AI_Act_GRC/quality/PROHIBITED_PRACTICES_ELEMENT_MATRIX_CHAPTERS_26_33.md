# Prohibited Practices Legal-Element Matrix — Chapters 26–33

**Status:** Controlling correction supplement pending direct consolidation into the final chapter files.

**Primary authority:** Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended, together with applicable definitions, recitals, safeguards, and official Commission guidance identified as non-binding where applicable.

## Use of this matrix

For each proposed, acquired, modified, or deployed AI use case, the reviewer must:

1. identify the exact Article 5 point potentially engaged;
2. test every statutory element rather than relying on the chapter title alone;
3. document facts, purpose, context, affected persons, actor role, and foreseeable effects;
4. identify exceptions, safeguards, authorisation conditions, and adjacent obligations;
5. suspend approval and escalate to qualified legal counsel when an element is uncertain;
6. retain the decision, evidence, reviewer, date, and disposition.

A category label such as “emotion recognition” or “remote biometric identification” is not, by itself, a complete legal conclusion.

## Chapter 26 — Manipulative, subliminal, and deceptive techniques

### Legal test

Confirm whether the AI system:

- uses subliminal techniques beyond a person’s consciousness, purposefully manipulative techniques, or deceptive techniques;
- has the objective or effect of materially distorting a person’s or group’s behaviour;
- appreciably impairs the ability to make an informed decision;
- causes the person to take a decision they would not otherwise have taken; and
- causes, or is reasonably likely to cause, significant harm.

### Required distinctions

- Persuasion, personalisation, recommendation, or ordinary interface design is not automatically prohibited.
- The assessment must examine the technique, impairment of informed choice, counterfactual decision, and significant-harm threshold.
- Separate consumer-protection, unfair-commercial-practice, privacy, accessibility, and discrimination duties may apply even when the Article 5 prohibition is not met.

### Control evidence

- design and behavioural-science review;
- user-interface and prompt records;
- intended-purpose statement;
- foreseeable-harm analysis;
- user testing and vulnerable-user testing;
- legal conclusion and approval or rejection record.

## Chapter 27 — Exploitation of vulnerabilities

### Legal test

Confirm whether the AI system exploits a vulnerability due to:

- age;
- disability; or
- a specific social or economic situation;

and whether the exploitation has the objective or effect of materially distorting behaviour in a manner that causes, or is reasonably likely to cause, significant harm.

### Required distinctions

- Serving, assisting, or adapting a service for a vulnerable population is not automatically exploitation.
- The reviewer must identify the vulnerability, the exploitative mechanism, the behavioural distortion, and the harm nexus.
- Accessibility and protective accommodations should not be misclassified as exploitation.

### Control evidence

- affected-population analysis;
- vulnerability and accessibility review;
- targeting and segmentation logic;
- harm scenarios and testing;
- safeguards and human-escalation design;
- legal approval record.

## Chapter 28 — Social scoring

### Legal test

Determine whether an AI system evaluates or classifies natural persons or groups over time based on social behaviour or known, inferred, or predicted personal or personality characteristics, and whether the resulting social score leads to detrimental or unfavourable treatment that is:

- in a social context unrelated to the context in which the data were originally generated or collected; or
- unjustified or disproportionate to the social behaviour or its gravity.

### Required distinctions

- Fraud detection, loyalty scoring, safety screening, and service-risk assessment require fact-specific analysis and are not exempt merely because they have a commercial purpose.
- The assessment must examine cross-context reuse, proportionality, the basis of the score, and the actual or foreseeable treatment.

### Control evidence

- scoring purpose and feature inventory;
- original data-collection context;
- downstream-use mapping;
- proportionality and necessity analysis;
- adverse-treatment testing;
- appeal and correction mechanism.

## Chapter 29 — Individual criminal-offence risk prediction

### Legal test

Determine whether the AI system is used to make risk assessments of natural persons to assess or predict the risk that a person will commit a criminal offence based solely on profiling or assessment of personality traits and characteristics.

### Required distinctions

- The prohibition does not cover AI used to support a human assessment already based on objective and verifiable facts directly linked to criminal activity, subject to all other legal requirements.
- “Solely” is a critical element and must not be assumed.
- Group, area, event, and operational-risk analytics require separate analysis and must not be used as a disguised individual prediction.

### Control evidence

- input-feature and model-purpose documentation;
- identification of objective and verifiable facts;
- human-assessment workflow;
- decision logs showing the role of AI;
- profiling review;
- law-enforcement and fundamental-rights legal assessment.

## Chapter 30 — Untargeted facial-image scraping

### Legal test

Determine whether an AI system creates or expands facial-recognition databases through untargeted scraping of facial images from:

- the internet; or
- closed-circuit television footage.

### Required distinctions

- Targeted, authorised, and lawfully sourced image processing is not automatically within this specific prohibition, but privacy, biometric-data, surveillance, copyright, and product-law obligations may still prohibit or constrain it.
- The reviewer must examine collection method, targeting, source, purpose, scale, and database function.

### Control evidence

- data-source register;
- collection and acquisition method;
- targeting criteria;
- biometric-template creation records;
- lawful-basis and rights assessment;
- supplier representations and technical verification.

## Chapter 31 — Emotion recognition in workplaces and educational institutions

### Legal test

Determine whether the AI system is used to infer emotions of a natural person in:

- a workplace; or
- an educational institution.

### Exception test

Document whether the use is intended to be put in place or into the market for medical or safety reasons and whether the facts genuinely support that exception.

### Required distinctions

- Not every system labelled “sentiment analysis,” “engagement analytics,” fatigue detection, or behavioural monitoring is outside the definition.
- Not every emotion-recognition use in every context is prohibited under this point.
- Where not prohibited, high-risk classification, transparency, employment, education, privacy, and discrimination obligations may still apply.

### Control evidence

- deployment-context analysis;
- technical capability and inference description;
- medical or safety justification, where claimed;
- necessity and proportionality assessment;
- worker, student, and representative consultation records;
- transparency and human-oversight controls.

## Chapter 32 — Biometric categorisation using sensitive or protected characteristics

### Legal test

Determine whether the AI system categorises natural persons individually based on biometric data to deduce or infer protected or sensitive characteristics covered by the prohibition.

### Required distinctions

- The prohibition must be mapped to the exact listed categories in the current amended text.
- Labelling or filtering lawfully acquired biometric datasets in specified law-enforcement contexts must be analysed against the statutory exception rather than treated as a general exemption.
- Biometric verification, identification, and categorisation are distinct concepts.
- A use that is not prohibited may still be high-risk and subject to GDPR special-category-data restrictions.

### Control evidence

- biometric function and data-flow diagram;
- inferred-category list;
- model outputs and downstream actions;
- exception analysis, if invoked;
- special-category-data assessment;
- fairness, necessity, and proportionality testing.

## Chapter 33 — Real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement

### Legal test

Determine whether the use involves:

- remote biometric identification;
- real-time operation;
- a publicly accessible space;
- law-enforcement use; and
- one of the narrowly defined permitted objectives, if an exception is claimed.

### Required safeguards for any claimed permitted use

The file must document, as applicable:

- strict necessity;
- the seriousness, probability, and scale of harm if the system is not used;
- the consequences for rights and freedoms;
- temporal, geographic, and personal limitations;
- prior judicial or independent administrative authorisation, subject only to the narrow emergency framework;
- registration and applicable fundamental-rights assessment;
- compliance with national-law conditions; and
- post-use review, logging, and oversight.

### Required distinctions

- Real-time and post remote biometric identification are legally distinct.
- Law-enforcement use and non-law-enforcement use are distinct.
- Publicly accessible spaces are a specific element.
- A use that is outside this prohibition may still be high-risk, restricted by national law, or subject to transparency and data-protection duties.

### Control evidence

- operational concept and system capability;
- purpose and objective;
- location and time boundaries;
- authorisation record;
- necessity and proportionality analysis;
- watchlist governance;
- accuracy and demographic-performance testing;
- complete logs, alerts, human confirmations, and after-action review.

## Mandatory legal-escalation triggers

Escalate and do not approve deployment when:

- any statutory element cannot be resolved from verified facts;
- a claimed exception lacks documented evidence;
- the vendor will not disclose relevant functionality, data sources, or model behaviour;
- marketing labels conflict with observed capability;
- the use affects children, workers, students, migrants, criminal suspects, or other vulnerable groups;
- the system could be repurposed into a prohibited practice;
- national law may impose a stricter restriction;
- a material change affects purpose, context, population, data, or output use.

## Audit procedure

For each chapter, select a sample of approved, rejected, suspended, and retired use cases. Verify that the organization tested every legal element, documented exceptions and safeguards, retained evidence, obtained appropriate legal review, and prevented deployment before approval. Confirm that control owners reassess the use following material change.

## Consolidation requirement

Before publication, this matrix must be incorporated into Chapters 26–33, Appendix D, the intake and classification workflows, and all related graphics. Any simplified statement that treats an entire technology category as universally prohibited must be removed or qualified.
